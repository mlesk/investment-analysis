## 1. BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING & FUNDAMENTAL STORY

**What Lennar is:** the largest U.S. homebuilder by volume (82–83k deliveries guided for FY26). It builds and sells single-family attached/detached homes across East/Central/South Central/West segments, plus a captive mortgage/title arm (Financial Services, 83% capture rate of its own buyers) and a shrinking Multifamily/tech-holdings tail [1].

**Why customers pay:** shelter scarcity. Management's own framing: "underlying demand is real and growing and supply remains structurally short" [1]. Pricing power, however, is set at the margin by mortgage affordability — the 30-yr fixed was 6.73% on 2026-08-17 [5].

**Cash-flow mechanics:** revenue → home gross margin (15.6% Q2-26, was 22%+ at FY24 peak) → less SG&A (~9.2% of home sales) and corporate costs → NOPAT. Working capital is deeply negative-carry in growth, positive in harvest; the land-light model (options with land banks incl. Millrose, 98% of lots controlled not owned) shrinks invested capital dramatically [1].

**Valuation story:** *This company will create value because it buys land with options instead of cash, harvests the balance sheet when volumes are soft, and returns capital while waiting for mortgage rates to normalize.* Cash flows today: trough (TTM FCF $717M) [3]. Growth: cyclical recovery, not secular — ~2–4% long run. Risk: rate-driven cyclicality (beta 1.40 [2]).

**Lifecycle:** Mature/cyclical. Model: two-stage FCFF (10-yr explicit grid in workbook, 5-yr reporting horizon).

## 3. COMPETITIVE ADVANTAGE & INDUSTRY ECONOMICS

- **Scale/cost (moderate-strong):** largest buyer of materials/labor per community; record-low cycle times; SG&A 9.2% vs smaller peers structurally higher (estimate).
- **Land-light structure (strong, differentiating):** 98% of 495k lots controlled via options; owned-lot supply = 0.1 years [1]. This converts a land-bank risk business into an option book — lower capital, lower impairment risk, but ongoing land-bank fees. Millrose spin (Feb 2025) moved $5.6B of land inventories off-balance-sheet [1].
- **No durable pricing moat:** homes are commodities with location scarcity. Excess returns are cyclical, not structural. Normalized ROIC ≈ WACC (see §17); competitive advantage rating: **moderate** — it shows up as lower risk and faster capital return, not higher normalized margins.

## 4. MANAGEMENT & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

Observable record: $5B buyback authorization Jan-2024, $1.0B remaining; H1-26 repurchases $737M at avg ~$88–98; $2.00 dividend; debt managed to 15.8% HB debt/capital; $700M 5.20% notes due 2030 issued Feb-26 [1]. Share count down ~7.5% YoY (240.9M vs 260.3M weighted Q2-25) [1]. Buybacks at ~$85–93 (below book-ish) are value-accretive at my base $95. Management quality: **Good** — disciplined, but total payout (~9% of mcap annualized) exceeds trough FCF ($717M), funded partly by balance-sheet harvest; sustainable only if margins recover.

## 6. GROWTH ANALYSIS

FY26 deliveries cut to 82–83k (from ~84–87k) on rates; Q2 deliveries +1.9% with ASP −4.6% [1]. Long-run: single-family starts remain structurally below demographic demand (management assertion [1]); LEN can grow units ~2–3%/yr with community count (+4.1% YoY) and share gains; pricing tracks wage/inflation ~2–3%. **Base: Y1 +4% (mix of units + ASP stabilization), decaying 1pt/yr to a 2% floor.** Fundamental growth check: reinvestment = options/deposits + community capex; at ~2% growth and ~13% forward ROIC, reinvestment rate ≈ 15% — consistent with the land-light model.

## 7. RISK ANALYSIS

- **Business risk:** rate/affordability cycle (the whole story); inflation 4.2% headline in May keeps Fed on hold [1]; cancel rate 14% stable [1].
- **Financial risk:** low — HB net debt/capital 9.4%; $4.9B liquidity; covenants at 14.7% leverage vs 60% cap [1]. FS warehouse ($1.84B) rolls annually but is non-recourse and collateral-backed [1].
- **Valuation risk:** value is ~57% terminal; each 100bps of terminal margin ≈ $25–35/share (estimate); 50bps WACC ≈ $9–10/share.
- **Failure risk:** low probability of distress; main downside is a *permanent* margin reset if high rates persist into 2027+ and land-bank fee structures compress GM (Severe Bear).

## 12. REVERSE DCF

EV at price = $20.85B + $4.38B = $25.23B. Solving my model at EV=$25.23B (estimate): the market is paying for **~2% perpetual growth, ~9.3–9.5% normalized op margin, 9.0–9.4% WACC** — i.e., it already assumes *full margin normalization*, just slower volume. Are these reasonable? The margin assumption is aggressive-to-fair (it requires home GM back to ~19–20% with rates ~6.7%); the growth assumption is conservative (2% ≈ floor). Verdict: **price embeds a fair recovery; it is not a bargain-basement valuation.** Upside requires normalization *plus* either faster volume or rate relief.

## 17. A–E RATING: B+

- **A** would require clear excess returns (ROIC > WACC by 300bps+) — LEN's normalized ROIC ≈ WACC.
- **B+** fits: modest discount to intrinsic value (+10% base, +37% weighted), fortress balance sheet, disciplined capital returns, but a market that already prices normalization.
- **C** would be right if margin recovery stalled permanently (Bear).

## 18. FIVE CONCLUSIONS

1. **This is a margin-normalization bet, not a growth story.** Home GM 15.6% vs FY24 22.5%; ~700bps of GM recovery is worth more than any plausible volume upside.
2. **The land-light balance sheet is the real edge** — 98% optioned lots, 0.1 years owned supply, 9.4% net HB debt/capital, 0.96x P/B. LEN is structurally less risky than legacy homebuilders and can sustain ~9% payout yields through the trough.
3. **The market already pays for normalization.** Reverse DCF says $86.54 embeds ~9.5% terminal margins; there is no free lunch left — only speed and optionality.
4. **ROIC (6.2% TTM) is below WACC (9%).** The investment case rests on buying below book plus capital returns, not on excess returns — which is why buybacks below ~$95 are the management action that matters most.
5. **Asymmetry is favorable but bounded.** Weighted $118 vs base $95; downside cushioned by book and net-debt-low leverage; upside needs rate relief the Fed is not promising. Buy <$82, hold to $125, trim >$125.

## 19. DAMODARAN FINAL JUDGMENT

Value Lennar as a *cyclical at trough earnings with a better-than-average balance sheet*, not as a compounder. The honest base case is $95 — a ~10% discount to price, which is a normal margin of error for this uncertainty. What makes it buyable is the probability-weighted $118, the $90 book floor, and management returning ~9% of market cap annually while the market doubts the recovery. I would buy on weakness below $82 and would not chase above $125, where the price would embed the Bull case. Rating: **B+**.
