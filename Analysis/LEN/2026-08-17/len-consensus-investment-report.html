03The Debate: Agreement, Contention, Resolution

Round 1: opening statements. Round 2: uncontested common ground. Round 3: six disputed points, cross-examined to resolution. Round 4: final concessions and the composite verdict.

Uncontested consensus core

Simple, permanent business — largest U.S. homebuilder (82,583 FY25 deliveries, $34.2B revenue), unchanged in essence since 1954, with a mortgage arm capturing 83% of buyers.
Narrow moat, not wide — real cost/capital-velocity advantages (121-day cycle, $81/sq ft, 98% of lots optioned); zero pricing power; proof not yet in margins (4.9% net vs DHI 9.1%, PHM 11.6%, NVR 11.8%).
Fortress balance sheet — homebuilding debt/capital 15.8% (net 9.4%), $4.9B liquidity, 0.1 years owned land supply; the classic builder killer is gone.
Best-in-class management — salary frozen since 2003, 0.20% profit-share after a 7.3% capital charge, $139M TTM SBC, unusually candid guidance.
Trough earnings are incentive math — 12.9% incentives vs 4–6% normal, declining three quarters running; each point ≈ $300M pre-tax.
The hidden costs are real — ~$18.5B land-bank capital at ~11% cost; $7.06B capitalized option deposits; normalized margins land below the pre-2022 era unless rates fall.
One macro gate — the 10-year (4.68%) with the Fed on hold at 3.50–3.75%; beta 1.40 makes LEN a leveraged expression of the mortgage rate.
Valuation is the binding constraint — 13.45x trough EPS is the top of LEN's own five-year band on the bottom of its cycle; 15.34x forward prices continued declines.
No short, no chase — every lens refuses to initiate at $86.54 (even the B-ratings are price-contingent); none recommends a short.
One valuation-critical variable: incentive/margin normalization (12.9% → 4–6%) driven by mortgage rates vs ~9% WACC — every framework, in its own vocabulary, lands on the same hinge.

  • Best-in-class management — comp built for owners (profit-share after a 7.3% capital charge), candid guidance, $139M TTM SBC
  • Trough pricing on book — 13.45x trough EPS, 0.96x book, 2.31% dividend + ~6.3% buyback yield; ~$5B returned in 2.5 years
  • Normalization leverage — 12.9% incentives vs 4–6% normal; each point ≈ $300M pre-tax; Q3 gross margin guided up
  • Positioning tailwind — 7.73% of float short, avg PT $86.23 below spot, −40% from the high; hike odds collapsed 82%→25%; Berkshire accumulating builders

  • Governance / key-person — dual-class (Miller ~42% votes on ~8% economics); 68-year-old CEO; no named successor

09The Five Consensus Conclusions

The Business. Lennar is the largest U.S. homebuilder with the leanest balance sheet in the industry — a narrow-moat, owner-managed cyclical whose 98%-optioned land model reduces structural risk but whose 4.9% net margin (worst of the big four) has not yet demonstrated the moat in returns.
The Economics. The sole valuation-critical variable is incentive/margin normalization (12.9% → 4–6%) driven by mortgage rates vs the ~9% WACC — each incentive point is ~$300M pre-tax, and normalized ROIC ≈ WACC, so the thesis is recovery, not compounding.
The Market's Expectations. At $86.54 the reverse DCF implies ~2% perpetual growth with ~9.3–9.5% normalized margins at a ~9.0% WACC — the market already assumes full margin normalization. The stock is cheap only relative to a faster recovery.

At a price of $86.54, the market is effectively assuming that Lennar's margin normalization will arrive on schedule — the reverse DCF embeds ~9.3–9.5% normalized operating margins, ~2% growth, and a ~9% cost of capital, a successful outcome already charged at full price. They disagree only on the discount rate a cyclical at trough earnings deserves — and therefore on the price that compensates for a binary rate path: Munger waits at $55–63, Druckenmiller strikes at $72–78 on confirmation, and Buffett/Damodaran/Soros buy below ~$80–82, while Minervini declines to trade the chart at all. The consensus reconciles this into B- — HOLD / BUY ON WEAKNESS: hold at $86.54, initiate nothing above ~$90, begin at $78–82, build through $72–78 and $64–72, meet Munger at $55–63, and buy with conviction below $55 — provided the monitoring hierarchy (home gross margin and incentives, mortgage rates, normalized ROIC vs ~9% WACC, orders and backlog, buyback price discipline) stays intact.
