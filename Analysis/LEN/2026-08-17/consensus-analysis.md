# Part II — The Consensus Synthesis

## 1. The Business (What You Actually Own)

Lennar is the largest U.S. homebuilder by volume: it options land (98% of 495,466 homesites, 2% owned), builds entry-level and move-up single-family homes across four regions, and attaches a captive mortgage/title machine that captures 83% of its buyers' financing. The land-light structure — including the spun-off Millrose land bank and ~$7.06B of option deposits — converts the classic land-bank risk business into an option book, at the cost of an ongoing, partly capitalized land-bank rent.

**The consensus story:**

> **Lennar creates value if the ~700bps of gross margin currently given away in incentives normalizes toward the stated 4–6% band as mortgage rates decline — and if the land-light structure keeps returns on a leaner capital base above the ~9% cost of capital. Both conditions are plausible. Neither is proven at the spot price.**

## 2. The Economics (The One Variable That Matters)

Every framework, through different vocabularies, reaches the same place. The valuation-critical variable is **incentive/margin normalization driven by mortgage rates vs WACC**:

- If incentives normalize from 12.9% toward 4–6% (each point ≈ $300M pre-tax), home GM recovers toward ~19–20%, and normalized ROIC clears ~9% WACC → the consensus base ~$95 and the bull cases are reachable.
- If rates stay ~6.7%+ into 2027 and the "volume over margin" posture proves structural, TTM margins (4.9% net) are the new normal → Munger's $63 (or his $47 bear) is the right anchor.
- Current ROIC is 6.2% — below cost of capital at the trough. The investment case rests on buying below book plus capital returns, not on demonstrated excess returns.

**Consensus monitoring hierarchy:** `Home GM + incentive trajectory (12.9% → 4–6%) > 30-yr mortgage / 10Y > normalized ROIC vs ~9% WACC > orders & backlog > buyback price discipline vs book`.

## 3. Management

Candid, owner-minded, and compensated to earn returns on capital rather than grow the balance sheet — the best comp structure in homebuilding (salary frozen since 2003; profit-share after a 7.3% capital charge; $139M TTM SBC). The allocation record is strong in direction (~$4.8B buybacks in 2.5 years, Millrose spin, Quarterra sale, counter-cyclical Rausch Coleman) and imperfect in timing (repurchases at $89–123; $700M notes issued with $1.8B cash on hand). Dual-class control (~42% votes on ~8% economics) and a 68-year-old architect-CEO with no named successor are the governance caveats. **Management score: 8/10** (integrity 9/10, allocation 7/10).
