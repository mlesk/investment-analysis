# Munger Analysis — Amprius Technologies (AMPX) — 2026-09-21

**Rating: D (Avoid) — Fascinating silicon-anode technology with accelerating revenue, but it fails Munger's wonderful-company-at-a-fair-price test: negative ROIC/FCF, narrow-to-no moat yet, Edge/Outside circle, and a ~$9.61 price ~6x a conservative intrinsic value with no 30–40% margin of safety.**

Amprius explains simply — "batteries with up to 2x energy density so drones fly twice as long" — and Q2 2026 was genuinely strong: record $34.0M revenue (+126% YoY), 27% gross margin, $24M European drone order plus a >$100M Stark Future opportunity from 2027, and raised FY26 guidance (≥$140M revenue, ≥28% margin, <$10M loss, ≥+$4M adj. EBITDA) [1][2]. The balance sheet is solvent (net cash $68.1M, current ratio 7.17) [3]. But Munger asks what can go wrong and what compounding is actually proven: TTM ROIC −9.45%, ROE −36.07%, OCF −$52.87M, FCF −$58.44M, PS 12.85x / EV/Sales 12.23x / PB 11.05x on unproven scale economics [3]. A conservative DCF at WACC+3% (20.05%) with 2% terminal growth values the equity at ~$1.47–1.58/share vs $9.61 — no margin of safety by ~500%. Add ~15 months of cash runway at current burn, +17% YoY share growth, 15.85% short interest, beta 2.34, and dependence on Korean contract manufacturers plus lumpy drone/defense orders, and the rational Munger action is to admire from the sideline: **Avoid; watchlist only if it ever earns 15%+ ROIC with real FCF.** All forward numbers below are _estimates_ unless cited.

## 1. Circle of competence — rating: Edge (borderline Outside)

Can I explain it to a 12-year-old? Yes: "Amprius makes silicon-anode batteries that store up to twice the energy of normal batteries, so drones and electric planes fly much longer" [1][7]. That simplicity passes.

Can I predict the competitive landscape in 5–10 years? No — and Munger would stop there. The relevant unknowns are electrochemical (cycle life vs energy-density trade-offs at 500 Wh/kg, safety, manufacturability on conventional equipment), industrial (yield at Korean contract manufacturers, qualification cycles for aviation/defense), and geopolitical (China supply-chain / tariff exposure flagged in its own forward-statements) [2][7]. I have not observed this niche for 10 years; neither have public markets — FY21 revenue was $2.77M, FY24 $24.17M [5]. Peers show how hard the niche is: Enovix ($35.9M rev, −$124M FCF) and QuantumScape (pre-revenue, −$273M FCF) both burn heavily with volatile stock (−70% / −62% 52-week) [9][10].

Most likely reason for investment failure: mistaking a lab/qualification lead (SiCore500 on standard equipment) for a durable, scaled, cash-generative franchise before yields, costs, and repeat orders prove it. **Rating: Edge, leaning Outside — fails the "within circle" buy condition.**

## 2. Multidisciplinary mental models (4)

**1) Biology — ecological niche + survival of the fittest.** Niche is real and narrow: HAPS (AALTO Zephyr 67-day record), long-endurance drones (Redwire Stalker, EU drone buyer), e-mobility (Stark Future) — missions where weight decides feasibility [2][7]. Symbiosis is mixed: AALTO/Redwire/Stark validation helps, but dependence on Korean contract manufacturers + DIU-funded Fremont expansion means Amprius does not fully control its own reproduction — parasitic risk if yields, pricing, or geopolitics turn [2]. Evolutionary capability is demonstrated (SiMaxx → SiCore → SiCore500 at 500 Wh/kg, 1C) but prior 500 Wh/kg cells needed specialized processes and never reached volume — second-gen SiCore must prove it on conventional lines [7].

**2) Physics — critical mass, compounding, friction.** No critical mass: 97 employees, $109M TTM sales, pilot-scale Fremont; scale is rented via partners [2][3]. Compounding cannot work yet: ROIC −9.45%, ROE −36.07%, EBITDA margin −13.46% — there is no ROE to compound [3]. Friction is high: aviation qualification, customer-specific formats + SAE JA1016 sUAS pouch, pack-partner integration, and working-capital drag (AR $40.65M ≈ 119% of Q2 revenue; inventories $11.51M, both up sharply from Dec 2025) [2]. Scale effects are a promise (contract mfg, minimal capex), not a result.

**3) Psychology — incentives, social proof, confirmation.** Incentives are equity-heavy, as expected pre-profit: FY26 SBC guide $8.3M; CEO 2024 $5.66M total ($3.83M stock + $0.57M options on $624k salary + $624k bonus); CFO $2.77M; director pay mostly equity; hedging/pledging prohibited — alignment directionally OK, dilution is the cost (shares +17.21% YoY, +4.76% QoQ; 146.08M vs 134.54M Dec 2025) [2][3][6]. Social proof is dangerous here: "Strong Buy / $23 PT (+139%)" consensus and 74.3% institutional ownership invite herd comfort while 15.85% short interest and beta 2.34 scream disagreement [3][4]. Confirmation risk: I see margin expansion (9% → 27% YoY in Q2) and narrowing losses; I must weight the mirror — negative FCF, one-off-heavy OCF, and non-GAAP exclusions (warrant mod, Brighton costs, SBC) [2].

**4) Math/Econ — permutations, probability/regression, opportunity cost.** Permutations are sensible: SiCore cylindrical for drones + SiCore500 pouch for HAPS/sUAS + e-moto packs via partners — right mix of high-value niches vs chasing EVs head-on [2][7]. Regression to the mean warns: 202% FY25 and ~93% 2026E revenue growth plus 593% Q2 gross-profit growth will not persist; 28%+ gross margins must survive contract-mfg pricing and competition [2][4][5]. Opportunity cost is the killer: every dollar here (PS 12.85x, negative FCF, unproven moat) competes with wonderful businesses compounding at 15%+ ROIC — Munger would demand a huge discount, not a story premium.

## 3. Inversion — how to ruin this investment

Invert, always invert. Assume we buy at $9.61 — how do we lose 60–90%?

- **Bankruptcy/dilution path:** $74.5M cash vs $58.4M TTM FCF burn ≈ 15 months of runway; H1 alone burned $40.1M OCF (even ex–$20.9M lease outflow, ~$19M) [2][3]. Any qualification delay, yield miss, or working-capital spike forces an ATM/raise — the pattern already (shares +17% YoY; $14.2M warrant exercises + $11.2M option exercises in H1) [2][3].
- **Moat evaporation:** 500 Wh/kg on conventional equipment is replicable in principle; CATL/BYD/Korean incumbents, solid-state entrants, or a safety/cycle-life trade-off customers reject erase the lead. Amprius itself warns lab performance may not replicate at volume or under operating conditions [7].
- **Fatal management mistake:** over-committing to fixed capacity or single-customer scale before repeat orders; Stark >$100M is an *opportunity*, not backlog, starting 2027; the $24M EU order is one buyer [2]. Customer concentration + contract-manufacturer concentration = double fragility.
- **Black swans:** China regulatory/export shifts, tariffs/trade barriers on partners/suppliers, loss of DIU support, drone/defense budget pivots — all cited as forward-statement risks [2].
- **Most fragile assumption:** that contract manufacturers deliver "expected quantities and caliber and at expected prices" and customers keep buying [2]. If either breaks, revenue, margin guide, and the stock's 12x sales multiple break together.
- **Bias self-check:** overconfidence (I understand batteries ≠ I can pick the winner); anchoring ($24.23 high makes $9.61 look cheap — it is still 11x book); authority worship (10 Strong Buys, $18–30 targets); loss aversion (hoping the Stark story saves a downtrend: −29% vs 100-day, RSI weak) [4][8]. Avoid-stupidity checklist: fails "don't chase story valuations," "don't depend on single customers/partners," "don't buy outside circle," "don't pay for unproven moats."

## 4. Quality-company standards checklist

| Test | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Durable advantage | **Narrow at best.** Patent/tech lead in silicon anode (500 Wh/kg demo, AALTO heritage since 2018) but no scale, network, or switching-cost evidence; contract mfg means process know-how is shared [2][7] |
| Pricing power | **Unproven.** Margin lift (Q2 27% vs 9% PY; guide ≥28%) reflects mix/volume, not demonstrated price hikes or inflation pass-through; 10-year pricing history does not exist [2] |
| Simple/transparent model | **Simple product, opaque profits.** "High-energy cells via partners, minimal capex" is clear; profit source is not — GAAP loss, two non-GAAP loss definitions, warrant/lease adjustments [2] |
| Excellent management | **Rational, shareholder-aware, unproven allocators.** Candid outlook raises (twice), detailed reconciliations, DIU/partner strategy; but capital-allocation record is short, CEO pay 40x+ the $0.86 book/share context is rich for a loss-maker, and SBC/dilution fund the ramp [2][6] |
| High ROIC, low capital needs | **Fails.** ROIC −9.45%, ROCE −14.23%, ROE −36.07%; FCF −$58.44M; FCF margin −53.5%; "low capex" (<$10M guide) is true only because capex is outsourced — working capital + OCF burn remain heavy [2][3] |
| Moat rating | **Narrow (generous) / No moat (strict).** Certainly not Wide or Moderate. |

Buy conditions require *all*: within circle, wide moat, honest/rational management, ROIC >15%, abundant FCF, pricing power, 30–40% MOS, inversion-clean. AMPX meets perhaps one (honest communication). **Fails buy; fails hold (no moat/FCF to hold for).**

## 5. Financial quality & accounting prudence

**Earnings-quality tests:**
- OCF vs net income: OCF −$52.87M vs net −$38.40M — ratio meaningless (both negative), fails >1.2 hurdle; owner-earnings proxy (net + D&A − capex − WC build) is deeply negative given AR +$16.8M and inventory +$4.8M in H1 alone [2][3].
- AR vs revenue: AR $40.65M (Jun) vs $23.74M (Dec), +71% in 6 months; H1 revenue +137% YoY — growth absorbs it, but DSO ~108 days on Q2 sales signals lumpy/partner-timed collection, not cash conversion [2].
- CapEx reasonableness: TTM capex $5.57M vs D&A $3.92M — light, consistent with asset-light story; FY26 guide <$10M credible (H1 $2.80M) [2][3].
- Goodwill: none disclosed; no impairment games; Altman 41.61 reflects equity-heavy, low-debt structure, not operating health; Piotroski 3/9 is weak [3].

**Conservatism:** No aggressive revenue admission visible; grant/deferred-revenue small ($2.74M / $0.41M); lease termination cleanly disclosed (Brighton Jan 2026, $20.9M liability outflow + $1.22M H1 costs stripped from adj. EBITDA) [2]. Watch non-GAAP leniency: Q2 adj. net −$3.15M and adj. EBITDA −$1.01M exclude $1.92M warrant-mod, SBC $2.54M, Brighton costs; FY26 "≥+$4M EBITDA" table computes $3.92M and excludes $8.3M SBC — Munger counts SBC as real cost and dilution as real [2].

**Safety margin:** Debt/equity 0.05, interest-piracy n/a (net interest income), current 7.17 — no solvency tripwire today; contingent risk is dilution + partner performance, not covenants [3]. Cash $74.52M covers ~1 year of guided burn only if H2 inflects as promised.

## 8. Investment decision — D (Avoid)

Fails buy (circle, moat, ROIC, FCF, pricing, MOS), fails hold (no moat/FCF to compound). Sell triggers already present for a Munger holder: thesis requires paying 6x conservative IV for unproven scale. Better opportunity cost elsewhere. **Watchlist condition:** consistent 15%+ ROIC, positive FCF through a cycle, repeat (non-lumpy) orders, and a price with 30–40% MOS — then re-rate to C/B. Until then, **D.**
