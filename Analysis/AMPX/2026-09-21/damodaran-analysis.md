## 1. Business understanding & fundamental story

**In one paragraph (12-year-old version):** Amprius makes rechargeable lithium-ion batteries whose anodes use silicon instead of the usual graphite, so they store up to ~2× the energy for the same weight. That extra endurance matters most where weight is everything — drones, high-altitude pseudo-satellites (HAPS), aerospace/defense, and performance e-mobility. It designs cells and silicon anodes at its Fremont, CA lab/pilot line and outsources volume production to contract manufacturers (notably in South Korea), so it can grow without building giant factories itself [IR][1][PR-SiCore][4].

**Who pays and why:** Aviation/defense OEMs and e-mobility makers (e.g., a major European drone developer — $24.0M SiCore cylindrical order in Q2 2026; Stark Future e-motorcycles — multi-year deal from 2027 targeting >$100M; Redwire Stalker Block 30 ISR drone) pay for flight time, range, payload, and fast-charge/high-power performance that graphite cells cannot deliver [PR-Q2][2]. New proof point: second-generation SiCore500 (500 Wh/kg on *conventional* equipment, sUAS pouch + custom formats, commercial availability targeted Q4 2026, initial Fremont build then volume via CM partners) closes the gap between lab records and orderable product [PR-SiCore][4].

**How revenue becomes cash:** Sell cells (battery-product revenue dominates: $71.59M of $73.01M FY25; customization/design services only $0.33M) → gross profit (now positive) → cover R&D + SG&A → operating profit → after tiny interest/tax, NOPAT → minus reinvestment (working capital for receivables/inventory + modest capex under the asset-light model) = FCFF. Today the chain breaks at step two-to-three: gross profit is positive but too small to cover opex, so EBIT/FCF are negative [PR-Q2][2][FIN][8].

**Economic drivers:** (a) qualification-to-volume conversion with a few large customers; (b) $/kWh and Wh/kg premiums vs graphite/LFP/NMC; (c) yields and utilization at CM partners + Fremont pilot (10 MWh expansion, DIU $14.8M award, ~40% of tools installing) [PR-Q2][2]; (d) working-capital intensity (AR $40.65M at Jun 2026 on $34M quarterly revenue); (e) tariffs/supply-chain (China exposure flagged in 10-K risk factors); (f) financing via equity/warrants rather than debt [10K][10][10Q][3].

**Pricing / margins / capital:** Pricing is value-based (pay for endurance), constrained by qualification cycles and competing chemistries. Margins depend on mix (SiCore vs SiMaxx, cylindrical vs pouch), yields, and CM pricing pass-through — Q2 27% GAAP gross margin (from 9% a year earlier) shows operating leverage is starting [PR-Q2][2]. Capital needs are light vs peers *by design* (FY26 capex guide <$10M; PP&E only $11.71M) but working capital is heavy (AR + inventories ≈ $52M) and the Brighton, CO 774k-sq-ft lease exit cost $20M cash in Jan 2026 [PR-Q2][2][10K][10].

**Economic structure:** Revenue drivers = drone/defense volumes + e-moto ramp + HAPS + design wins converting to production. Unit economics = high $/cell, high gross margin *if* yields hold, offset by R&D/SG&A scale burden. TTM: gross 22.25%, EBIT −17.05%, net −35.16%, FCF margin −53.5% [MKT][5]. Capital intensity looks low on capex/revenue (~5% TTM) but high on receivables (AR ~37% of TTM revenue). No debt leverage (D/E 0.05, current ratio 7.17); tax economics currently nil (losses; marginal rate assumed 21% in valuation) [MKT][5][10Q][3].

**Fundamental story:** *"Amprius will create value because its silicon-anode performance commands a durable premium in weight-sensitive markets, and its asset-light CM model lets incremental volume fall through to high incremental margins once fixed R&D/SG&A is covered."*

- **Cash flows:** True today — negative (OCF −$52.87M, FCF −$58.44M TTM) but inflecting (gross profit +$24.31M TTM vs −$18.33M FY24; adj. EBITDA −$1.0M in Q2 vs −$2.1M YoY) [MKT][5][PR-Q2][2]. Must change: EBIT must cross zero and stay there. Why it could: 2.3× revenue growth + 18pp margin expansion in a year shows leverage. What breaks it: concentration (2 customers = 54% of AR [10Q][3]), pricing pressure, yield slippage at CMs.
- **Growth:** True today — explosive off a tiny base (FY21 $2.77M → FY25 $73.01M; TTM $109.23M) [FIN][8]. Must change: growth must persist *after* the easy 100%+ prints fade. Evidence: $24M drone order + Stark >$100M + SiCore500 pipeline [PR-Q2][2][PR-SiCore][4]. Breaks if: qualification fails, Stark timing slips past 2027, or defense budgets shift.
- **Risk:** True today — very high (beta 2.34, short interest 15.85%, pre-profit, single-technology) [MKT][5]. Must change: risk must fall as earnings diversify and the share count stabilizes. Evidence for lower forward risk: net cash, no debt, DIU backing. Breaks if: repeated equity raises (shares +17.2% YoY) keep resetting per-share value [MKT][5].

**Lifecycle:** Young / high-growth, bordering on Growth. Revenue 5-yr CAGR >100%, margins just turned positive at gross line, EBIT/FCF negative, financing from equity — classic young-growth. Valuation model: customized 10-year explicit FCFF (high-growth fade) + stable-growth terminal — effectively a two-stage DCF with a long explicit runway, appropriate when current earnings are meaningless and value lives in the transition to maturity.

## 3. Competitive advantage & industry economics

**Industry:** Lithium-ion for mobility/aviation is large, fast-growing (analyst 3-yr revenue-growth forecast for AMPX alone 71% [MKT][5]), unconcentrated at the performance edge, and technologically fluid. Forces: (a) substitutes everywhere (graphite/NMC/LFP/solid-state/sodium); 10-K warns alternative/improved chemistries can impair pricing and gross margin [10K][10]; (b) customer power high (few large OEMs, long qualifications, 54% AR concentration); (c) supplier/CM power moderate (Korea partners provide scale but capture margin; tariffs/China retaliation flagged [10K][10]); (d) entry barriers moderate-high on performance (silicon expansion/containment know-how, safety certification) but low on capital *for Amprius* precisely because CMs supply the factories; (e) regulation as tailwind (DIU $14.8M, defense demand) and risk (trade barriers).

**Advantage mechanisms (does each move margin, growth, ROIC, or risk?):**
- *IP / performance (silicon anode, 500 Wh/kg on conventional kit):* Supports **pricing + growth** (design wins) and potentially **margin** — but only if cycle-life/safety trade-offs satisfy customers at volume; 10-K and SiCore500 PR both flag that lab performance may not replicate at scale [PR-SiCore][4][10K][10]. Durability unproven.
- *Switching costs / qualification:* Aviation/defense qualification is sticky once designed-in (supports **growth durability**, lowers **risk**) — but pre-design-in it is a *barrier to Amprius*, lengthening sales cycles.
- *Network of CMs + pack partners:* Supports **growth without capex** (higher forward ROIC, lower risk) — but cedes process control and some margin to partners; capacity is not owned.
- *Brand (AALTO/Zephyr 67-day record, Redwire):* Supports **growth** (reference customers) more than margin.
- *Scale / cost:* **None yet** — 97 employees, $1.13M revenue/employee but −$396k profit/employee [MKT][5]; no purchasing or learning-curve moat at $109M revenue vs CATL-scale rivals.
- *Capital advantage:* Net cash + DIU co-funding lowers **risk** vs pre-revenue peers (QS burns −$272.6M FCF on nil revenue [PEER][11]), but equity dependence persists.

**Excess returns:** ROIC − WACC ≈ −9.45% − 13.5% ≈ −23pp today — deeply negative. Forward spread in my Base is +1.5pp (15% − 13.5%). Widening is *assumed*, not demonstrated. Persistence depends on silicon-anode lead surviving solid-state and scaled silicon-competitor responses — 5–7 years at best before commoditization pressure, in my judgment.

**Competitive Advantage Rating: Weak-to-Moderate (closer to Weak).** Real technology edge in a niche, but no evidence yet of sustained excess returns, pricing power net of CM share, or scale. Do not pay a moat multiple.

## 6. Growth analysis

**Decomposition:** 2024→2025 +$48.84M (+202%) was ~$49.5M battery-product growth offset by −$1.2M design-services decline [FIN][8] — pure product volume, not price or M&A._fwd_: (a) market growth (drones/HAPS/defense electrification — strong but unquantified here; gap stated); (b) share gains from Wh/kg leadership (SiCore500); (c) pricing roughly flat-to-up on mix (premium cylindrical/pouch); (d) volume from $24M drone order (2026) + Stark (2027+) + Redwire; (e) no geographic split disclosed (gap); (f) no acquisitions. Analysts expect +92.6% (2026 to $140.65M) then +50.8% (2027 to $212.11M) [FCST][6] — i.e., the Street already models the Stark/CM ramp landing.

**Fundamental growth (reinvestment × ROIC):** With ROIC negative, the formula is meaningless historically. Forward: my Base implies Y1–Y5 revenue CAGR ~21% (144→369) with reinvestment rates 81–213% of NOPAT (growth/15%) — i.e., growth consumes *more* than earnings for five years, funded by the existing cash + future raises. Sustainable only if ROIC ≥ 15% materializes; at 10% ROIC the same growth needs 1.5× the reinvestment and value collapses (see sensitivity §14). Management *can* fund the volume (CMs supply capacity) but *cannot* self-fund the cash burn — external capital is a when, not if, beyond 2026 (cash $74.5M vs TTM burn ~$58M).

**Quality:** Moderate-to-Low. Each $1 of revenue needs ~$0.35–0.50 of incremental working capital + SBC dilution — high-friction growth. Not value-destructive *if* terminal ROIC 15% holds (spread +1.5pp), but value-destructive in every scenario where ROIC ≤ 12% (Bear/Severe). Growth duration: explicit 10 years (32% → 12% fade, floor 5%), then 3% stable. Growth slows because (a) drone/HAPS niches saturate, (b) silicon-anode premiums compress as competitors (solid-state, scaled Si) arrive, (c) CM capacity allocation tightens, (d) law of large numbers on a $794M Y10 base.

## 7. Risk analysis

**Business risk:** Extreme concentration (2 customers, 54% of AR [10Q][3]) — loss/delay of one program impairs the year. Technology risk (500 Wh/kg at volume with acceptable cycle life/safety unproven [PR-SiCore][4]). CM execution risk (quality/yield/pricing at third parties; Korea concentration + tariff/China-retaliation exposure [10K][10]). Customer qualification risk (long cycles; SiCore500 revenue only from Q4 2026 *target*). Key-person/ tar/ FX / commodity (lithium) risks standard for the sector; aviation cyclicality + defense-budget exposure.

**Financial risk:** Low leverage risk (no funded debt; D/E 0.05; current 7.17 [MKT][5]; Altman 41.61 — no near-term distress) but high *liquidity-burn* risk: $74.52M cash vs H1 OCF burn $40.13M (even ex-Brighton ~$19M) and FY capex <$10M [PR-Q2][2] → 12-month sufficiency asserted by management [10Q][3] but 2027 raise likely if FCF stays −$30–40M. Operating leverage high (small revenue misses swing EBIT); financial leverage nil. Share-count leverage is the real gearing: +17.2% YoY dilution [MKT][5].

**Valuation risk:** Nearly all value is terminal (≈105% of EV in my Base — explicit-period PV is actually slightly negative, −$15M, with $310M PV of terminal on $295M EV; see §9–10). Value is hyper-sensitive to WACC (±1pp moves Base ±35–45%), terminal margin (±5pp moves ±25%), and terminal growth. Optimism already embedded is severe (see §12).

**Failure risk:** Severe distress <5% (net cash, no maturities). Permanent impairment 15–25% (technology bypass or anchor-customer loss strands the premium). Business-model failure (CMs cannot yield at spec) ~15%. Substantial dilution (>10% in any 12m) ~60% base-case (history says yes). Need for external capital by end-2027 ~50–60%. Weighted into scenarios via 10% Severe + 20% Bear tails.

## 8. Cost of capital & required return

- Risk-free rate: ~4.2% (*estimate* — 10-yr U.S. Treasury early-Sep 2026; FRED fetch timed out, so labeled estimate; ±30bp does not change the rating).
- Equity risk premium: 4.23% U.S. mature-market ERP (Damodaran, Jan 5 2026 country-risk update) [ERP][9].

- Cost of debt: immaterial (no funded debt; $6.38M total debt is lease-inclusive [MKT][5]); pre-tax ~7% *estimate*, after-tax ~5.5% — weight ~0%.
- Capital structure: ~100% equity at market values (EV $1.34B vs net cash). Target leverage: remain ~all-equity through the horizon (raises, not debt).
- **WACC: 13.5% base** (*judgment*). Below the 14.1% spot CAPM and well below the 17.05% quantitative reference [MKT][5] (which embeds small-cap/estimation premia) to reflect (a) forward beta decay as earnings diversify, (b) net-cash + DIU de-risking, (c) averaging today's ~14–15% with a mature ~10–11% — standard Damodaran forward-blend practice. Range-tested 11.5–15.5% in sensitivity (§14); scenarios add +2.5pp (Severe) to −1.5pp (Extreme). If you insist on 14.1–17%, my values fall *further* (Base $2.36 → ~$1.6–1.9) — the Avoid stands.
- **ROIC vs WACC:** History −9.45% vs 13.5% (destroys value); forward Base 15% vs 13.5% (creates modest value *only if* terminal margins/ROIC land). Growth without the ROIC flip is value-destructive — the central bet.
