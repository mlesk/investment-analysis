CONSENSUS INVESTMENT REPORT

Netflix — Consensus Investment Report

Netflix has already won the global streaming competition and can now convert an enormous, engaged subscriber base into compounding free cash flow through pricing power, margin expansion, and a new advertising business — with minimal incremental capital. The only open question is whether ~30%+ operating margins prove durable as growth decelerates — and whether $78.16 is the right price to pay for that question.
NASDAQ: NFLX Report date: August 16, 2026 Reference price: $78.16 C — HOLD / WATCH

01Executive Summary

Netflix is no longer the hyper-growth subscription story of the 2010s. It is a mature, extraordinarily profitable global entertainment franchise — ~$48B TTM revenue, ~30% operating margins (29.7% TTM), ~$12.5B of guided 2026 free cash flow, ~4.16B diluted shares shrinking ~1.5%/yr, and a ~$325B market cap — whose growth engine is decelerating on schedule (13–14% guided for 2026) while a young, doubling ads business and international pricing carry the future. Three independent analyses reached the same practical instruction: C — Hold/Watch at $78.16. The disagreement — worth roughly 50% of fair value — is entirely about what those facts are worth.

03Points of Agreement

The uncontested consensus core — Round 2 of the debate. Items marked ✅ were not even contested in cross-examination.

The business is wonderful — the debate is only about price. Every profile grades the company near-perfect on quality (Buffett "quality A-grade"; Munger 6.9/10 overall driven down by valuation alone; Damodaran "value-creating by any reasonable test").
The moat is real and empirically confirmed. ~$17B of content spend amortized over 325M+ memberships, a two-decade brand, and ~30% operating margins against breakeven-to-single-digit peer economics. Competitors now license content to Netflix. Nobody grants the moat "invincible," but nobody disputes its existence.
Cash generation is genuine and accelerating. FCF went from −$0.13B (2021) to ~$11.2B TTM and ~$12.5B guided for 2026; capex is ~1.5% of revenue; content cash spend ≈ amortization, so FCF ≈ economic earnings.
Capital returns are disciplined and shareholder-friendly. ~$21B of buybacks in three years, a $25B authorization, $27.1B remaining, shares shrinking ~1.5%/yr, no dividend, and no completed dilutive M&A.
Growth is decelerating structurally, and that is fine — the model still compounds. Revenue 16% → 13% → 11.7% (Q3'26 guide); UCAN +10%; view hours +2%. Pricing, ads and international ARPU — not membership additions — are the next growth legs.
The WBD episode is the single management caveat. An ~$82.7B EV, leverage-financed acquisition attempt that ended in a $2.8B termination fee. All three read the walk-away as disciplined and the attempt as a warning.
Reported TTM earnings overstate normalized earnings. The $2.8B WBD fee (and ~$619M Brazil tax) inflate TTM net income; normalized TTM EPS is ~$2.62–2.75 and normalized FCF ~$9–10.7B. No participant uses $3.17 TTM EPS.
The multiple is de-rated but not cheap. 24.6× TTM / ~29× normalized earnings sits at the low end of Netflix's own five-year range (36–52× at each year-end 2023–2025) — yet all three find no margin of safety at $78 (Buffett 4/10, Munger 3/10, Damodaran "negative").
The failure point is valuation, not the company. Each profile's inversion identifies paying too much as the most likely way to lose money — a failure that requires no bad news.
No one rates D or E; no one rates A. The company's quality blocks short-side ratings and the price blocks top ratings. Ratings: B (Buffett), C (Munger), C (Damodaran).

04Points of Contention & Resolution

Round 3 — seven disputed points, cross-examined to resolution.

Reference price: $78.16 versus the $65–78 band. Buffett and Damodaran anchor on the August 14 close; Munger argues the stock traded below $70 in July 2026, hit a two-year low of ~$65, and sits 38% below the $126.71 high. Resolved: $78.16 is the working reference price for all math; the actionable band $65–78 is recognized — the stock has traded through every profile's first threshold within eight weeks.
Where does base-case intrinsic value sit? Munger $43–60 (11.5% WACC+3 discount, 2% terminal growth); Damodaran $62.84 (9.0% WACC, 3.0% terminal growth); Buffett $66–72 (~9–10% discount on ~$10.7B normalized owner earnings) — a ~67% spread. Resolved: defensible central estimate ~$60–66, anchored on Damodaran's $62.84; Buffett's $66–72 becomes the "fair-plus" value; Munger's $43–60 the conservative value that must be paid for, not worth.
How much credit for the right tail? Damodaran: bull $95.59 (20%) + extreme bull $135.18 (10%); Munger: a $135 case at 25% in his 3-year scenario; Buffett refuses to weight any distribution. Resolved: bull ~$93–96 (20%), extreme bull ~$135 (10%), combined ~30% — the tail justifies watching closely and holding if owned; it does not justify paying $78.
What price compensates for the uncertainty? Damodaran $50–60 (preferred); Buffett $52–64; Munger first tranche $60–65, Munger-style entry $40–50. Resolved (unanimous): the composite ladder — first tranche $60–64, accumulate $52–60, build with conviction $43–52. A dispute about sizing and temperament, not about the stock.
Management quality and the WBD episode. Munger 9/10 ("the walk-away is the evidence of discipline"); Buffett 8/10 (the alarm was the attempt); Damodaran "Good to Excellent, with one meaningful reservation" — the cost-of-capital question. Resolved: 8/10 — elite operators with a capital-allocation watch; WBD recidivism is a standing agenda item for every future quarter.
The ~29× normalized multiple: fair, full, or rich? Buffett: fair-to-fully valued; Munger: rich (compression to 18–20× = −35–40% even on perfect execution); Damodaran: reverse DCF — the price embeds an ~8% WACC, ~13.5% initial growth, or ~40–42% terminal margins. Resolved: at $78 the market pays for modest execution of the base-to-bull path. 24.6× reported is de-rated; ~29× normalized is full. Neither a bubble nor a bargain.
Moat width and the engagement signal. Buffett: wide on economics, moderate on durability (switching costs 4/10); Munger: WIDE within premium subscription video (8.5/10) — flagging the shift from semi-annual to annual engagement disclosure; Damodaran: strong, not exceptional (view hours +2% = saturation). Resolved: Wide on economics, Moderate on durability — "Strong, not exceptional." Engagement (view hours) is the single most-watched operating metric across all three frameworks.

06Pros & Cons — At ~$78

FOR the investment
  • Wonderful business at near-peak economics — ~30% operating margins, 32%+ ROIC, 17–32% ROIC spread over a 9% WACC
  • Genuine FCF machine — ~$12.5B guided 2026 FCF, capex <2% of revenue, FCF ≈ net income by design
  • Moat proven in battle — the best-capitalized attackers on Earth ended up licensing content back to Netflix
  • Two real second curves — ads doubling to ~$3B (250M ad-tier MAU, upfronts nearly doubled) and live events — plus games/podcasts as unpriced options
  • Fortress balance sheet and shareholder returns — net debt ~$5.2B, ~17× coverage, $27.1B buyback capacity, shares shrinking ~1.5%/yr
  • De-rated from its own history — 24.6× TTM at the low end of the five-year range, 38% below the high; the market has done part of the discounting already
AGAINST the investment
  • No margin of safety — price is ~25% above base ($62.84) and ~11% above the probability-weighted value ($70.07)
  • Multiple compression alone is fatal — at 18–20× normalized earnings the stock falls 35–40% even on perfect execution
  • Growth deceleration is real and guided — Q3'26 at +11.7%, UCAN +10%, view hours +2%; pricing is the only lever left in saturated markets
  • Engagement and attention risk — YouTube's $11.1B/quarter ad machine, short-form video, and AI-generated content contest the same 24 hours
  • The WBD shadow — a demonstrated appetite for leverage-financed mega-M&A; the next attempt may not end in a break fee
  • The content treadmill is re-arming — sports rights inflating, guild contracts expired (WGA May 1, 2026; SAG-AFTRA/DGA June 30, 2026); a 2026 strike would freeze the pipeline
  • Mediocre expected return — ~+6.5%/yr (Munger) to −2.2%/yr (Damodaran's 5-year), versus ~10% available in the index this year

08The Five Consensus Conclusions

The Business. Netflix is a mature global subscription-entertainment franchise, not a hyper-growth story — one segment, ~325M+ memberships, ~1.5% capex/revenue, ~30% margins, ~$10B normalized FCF, the only stand-alone profitable streamer at scale, culturally embedded in ~190 countries.
The Economics. The sole determinant of value from here is operating-margin durability. ROIC (17–32%) versus WACC (9.0%) is settled in Netflix's favor; the open question is whether pricing, ads and international ARPU keep outrunning content inflation, sports-rights escalation and guild resets as growth decelerates from ~13% toward ~3%.
The Market's Expectations. At ~$78, the market assumes ~8% WACC, or ~13.5% near-term growth, or ~40% terminal margins — an outcome between the base and bull cases, individually plausible and collectively optimistic. The stock is not obviously overvalued — and not cheap. The 38% de-rating from $126.71 was rational and is not yet sufficient.
The Valuation. Base intrinsic value ~$60–66/share (Damodaran workbook anchor $62.84); probability-weighted ~$70/share (workbook $70.07); bear ~$45–50; bull ~$93–96 with an extreme tail near $135. Confidence: Medium. At $78.16 the expected upside is ~−10% — the weighted value is below the price, and the median outcome is below both.
The Investment Decision. Hold/Watch at ~$78 — majority C, with Buffett's B contingent on price. Hold if owned. New money: first tranche at $60–64, accumulate at $52–60, high conviction at $43–52, provided engagement, margin durability, ads and FCF/share remain intact. The three profiles disagree by ~50% on fair value and agree on the remedy: let the price, not the story, make the decision.

09Final Consensus Judgment

“

At a price of roughly $78, the market is effectively assuming that Netflix will compound free cash flow at double-digit rates for years while expanding margins into the mid-30s — an outcome between the base case and the bull case of every framework examined. Three independent lenses — owner-earnings value, multidisciplinary quality, and fundamental DCF — all conclude the business itself is one of the best on Earth: proven moat, ~30% margins, 32%+ ROIC, ~$12.5B of guided 2026 FCF, a fortress balance sheet, and shareholder-friendly capital returns. They also conclude, with a candor rare in consensus documents, that they genuinely disagree on what it is worth: roughly $43–60 (Munger), $62.84 (Damodaran), and $66–72 (Buffett) — a ~50% spread driven almost entirely by the discount rate each profile requires. What they do not disagree on is the instruction. At $78.16 there is no margin of safety; the expected return is negative to mediocre; and the biggest risk to a purchase here is not a bad company but a full price — multiple compression alone could cost 35–40% without any operating disappointment. Therefore, at today's price the consensus is to wait: hold if owned, watch engagement and margin durability, begin the first tranche at $60–64, accumulate at $52–60, build conviction at $43–52, and let demonstrated economics — not the streaming-wars narrative, not Ackman's 3.15M shares, not the 38% drawdown — decide when a wonderful company becomes a wonderful investment.
