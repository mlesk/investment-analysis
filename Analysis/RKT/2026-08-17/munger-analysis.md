# RKT — Munger-Style Multidisciplinary Investment Analysis

**Bottom line first:**

> **Rocket Companies is a genuinely understandable, well-positioned *franchise* — the largest U.S. mortgage originator AND the largest servicer, with a $2 trillion servicing book, a recapture engine, and a Redfin purchase funnel — but at $14.50 it is a **C — WATCH / do-not-buy** on every Munger margin-of-safety test.**

Munger's first question is: _"Can I explain this business to a 12-year-old, and can I predict its economics with confidence?"_ The first half is easy — Rocket takes people's mortgage applications, funds them, services the loans, and sells them the next product. The second half is where the story turns: the gap between the company's **adjusted** earnings (Q2'26 adj net income $441M, ~16% adj net margin, "most profitable quarter in four years") and its **GAAP** earnings (Q2 GAAP net income $229M; TTM net margin **4.6%**, TTM net income $471M) is the crux of the whole investment [SEC][2][TR][1][FP][16].

Strip out what the company adds back and Munger's conservative **normalized owner earnings land near $1.2B** (adj NI annualized ~$1.76B, minus ~$430M TTM stock-based compensation that is a real cost to existing shareholders, minus a haircut for integration/one-time drag) [FP][16][FP][18]. At the Munger-required discount rate (WACC ≈ 10.3% + 3% safety margin = **13.3%**) and terminal growth ≤2%, a 10-year DCF supports roughly **$2 (bear) / $5.50 (base) / $10 (bull) / $12.50 (extreme bull) per share** — a probability-weighted intrinsic value near **$5.70**, roughly **-60%** from the $14.50 reference price.

Why is the market at $14.50 (fwd P/E ~20x, P/B 1.74 on a 3% ROE)? Because it prices the **adjusted, pre-SBC earnings** (~$2.0B implied forward net income) as if they were real owner earnings, plus pays for a rate-recovery option. Munger would refuse that trade: **you are paying the bull case in full, with zero margin of safety, for a fair-not-wonderful business with a controller who owns ~62% of the vote.** This is precisely the "fair company at a wonderful price" trap.

### My overall rating: **C — WATCH (do not initiate at $14.50; first interest only below ~$8)**

# 1. Circle of Competence

## Explain RKT to a 12-year-old

> Rocket is the biggest company in America that helps people borrow money to buy a house. It takes your application, lends you the money (Rocket Mortgage), then keeps collecting your monthly payment for years (Mr. Cooper servicing — the biggest such business in the country), and later tries to refinance you, lend you money against your home (Rocket Home Equity), or sell you a personal loan (Rocket Loans). Redfin is a home-search website and real-estate brokerage it bought so it meets you earlier, before you even choose a home. ([TR][1][FP])

That is genuinely simple to understand — which is why the industry sits **inside** a reasonable investor's circle of competence. Mortgages are a 50-year-old, regulated, well-understood product; Rocket has been doing this since 1985 [CO][14].

But Munger's harder question — _"can I predict the economics with confidence?"_ — exposes the real difficulty:

- What is the true, durable earnings power when **adjusted** and **GAAP** earnings differ by a factor of ~2?
- What happens to a $2T servicing book and a $20.9B intangible asset when rates move 100bps?
- How much of today's market-share gain is durable vs. a temporary gift from distressed competitors?
- Does the ~62%-controlling founder/chairman act for minority shareholders or for himself?
- How much of "earnings" is actually stock-based compensation (a transfer from existing shareholders to employees)?

### Circle-of-competence score

| Question                                | Assessment                                             |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Can you explain the business?           | **Yes**                                                |
| Is the basic economics understandable?  | **Yes (conceptually), No (financially)**               |
| 10+ years of industry observation?      | **Yes**                                                |
| Predict 5–10 yr competitive landscape?  | **Yes, directionally** (consolidation → scale winners) |
| Predict long-term _returns on capital_? | **Low confidence** (ROIC 2.75% << WACC)                |
| Unknown unknowns                        | **High** (MSR marks, rate path, integration)           |

### Rating: **EDGE** (leaning inside)

The business model is inside my competence — this is not a biotech or an AI-infrastructure story. But the _accounting_ is outside it: negative tangible book, fair-value-marked MSRs, warehouse financing, and an adjusted/GAAP earnings chasm. Munger says a business you can't "know with high confidence" deserves a bigger discount. RKT gets one.

# 2. The 5 Most Important Munger Mental Models

## Model #1 — Competitive Advantage / Moat (scale + switching costs)

Rocket is the **largest U.S. mortgage lender and the largest servicer simultaneously** — a combination "very few companies have" ([TR][1]). The moat is real:

- **Servicing scale:** $2T UPB creates a captive, recurring client base. Existing service clients were **57% of refinance closing volume** in Q2, at "near zero client acquisition cost" ([TR][1]).
- **Recapture:** 26% of the owned MSR book ($320B UPB) carries a note rate >6% — a pool "first in line to refinance when rates fall, and ours to recapture" ([TR][1]).
- **Purchase funnel:** Redfin's ~50M MAU + 47% mortgage attach rate (target 50%) pulled D2C purchase volume **+45% YoY** ([TR][1][FP]).
- **Scale economics:** 70%+ of revenue is recurring or less rate-sensitive; fixed-cost leverage + AI gives Rocket a widening cost advantage vs. peers ([TR][1]).

But a moat that preserves **market share** is not the same as a moat that preserves **returns on capital**. TTM ROIC is **2.75%** versus a 10.3% WACC [FP][15] — the moat defends volume, not profitability. This is the single most Munger-relevant distinction at RKT.

### Score: **7/10 (real moat, weak economics)**

## Model #2 — Incentive-Caused Bias (the Dan Gilbert problem)

Munger's most important lesson: _"Never underestimate the power of incentives."_ Here the incentives deserve hard scrutiny.

- Founder/chairman **Dan Gilbert controls ~62% of the vote** through the new Class L shares (with a 79% voting cap) [FP][1][P5].
- The **2025 recapitalization** collapsed the up-C structure, collapsed four classes into two, paid a **$0.80 special cash dividend to Class A holders**, and created Class L with near-total voting control for the founder ([FP][1]). The share count jumped from ~147M (Class A only) to 2.83B — the fact pack confirms this is the recap reflecting full economic ownership, _not_ real shareholder dilution [FP][18].
- Compensation is **heavily equity-based**: $430M TTM SBC, rising from $145M (FY24) → $343M (FY25) [FP][18]. The proxy is definitive authority on the structure [P5].

Two Munger concerns follow. First, the recap was a classic **structural favoritism toward the controller**: he received the voting power while Class A got cash — the minority is being managed, not partnered with. Second, when equity compensation equals ~91% of net income, employees and shareholders are fighting over the same dollar; the "profit" that reaches owners is far smaller than reported.

### Score: **4/10 (controller overhang + SBC-heavy comp)**

## Model #3 — Opportunity Cost

At $14.50, what are you giving up? Munger always frames a purchase against the _next-best_ alternative.

|                        |         RKT | PFSI (servicer) | UWMC (originator) |
| ---------------------- | ----------: | --------------: | ----------------: |
| Fwd P/E                |      ~20.2x |           ~8.8x |             ~6.1x |
| P/B                    |       1.74x |           0.91x |             3.81x |
| Net margin (TTM)       |        4.6% |           11.8% |             -1.3% |
| ROE (TTM)              |        3.0% |            9.4% |             -7.7% |
| Net corporate leverage | 0.9x (mgmt) |        5.5x D/E |         16.1x D/E |

Sources: [FP][15][U][10][P][11]

PFSI — a pure, simpler, servicer-heavy business with a **9.4% ROE, 11.8% net margin, and positive tangible book** — trades at **0.91x book and 8.8x forward earnings**, roughly half RKT's multiple with _better_ GAAP economics [P][11]. Unless you specifically want Rocket's growth-plus-recovery option, the opportunity cost of RKT's price is large. Munger would call that a poor exchange.

### Score: **3/10 (cheaper, better-quality alternatives exist)**

## Model #4 — Critical Mass / Scale Effects

Scale does cut unit costs: loan officers serve ~40% more clients YoY with double-digit conversion gains; "once fixed costs are covered, incremental revenue drops to the bottom line at a very high rate" ([TR][1]). The $300B origination capacity is the upside **option** — a call on a rate recovery that costs little to maintain today.

But scale effects are only valuable if the product remains economically worthwhile. Rocket's fixed-cost discipline is real (expenses guided _down_ ~$100M QoQ in Q3 even as revenue falls) [TR][1]. The danger is that scale is being used to buy share with pricing incentives (e.g., the up-to-$20,000 combined savings offer) — share at the expense of margin. GoS margin ex-correspondent already slipped from 322bps (Q1) to 311bps (Q2) [FP][TR][1].

### Score: **6/10 (genuine leverage, funded partly by pricing concessions)**

## Model #5 — Regression to the Mean / Capital Cycle

Two mean-reversion forces pull in opposite directions:

- **Upward:** the industry has been consolidating under rate pressure — UWMC is down ~72% in 52 weeks with negative earnings, Altman Z 0.65; dozens of lenders have exited [U][10]. Rocket is "a beneficiary" and expects to keep taking share if rates stay elevated ([TR][1]). That's a survivorship-driven share-gain story.
- **Downward:** ROE of 3% and ROIC of 2.75% are far below any sensible cost of capital [FP][15]. Mean reversion _upward_ toward a normal financial return is the entire bull case — but nothing guarantees it; the current "most profitable quarter in four years" (adj basis) is already a cyclical peak in a market that management itself says will shrink in Q3 [TR][1][FP].

Munger's caution: **"demand growth" (or here, share growth) is not the same as "shareholder returns."** Rocket can grow revenue and market share for years and still earn below its cost of capital.

### Score: **7/10 (risk and opportunity in equal measure)**

# 3. Inversion — How Do We Ruin This Investment?

Munger: _"Invert, always invert."_ Suppose we buy at $14.50 and lose 60%. What happened?

## Failure #1 — Rates stay high or rise, and the market keeps shrinking

The 30-yr fixed is **6.67%** (8/13/26), up from 5.98% a year ago, with a 52-week range of 5.98%–6.69% [PM][3]. Rocket itself guided that **Q3 will be smaller than Q2 — the first time since 2022** ([TR][1][FP]). NAR confirms the stall: June existing-home sales were 4.09M annualized with 4.6 months of inventory; July sales fell another 1.7% [N][9]. The "expected 2026 housing recovery has not materialized" ([TR][1][FP]). If 6.5–7% rates persist, volumes shrink, gain-on-sale margins compress, and the $300B capacity option expires worthless — you paid 20x forward earnings for a shrinking pie.

## Failure #2 — Servicing value / MSR marks fall

The $2T servicing book and the ~$20.9B intangible asset (mostly MSRs) are fair-value-marked. Rising rates pressure affordability and volumes; falling rates (if inflation breaks) can trigger hedging losses and MSR write-downs — Rocket's own hedge strategy accepts lower coverage specifically because recapture provides a "natural hedge" ([TR][1]). A sharp rate move either way can hit quarterly marks. And with **negative tangible book (-$8.0B)**, there is no hard-asset cushion under the equity; book value _is_ goodwill + intangibles [FP][17].

## Failure #3 — Integration fails to deliver the synergies

Only **$100M of the $400M Mr. Cooper expense-synergy target is realized** so far; the extra $100M is promised for H1 2027 ([TR][1][FP]). Mr. Cooper and Redfin were both acquired in the back half of 2025 — two large, culturally distinct integrations at once (the "largest servicing migration in industry history" was completed only this quarter). If the $400M+ doesn't land, or Redfin's losses (it ran ~-14% operating margins and ~$191M net losses pre-deal [RD][13]) don't convert, the whole "one platform" premium evaporates.

## Failure #4 — GAAP margins stay ~5% and SBC dilutes the rest

TTM net margin is **4.6%**; the Q2 "adjusted" 16% margin exists only after adding back SBC, intangible amortization, and one-time costs [FP][16][TR][1]. SBC of **$430M TTM is ~91% of $471M net income** [FP][18]. If adjusted earnings never convert to GAAP/cash earnings, the stock's 20x forward multiple is being paid on fictional profits.

## Failure #5 — The controller acts against minority interests

Dan Gilbert's ~62% voting control (Class L, 79% cap) means minority shareholders cannot discipline capital allocation [FP][1][P5]. The 2025 recap — special dividend to Class A, voting power to the founder — shows exactly how that power gets used. A future value-destroying acquisition, a dilutive issuance, or a related-party transaction can be rammed through with no recourse.

## Failure #6 — The financing/leverage tail

GAAP total debt is **$33.2B** (largely warehouse/MSR-matched, with net corporate leverage 0.9x and $11.2B liquidity — genuinely strong, the only IG-rated public mortgage lender [TR][1]). But interest coverage is only **1.61x** and Altman Z is **1.26** on the GAAP view [FP][15]. In a credit stress, warehouse lines and MSR funding can seize up precisely when markets panic.

# 6. Valuation: Quality + Fair Price (Conservative DCF)

Munger's rule: **discount at WACC + 3% and cap terminal growth at 2%.** SA estimates WACC at 10.29% [FP][15]; with a 2.21 beta and financial leverage, I use **r = 13.3%**, terminal growth **g = 2%**, 10-year horizon, and an **economic net-debt convention** (management's 0.9x net corporate leverage ≈ $2.3B, _not_ the $29.7B warehouse/MSR-matched GAAP figure) [FP][17][TR][1]. I discount equity free cash flow directly.

### Scenario DCF (normalized FCF to equity → intrinsic value/share on 2.83B shares)

| Scenario     | Normalized FCF yr-1 | Growth | IV/share @ 13.3% | IV/share @ 10.3% (sensitivity) |
| ------------ | ------------------: | -----: | ---------------: | -----------------------------: |
| Bear         |               $450M |     0% |           ~$1.30 |                         ~$1.70 |
| **Base**     |         **$1,200M** | **4%** |       **~$4.20** |                     **~$5.80** |
| Bull         |             $2,000M |     6% |           ~$7.90 |                        ~$10.90 |
| Extreme bull |             $2,600M |     7% |          ~$10.80 |                          ~$14+ |

Even at an undemanding 10.3% discount (no safety margin), the **bull** case reaches only ~$11 — the stock at $14.50 is _above_ even the optimistic scenario under Munger's arithmetic. To justify $14.50 you need sub-9% discounting of **adj-before-SBC** earnings — i.e., you must accept the company's own adjusted accounting and its full recovery thesis. That is not a margin of safety; it is a forecast.

### Relative valuation cross-check

- **P/E:** trailing ~82x (distorted); forward ~20.2x on adj-before-SBC earnings [FP][15]. A cyclical financial trading at 20x forward normal earnings is rich.
- **P/B:** 1.74x on 3% ROE [FP][15]. Even a 0.9x-book servicer (PFSI) earns 9.4% ROE [P][11]. Book multiple is unsupported by returns.
- **EV/EBITDA:** 27.4x — versus COOP's pre-deal ~12.9x [FP][15][C][12]. Rocket's _own_ earnings power does not justify the multiple the market pays for its adj-EBITDA.
- **PEG:** 0.34x on SA's 3-yr EPS growth forecast [FP][15] — the only ratio that flatters, and it relies on adj-EPS growth that GAAP does not confirm.

### Quality premium

A wonderful company at a fair price is a great investment; a fair company at a wonderful price is a trap. RKT is a **moderate-quality franchise at a growth-company price**. No quality premium is warranted until GAAP earnings validate the adj numbers — i.e., until SBC stops consuming ~91% of net income and the company consistently converts adjusted profits into cash.

### Margin of safety

Required: IV 30–40% above price. Here IV (weighted ~$5.70) is **~60% below** price. There is no margin of safety — there is a margin of _disappointment_.

# 12. Final Munger Verdict

## **Rating: C — WATCH (do not initiate at $14.50)**

RKT is **not** a Munger "wonderful company at a fair price." It is a **fair-to-good company — with a genuinely strong franchise, real scale, and a real recovery option — priced as if the bull case has already happened.**

- **What I respect:** the franchise (largest lender + largest servicer, $2T servicing, recapture, Redfin funnel), the execution (integration "ahead of plan," $11.2B liquidity, IG rating, 0.9x net leverage), and the honest, transparent management commentary [TR][1].
- **What scares me:** the **earnings-quality chasm** (SBC ≈ 91% of net income; 4.6% GAAP vs ~16% adjusted margin; negative tangible book), the **controller overhang** (~62% vote via the 2025 recap), the **shrinking market** (Q3 guided down, first since 2022), and the fact that **the price embeds a rate recovery plus the company's own adjusted accounting** with zero margin of safety.

### The price I'd pay

- **At $14.50 — No.** You are paying ~20x forward _adjusted_ earnings for a business whose GAAP return on capital is ~2.75%, with a controller, in a shrinking market.
- **At ~$8 — Interesting** (≈1.5x my base IV; the bull case starts to be free).
- **At ~$5–6 — Accumulate** (≈ my base IV; margin of safety appears).
- **At ~$4 or below — High conviction** (bear case largely priced; asset-value floor approaches).
- **Below ~$3 — Exceptional** (you get the recovery option for free and are near-tangible-book-plus-franchise value).

### The single most important insight

Don't ask _"will Rocket gain more market share?"_ — almost certainly yes. Ask:

> **"For every $1 of 'adjusted' profit Rocket reports, how many dollars of real, GAAP-confirmed, non-SBC earnings do shareholders actually receive — and at what multiple am I paying for the rate-recovery dream?"**

Until SBC stops consuming ~90% of net income and GAAP margins close the gap to adjusted margins, this is a **fair company at a wonderful price** — the exact combination Munger told us to avoid.
