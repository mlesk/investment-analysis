# Druckenmiller-Style Analysis: Rocket Companies, Inc. (RKT)

**BOTTOM LINE FIRST:**

**Rating: C+**

Rocket is a hyper rate-sensitive, high-beta (2.21) name whose dominant macro driver — the long end of the Treasury curve and the 30-year mortgage rate — is currently a **headwind with a hawkish Federal Reserve**. The Fed held at 3.50–3.75% on July 29 with **three dissents voting for a hike**, and the June SEP median implies **one more 25bp hike by end-2026**; the 10-year sits at 4.72% and the 30-year at 5.31% (both up ~45–55bp YTD), pinning the 30-year mortgage at 6.67% — near its >1-year high. That is the wrong macro tape for a mortgage-volume story, and it is why the market expects Q3 to be smaller than Q2 for the first time since 2022.

But the company-level tape is the opposite: record purchase share (6.2%), record refi share (14.3%), adjusted EBITDA margin expanding to 28%, $1B/quarter of steady servicing cash flow, a $2T servicing book, $300B of origination capacity, and $320B of owned-MSRS at note rates >6% sitting **in the money to refinance** the moment the 30-year breaks below ~6.25%. The stock at $14.50 (11% below its 200-day) already prices a permanently mediocre market and **does not price the 2027 refi option**.

This is therefore **not a high-conviction directional trade today** — the macro catalyst (a durable fall in mortgage rates) is an H1-2027 event at best, not an imminent one, and the expected 12-month return is only about **+5–6%**. What it *is* is a **cheap call option on the 2027 refinance wave**: limited downside near the $12.17 52-week low (servicing cash flow + 0.9x net corporate leverage + $11.2B liquidity floor), large upside ($18–24) if the rate cycle turns. The correct Druckenmiller posture is **monitor; wait for confirmation that the rate cycle has inflected before getting aggressive**; a small (1–2%) starter is defensible only below ~$13.50 with a stop under $11.50. There is no Druckenmiller-quality setup here today — the ducks are not all lined up.

## 1. Macroeconomic Context (Macro-First)

The dominant macro force for RKT is **the level and direction of the long end of the yield curve and the 30-year mortgage rate**. This is not optional context; it *is* the thesis. Establish the regime first.

### Fed Policy (FOMC, July 29, 2026)

- **Held the federal funds target at 3.50–3.75%** on a 9–3 vote [1].
- **Three dissents — Hammack, Kashkari, Logan — preferred a 25bp HIKE** [1]. A committee with three members voting for tighter policy is not a committee about to cut.
- Statement language: economic activity "expanding at a solid pace," productivity strong, unemployment "changed little," but **"inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal, in part reflecting supply shocks… including energy"** (Middle East conflict), and a blunt commitment: **"The Committee will deliver price stability"** [1].

### The Dot Plot (SEP, June 16–17, 2026)

- Median fed-funds projection: **2026 = 3.8%, 2027 = 3.6%, 2028 = 3.4%, longer-run = 3.1%** — versus March's 3.4 / 3.1 / 3.1 / 3.1 [2].
- The 2026 median of 3.8% against a current midpoint of 3.625% **implies one more 25bp hike by end-2026 is the modal path**, followed by *gradual* cuts through 2027–2028 [2].
- Real GDP medians 2.2 / 2.3 / 2.2; unemployment 4.3 / 4.3 / 4.2 — **no recession in the Fed's base case** [2].

### Inflation

- **Headline CPI +3.3% YoY (July 2026)**, down from 3.46% in June and 4.17% in May; the energy/Middle East spike is fading [5].
- **Core CPI +2.5% YoY (July)** — genuinely close to target [5].
- PCE inflation (Fed's preferred): SEP median 3.6% for 2026, fading to **2.3% in 2027** and 2.0% in 2028 [2].

**Interpretation:** Headline is elevated but driven by an energy supply shock that is already rolling over; core is near 2.5%. This is the *one* macro bull point for RKT: it makes a 2027 easing path credible even while the Fed stays hawkish into year-end.

### Growth & Labor

- Real GDP ~2.2% (SEP median), unemployment 4.3% — solid growth, firm labor [2]. Housing is the weak spot, not the economy broadly.

### The Long End (the operative variable)

- **10-year Treasury 4.72%** (8/17/26); **2-year 4.19%**; **30-year Treasury 5.31%** [3][6].
- **YTD: 10Y +54bp** (from 4.18% on 1/2/26), **30Y +45bp** (from 4.86%) [3]. The long end has *backed up* through 2026 — this is the tightening that matters for mortgages.
- **30-year fixed mortgage 6.67%** (Freddie Mac PMMS, 8/13/26); 15-year 5.96%; a year ago 6.58% [4][7]. The fact pack frames it as "~6.8%, highest in >1yr, +50bp vs H1 average."

**Why is the long end high?** Term premium is being pushed up by heavy fiscal supply — the federal monthly deficit ran −$432B in July and −$120B in June, an annualized pace well above $1.5T [10] — against a Fed that is on hold rather than buying.

**Implication for RKT:** every 25bp of mortgage-rate move swings refinance demand violently. At 6.67%, the refi window is mostly shut (only deeply in-the-money borrowers act); RKT's own numbers confirm the market is shrinking.

## 2. Liquidity & Credit Regime Assessment

**Classification: NEUTRAL (stabilizing at the margin, but no tailwind).**

- **Fed balance sheet:** $6.76T (8/12/26), up ~1.8% YoY from $6.64T — **the balance sheet is no longer shrinking**; the Fed has moved to an "ample reserves" posture [1][8]. This removes the QT headwind that prevailed in 2025.
- **ON RRP:** drained to ~**$0.26B** (8/17/26) from over $200B a year earlier [9]. The liquidity buffer that used to absorb Treasury supply is gone — that is *why* the long end is sensitive to every auction.
- **Credit conditions:** IG spreads near normal (fact pack: RKT is the only IG-rated US mortgage company; net corporate leverage 0.9x, $11.2B liquidity) [14].
- **Fiscal/liquidity offset:** large deficits (−$432B July monthly) mean heavy issuance; term premium pressure is the tightening channel [10].

**The operative "liquidity" for RKT is the mortgage rate itself.** Broad monetary liquidity is neutral-to-stabilizing; but for a company whose volumes hinge on a 6.67% mortgage rate, the liquidity regime is effectively **moderately contractionary for housing finance** — no dislocated, forced-selling conditions, but also no easing impulse. RKT's own liquidity is excellent ($11.2B, +$1.8B QoQ, 0.9x net corporate leverage), so the company is a *survivor* in any rate environment [14].

## 9. Variant Perception & the "What's Priced In?" Test

**Consensus believes:** Q3 will shrink (first since 2022), the rate cycle stays adverse, and RKT is a mid-cycle lender worth ~$0.70 of forward EPS at ~20x — roughly the current price. The 2026 housing recovery failed, so the whole sector is a "show me" story. [13][14]

**We believe:** The market is right about the *near-term* tape but wrong about the *option*. RKT's own operating momentum (record share, 28% margin, $1B/qtr servicing cash flow, $400M+ synergies) means it compounds through the downturn; and the refi wave is not "never" but "H1-2027," contingent on core disinflation (2.5%) that is already visible. When the market prices 2027 cuts, the 30-year mortgage falls toward 6% and RKT's in-the-money MSR book converts — a step-change in volume, margin, and multiple, not a linear extrapolation.

**The difference:** near-term neutral, medium-term positively skewed.

**Why consensus may be wrong:** it anchors on a 6.67% mortgage rate as persistent because the Fed is hawkish *now*; but forward curves and core inflation already imply 2027 easing, and rate-sensitive equities typically lead the turn by 3–6 months.

**What would confirm:** mortgage rates below ~6.25% for several weeks; purchase/refi applications inflecting (MBA data, not currently retrieved — **gap**); RKT guidance stabilizing or raising; Q3 beat on share/margin.

**What would destroy it:** a Fed hike + 10Y >5% + mortgage >7%; EHS below ~3.8M; Cooper/Redfin integration slips; refi share rolls over despite low rates.

**What's priced in test:** At $14.50, fwd PE 20.2x ≈ $0.72 EPS — the market is paying for normalized mid-cycle earnings with **no refi wave and no 2027 rate decline**. The bull case (refi returns) is *not* priced. The bear case (one more hike, mortgage 7%) is also not priced — the stock is priced for a stationary, mediocre world, which is precisely why the skew, not the level, is the opportunity.
