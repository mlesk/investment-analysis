# RKT (NYSE: RKT) — Buffett-Style Value Investment Analysis
### Rocket Companies, Inc. · 2026-08-17 · Reference price $14.50

**Bottom line first:** Rocket Companies is a genuine, well-run franchise with real structural advantages — it is simultaneously the largest US mortgage **lender** and the largest **servicer**, owns the Redfin purchase funnel, and derives ~70% of revenue from recurring or less rate-sensitive businesses, with record market-share gains and a 28% adjusted-EBITDA margin delivered even in one of the toughest spring housing markets in years. But it is **not a Buffett "buy" at $14.50.** This is a rate-cyclical, balance-sheet-heavy financial whose GAAP economics are thin (net margin ~4.6% TTM; negative TTM free cash flow; tangible book value −$7.97B), whose stock-based compensation (~$430M TTM) is roughly equal to reported net income ($471M), and which now carries ~$10.6B goodwill and ~$20.9B intangibles from the 2025 Mr. Cooper/Redfin acquisitions. My through-cycle owner-earnings DCF and normalized-earnings cross-check put base intrinsic value near **$9/share** (bear ~$4.50, bull ~$17, weighted ~$10) — meaning the market's ~20x forward P/E on cyclical earnings provides **no margin of safety** today.

> **Rating: C — Watch / Not a Buy at $14.50.**
>
> I would not initiate at the current price. The stock becomes a serious Buffett candidate around **$9–10**, more compelling at **$6–8**, and high-conviction below **$6**. At $14.50 the market is paying a quality-growth multiple for what is still fundamentally a cyclical earnings stream whose durability and per-share economics (post-SBC, post-acquisition amortization, post-goodwill risk) are not yet proven.

# 2. What Exactly Is Rocket Companies?

In the simplest possible terms:

> **Rocket runs the largest home-financing ecosystem in America: it searches for the home (Redfin), lends the money (Rocket Mortgage), services the loan for years (Rocket + Mr. Cooper, $2T UPB), and then re-serves the same client with the next mortgage, a home-equity loan, a personal loan, or a subscription (Rocket Money).**

It makes money four ways ([SA][4], [SA][6]):

1. **Origination** — gain-on-sale fees when it originates and sells mortgages (rate locks $47B, closed volume $49B in Q2'26; GoS margin ex-correspondent 311bps).
2. **Servicing** — recurring servicing fee income on a $2T UPB portfolio (~$1B of steady cash flow per quarter per management) plus MSR economics.
3. **Real estate** — Redfin brokerage/lead generation (50M monthly active users) feeding the funnel.
4. **Adjacent fintech** — Rocket Home Equity (largest home-equity lender; 250k borrowers, $24B accessed), Rocket Loans (volume nearly doubled YoY in H1'26), Rocket Money subscriptions.

Two things are unusual about the *economics* vs. a typical Buffett holding. First, **~70% of revenue is recurring or less rate-sensitive** (servicing, purchase, cash-out refi, home equity, Redfin), with only ~30% exposed to rate-and-term refi ([SA][6]). Second, the **GAAP statements are misleading** — revenue/net income are distorted by loan-sale timing, MSR marks, warehouse financing, acquisition amortization, and $430M TTM of SBC. A Buffett analyst must look through the cycle and the accounting to find owner earnings (Sections 7 and 11).

**Will this business still be needed in 10 years?** Yes. People will still buy homes, need mortgages, and carry servicing relationships a decade from now; homeownership demand is durable. The commercial essence of mortgage origination/servicing will be *disrupted in process* (AI, digital closing, VantageScore, trigger-lead bans) but not in *substance* — and Rocket is positioned as a low-cost, technology-enabled scale leader, likely a consolidator rather than a victim. ([SEC][1], [SA][6])

**Circle of competence: 7/10.** The business is understandable to ordinary people. The challenge is not comprehension but *predictability* — interest-rate cycles, MSR valuation swings, and acquisition accounting make the earnings path genuinely hard to forecast, which is precisely why I demand a wide margin of safety.

# 3. Will Technology Change the Industry?

Yes — but in ways that may favor the scale leader. Three structural changes matter:

- **AI / operating leverage** — Rocket says loan officers now serve ~40% more clients with double-digit conversion improvements vs. a year ago, and Voice AI has handled 1M+ servicing calls with >50% resolved without a specialist. Management frames this as expanding "earnings power through operating leverage" — fixed costs flat while volume grows. ([SA][6])
- **Credit-scoring & lead-generation regulation** — VantageScore adoption (Rocket was first in production) and the trigger-lead ban lower competitors' ability to poach Rocket's servicing clients, improving recapture economics. ([SA][6])
- **Distribution consolidation** — the Redfin + Compass partnerships and the Mr. Cooper platform create a search→finance→service network that pure lenders, pure servicers, and pure portals cannot replicate alone. ([SA][6])

This is not a technology-threat story; if anything it is a **technology-as-moat-widener** story. But it also means the "moat" depends on continued execution, not on an unassailable proprietary asset.

# 4. Economic Moat — Four Sources

## 4.1 Intangible Assets (brand, licensing) — 5/10

- **Brand:** Rocket is one of the most recognized consumer mortgage brands in America (heavy advertising, arena sponsorships, high-trust positioning). Brand *helps* conversion and lowers acquisition cost, but **consumers do not pay a price premium for a mortgage** — it is a rate-and-service game. Rocket's brand is an efficiency asset, not pricing power. Redfin adds a strong consumer search brand. ([SEC][1], [SA][6])
- **Regulatory/licensing:** Mortgage lending/servicing is licensed state-by-state with capital requirements — a real but surmountable barrier (UWMC, PFSI, etc. all operate). The only investment-grade-rated US mortgage company enjoys a funding/hedging-cost advantage, an intangible with real dollar value. ([SA][6])

**Intangible moat: moderate.** Helpful brand and licensing; no Coke-like pricing power.

## 4.2 Switching Costs — 7/10

This is Rocket's second-best source of moat. Servicing creates a multi-decade client relationship, and Rocket captures the "next transaction":

- **57% of refinance closings come from existing servicing clients** (up from 54% in Q1), at **near-zero client acquisition cost** ([SA][6]).
- **Recapture rates on the Mr. Cooper portfolio reached another record**; Rocket claims the best recapture in the industry on both originated and correspondent/bulk-acquired loans ([SA][6]).
- $320B of the owned MSR portfolio (26%) carries a note rate **above 6%** — a large pool "first in line to refinance" when rates fall, and "ours to recapture" ([SA][6]).

Caveat: these are *behavioral/data-driven* advantages, not contractual lock-in. Borrowers can refinance with anyone. The switching cost is really **Rocket's low cost of re-acquisition**, not a penalty the customer pays to leave. Still, the data advantage (knowing the client, rate, and life event timing) is genuinely hard to replicate.

## 4.3 Network Effects — 5/10

Partly present, partly absent.

- **Redfin** is a two-sided market (buyers ↔ agents) with 50M MAU and ~25k exclusive listings via the Compass partnership — more inventory attracts more serious buyers, who produce more mortgage leads ([SA][6]).
- The **ecosystem flywheel** (servicing → recapture → home equity → Rocket Loans; >50% of Rocket Loans volume comes from existing servicing clients) makes every product more valuable as the installed base grows ([SA][6]).
- But this is **not a classic network effect** — mortgage economics don't improve for one client just because another joins. It's more accurately an *eco-system cross-sell* advantage. Moderately defensible, not Visa-like.

## 4.4 Cost Advantages — 8/10 (the core moat)

Rocket's strongest and most durable edge:

- **Scale:** simultaneously the largest lender and largest servicer ($2T UPB) — rare, hard-to-replicate combo; $300B origination capacity already built out ([SA][6]).
- **Near-zero acquisition on recaptured clients** and a Redfin funnel that converts at low CAC (mortgage attach 47%, approaching the 50% target; D2C purchase volume +45% YoY) ([SA][6]).
- **AI-driven productivity** — ~40% more clients per loan officer, double-digit conversion gains, Voice AI resolving >50% of servicing calls ([SA][6]).
- **Balance-sheet cost advantage** — only IG-rated public mortgage company, 0.9x net corporate leverage (down 20% since year-end), $11.2B liquidity, $1.5B senior-note refi executed at investment-grade pricing ([SA][6]).
- **Expense synergies** — $100M annualized Mr. Cooper synergies realized in Q2, on track for $400M by year-end, plus a new ~$100M line of sight for H1 2027 ([SA][6]).

**Overall moat: Moderate (7/10), potentially widening.** The combination of (largest lender × largest servicer × Redfin funnel × AI cost curve × IG balance sheet) is genuinely rare. But it is not a "wide" Buffett moat because: (a) the product is a commodity priced on rates; (b) earnings are highly cyclical; (c) the moat rests on execution that must be renewed every quarter; and (d) competitors (UWMC, PFSI, and aggregators) operate with far lower multiples precisely because the economics are cyclical.

# 5. Management Quality

## 5.1 Integrity & Shareholder Orientation — 8/10

- **Candid and realistic.** On the Q2'26 call, management explicitly said the "expected housing recovery in 2026 has not materialized," that Q3's market would be **smaller than Q2 for the first time since 2022**, and guided revenue down sequentially — refreshing candor in a tough tape. They did not spin the macro. ([SA][6])
- **Disciplined balance sheet as a competitive weapon:** "capital is not just defense, it's offense"; 0.9x net leverage, IG rating, $11.2B liquidity, prudent MSR hedging (low-cost TBAs/treasury futures; no rate bets), and opportunistic low-coupon MSR sales (retaining subservicing and recapture). ([SA][6])
- **Governance caveat:** Founder/Chairman **Dan Gilbert controls ~62% of voting power via the Class L structure** (79% voting cap created in the 2025 recapitalization). Minority shareholders have limited ability to challenge him. The 2025 recap (four classes → Class A + Class L, $0.80 special dividend to Class A) centralized control — a real governance consideration. ([SEC][3], fact pack)
- **Compensation:** I could not extract named-executive compensation figures from the 191MB 2026 DEF 14A within my fetch budget — a stated gap. The material point for shareholders is aggregate: **SBC of ~$430M TTM, roughly equal to GAAP net income ($471M)** — a genuine per-share cost that a Buffett investor must not ignore. ([SEC][3], [SA][5])

## 5.2 Capital Allocation — 6/10

The 2025 capital program is the crux:

- **~$14B+ in M&A** (Mr. Cooper — the largest servicer — and Redfin) was a bold, high-stakes bet to become the integrated homeownership platform. It is being *executed well so far* (three straight quarters of share gains + margin expansion across rising and falling rates; $400M+ synergies on track, plus ~$100M extra). ([SA][6])
- **But the accounting cost is heavy:** ~$10.6B goodwill + ~$20.9B intangibles; tangible book value is **−$7.97B**. If the integration thesis stalls, impairment risk is real. Goodwill is ~26% of total assets. ([SA][5])
- **Shareholder returns are minimal:** buybacks were small (−$177M TTM, −$105M FY25) and there is **no recurring dividend** (only the 2025 one-time $0.80 special to Class A). Net dilution is driven by SBC. ([SA][5], fact pack)
- **Balance sheet use is smart:** the $1.5B senior-note offering refinanced debt at IG pricing and funded liquidity (+$1.8B QoQ to $11.2B). Investing through the cycle while competitors pull back is exactly the counter-cyclical behavior Buffett admires — provided the M&A returns ultimately clear the cost of capital. ([SA][6])

Net: bold, capable, but *leveraged to a single strategic thesis*. Score 6/10.

## 5.3 Operational Capability — 8/10

- Record purchase share **6.2%** (from 5.5% in Q4'25) and refi share **14.3%** (from 12.2%) — best-ever quarterly share in both, in a contracting market ([SA][6]).
- Adjusted-EBITDA margin expanded to **28%** (from 26% Q1); most profitable quarter in four years; adjusted EPS $0.16 (from $0.15) ([SA][6]).
- Integration execution ahead of plan; largest servicing platform migration in industry history completed; $100M synergies realized in one quarter ([SA][6]).
- The biggest mortgage platform's cost-per-origination and cost-per-serviced-loan are structurally below peers, and the gap is widening with AI. ([SA][6])

**Management overall: 7.5/10** — high-integrity, operationally excellent, disciplined balance sheet; deducted for founder control, SBC≈NI, and a large M&A-driven goodwill base.

# 6. Financial Health & Earnings Quality

## 6.1 Profitability (Buffett tests)

- **ROE 3.03%, ROIC 2.75%, ROA 2.81%, ROCE 4.42%** (TTM) — far below a 15% Buffett threshold, and **ROIC (2.75%) is well below WACC (~10.3% per StockAnalysis)** ([SA][5]). Note: these ratios are depressed by the matched-asset warehouse/MSR base, but even on management's adjusted frame the through-cycle returns on the full capital base are modest.
- **Margins:** op margin 20.0% TTM, but **net margin only ~4.6%** — the gap (pretax $665M vs. op income $2,053M) reflects interest expense, MSR/loan-sale marks, and acquisition amortization. EBITDA margin 25.2%. ([SA][4], [SA][5])
- **Earnings source:** the TTM GAAP figures include acquisition-related noise; the cleaner signal is **adjusted net income of $441M in Q2'26** (adj EPS $0.16) — an **~$1.8B annualized** run-rate — which is what the 20x forward multiple is really pricing. ([SA][6], fact pack)

## 6.2 Cash-Flow Quality — the Buffett test

- **TTM operating cash flow −$634M; free cash flow −$772M** ([SA][5]). Negative FCF at a mortgage company is largely **warehouse/loan-sale timing**, not fundamental destruction — management reports **servicing generated ~$1B of steady cash flow in Q2'26** ([SA][6]).
- Nonetheless: **a business that cannot demonstrate positive normalized FCF to shareholders has not passed the Buffett earnings-quality test yet.** The adjusted (non-GAAP) figures — which add back SBC, intangible amortization, and acquisition costs — are flattering vs. cash reality.
- CapEx is tiny (~$138M TTM) — this is a **low-capEx, working-capital-heavy** model, not a capex consumer. Good for maintenance, but the "inventory" (loans/MSRs) must be funded.

## 6.3 Financial Safety

- **GAAP leverage looks scary; economic leverage is fine.** Total debt $33.2B vs. cash+securities $3.4B → net debt −$29.7B; D/E 1.41; debt/EBITDA 12.9x; interest coverage 1.61x; Altman Z 1.26. **But** the debt is dominated by warehouse lines and MSR/securitization financing *matched to loan and MSR assets*. Management's economic frame: **net corporate leverage 0.9x EBITDA**, $11.2B liquidity, and an IG rating — the only public mortgage company under 1x leverage. ([SA][5], [SA][6])
- **Hidden/off-balance-sheet considerations:** the $2T servicing portfolio (much subserviced/partner-owned), MSR hedging via TBAs/futures, and acquisition earn-outs are the main contingencies. Goodwill $10.6B + intangibles $20.9B vs. equity $23.5B means tangible book is **−$7.97B** — an impairment to intangibles would hit book directly. ([SA][5])

## 6.4 Accounting Prudence

- Non-GAAP metrics (adj revenue, adj EBITDA, adj EPS) exclude SBC, intangible amortization, and one-time acquisition costs — reasonable for a company in an integration year, but they **systematically flatter** earnings power. Q3 guide expenses ($2.35B) include ~$110M intangible amortization, ~$90M SBC, and ~$100M one-time acquisition costs per quarter ([SA][6]).
- The 2025 recapitalization (147M → 2.83B shares) is an **accounting/structural artifact of collapsing the up-C structure, not real shareholder dilution** — use 2.83B shares for per-share valuation. ([SEC][3], [MT][13])

**Financial health: 6/10; earnings quality: 4/10.** A fortress balance sheet by industry standards, but thin, volatile GAAP earnings and an unproven normalized-FCF track record.

# 7. Growth Prospects

- **Market share is the engine, and it is working.** Purchase share 5.5% → 6.2% and refi share 12.2% → 14.3% in just two quarters, in a shrinking market; management's stated targets are ~8% purchase / ~20% refi. If the industry consolidates (as it is, at high rates), Rocket is the likely primary beneficiary. ([SA][6])
- **Rate-recovery optionality is enormous and asymmetrically owned.** $320B of >6% MSR UPB + $300B origination capacity = a large, captive refi wave "when rates cooperate." Q3's guide (adj rev $2.5–2.7B, smaller than Q2) is the near-term headwind; the recovery is the long-term upside. ([SA][6])
- **Organic/new-curve growth:** Redfin (leads +2x YoY in June; attach 47%→50% target), home equity (largest US lender; 250k borrowers, $24B accessed), Rocket Loans (volume nearly doubled H1'26, record June), Rocket Money subscriptions. All reuse the existing client base — a compounding, low-CAC model. ([SA][6])
- **Synergies:** $400M+ Cooper expense synergies by year-end plus ~$100M incremental in H1 2027, plus revenue synergies (recapture) already >50% realized on an annualized basis. ([SA][6])
- **Growth is a mix of M&A and organic share gains** — the 2025 acquisitions drove the +98.8% TTM revenue jump; go-forward growth is increasingly organic (share + productivity). ([SA][4], [SA][6])
- **Industry headwind:** existing home sales ~4M annualized, 30-yr at ~6.67% (52-wk avg 6.32%), and Q3 industry volume expected to shrink for the first time since 2022 — the near-term backdrop is genuinely weak. ([FREDDIE][11], [SA][6])

**Growth prospects: 8/10** — strong share-gain momentum and rate-recovery leverage, tempered by a weak housing tape that could persist.

# 8. Owner Earnings — The Buffett Number

The formula I use: **Owner Earnings ≈ Net Income + D&A − Maintenance CapEx − Working Capital increase (normalized through the cycle), with SBC treated as a real cash cost.**

RKT cannot be valued on trailing GAAP (NI $471M; FCF −$772M — both distorted). I normalize from the cleanest signal — **annualized adjusted net income ~$1.8B** (Q2'26 adj NI $441M × 4; [SA][6]) — then apply a **Buffett haircut for SBC (~$430M TTM)** and adjust for acquisition amortization to reach normalized owner earnings:

- **Normalized owner earnings ≈ $1.3–1.6B** in a tough market (adj NI run-rate $1.8B − SBC $0.43B ± acquisition/amort noise), rising toward **$2–2.5B** in a rate-recovery year (refi recapture + purchase recovery + synergies).
- Servicing alone contributes ~$4B/yr of cash-flow-generating UPB economics, but that gross flow funds MSR amortization, hedging, and funding — it is a **floor, not free cash**.

Three scenarios on a 10-year owner-earnings DCF (discount 10–12% per profile; terminal growth 2–3%, ≤ GDP; **economic net-debt convention: ~$2.7B corporate net debt at 0.9x EBITDA — disclosed, warehouse/MSR financing excluded as asset-matched**):

| Scenario | Normalized OE₀ | Growth (yrs 1–10) | Discount rate | Terminal g | Equity value | **Value/share** |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **Bear (conservative)** | $1.1B | 3% | 12% | 2% | ~$9.3B | **~$3.30** |
| **Base (neutral)** | $1.5B | 5% | 10% | 2.5% | ~$23.5B | **~$8.30** |
| **Bull (optimistic)** | $2.4B | 7% | 10% | 3% | ~$44.5B | **~$15.70** |

*(All values are estimates from the stated inputs; shares = 2.83B; corporate net debt ~$2.7B is an estimate. Discount rates 10–12% are at the Buffett-appropriate end; a lower WACC would raise values proportionally.)*

**Cross-check — normalized-earnings multiple:** On ~$1.8B through-cycle adjusted earnings at a fair 15–18x multiple for a high-quality cyclical with 70% recurring revenue, equity value is ~$27–32B → **~$9.5–11.5/share**. On ~$2.0B recovery earnings at 15x → ~$10.6/share. This broadly **corroborates the base DCF (~$8–11)** and is far below the market's 20x forward pricing.

**Probability-weighted:** Bear 25% / Base 50% / Bull 25% → **weighted ≈ $10.00/share** (0.25×$3.30 + 0.50×$8.30 + 0.25×$15.70 = $8.90 on pure DCF; ~$10.00 blending the multiple cross-check).

# 10. Margin of Safety

- **Base IV ≈ $9; weighted ≈ $10.** At $14.50, the stock trades **~45–60% above** base intrinsic value and **~45% above** the probability-weighted value. **There is no margin of safety; there is negative margin of safety.**
- Even the **bull case (~$15.70)** is only marginally above the current price — meaning the market is *already pricing* a largely successful integration, sustained share gains, and rate-recovery earnings, with nothing left for error.
- For a rate-cyclical financial with thin GAAP margins, negative TBV, SBC ≈ NI, and unproven normalized FCF, I require **at least 30–50% below base IV** — i.e., a buy zone around **$6–9**, with meaningful accumulation only materially lower.
- Price ladder (my Buffett frame): **>$15–17** too much priced in (reduce/trim); **$11–15** hold/do-not-initiate (current zone); **$9–10** first serious interest; **$6–9** accumulate; **<$6** high-conviction.

# 11. Risks

1. **Protracted high-rate / weak-housing cycle.** 30-yr at ~6.67% (52-wk avg 6.32%), existing home sales ~4M, and Q3 industry volume expected to shrink for the first time since 2022 ([FREDDIE][11], [SA][6]). If rates stay elevated, the purchase/refi recovery never comes and the ~$1.8B earnings power erodes with volumes.
2. **M&A integration / goodwill impairment.** ~$10.6B goodwill + $20.9B intangibles (TBV −$7.97B). If Mr. Cooper/Redfin synergies ($400M+$100M) or the revenue story disappoint, impairment charges could wipe out much of book value. ([SA][5], [SA][6])
3. **SBC ≈ net income.** $430M TTM of stock comp vs. $471M GAAP NI means per-share value creation is muted; a Buffett buyer must treat SBC as a real cash cost. ([SA][5])
4. **Cyclical GAAP volatility / negative FCF.** TTM FCF −$772M; warehouse/MSR accounting makes reported cash flows swing wildly — hard to underwrite a stable owner-earnings stream. ([SA][5])
5. **Founder governance.** Dan Gilbert's ~62% voting control (Class L, 79% cap) means minority shareholders cannot block major decisions; 2025 recap centralized control. ([SEC][3], fact pack)
6. **Valuation/expectations risk.** At ~20x forward on cyclical earnings, the stock already discounts success; any share-gain or synergy stumble would compress the multiple violently (beta 2.21). ([SA][4][5])
