# Part I — The Debate

## Round 1: Opening Statements

### Buffett's position

> "This is a genuine, well-run franchise — simultaneously the largest mortgage lender and the largest servicer, with a $2T servicing book, record share gains, and a fortress balance sheet. But it is **not a buy at $14.50**. Through-cycle owner earnings support a base value near **$9**, bear **$4.50**, bull **$17** — so the market's ~20× forward multiple on cyclical earnings provides no margin of safety. My rating is **C — Watch**. I become a serious buyer in the **$6–10** zone. The deal-breaker is price, not quality."

### Munger's position

> "A genuinely understandable, well-positioned franchise — and a fair-company-at-a-wonderful-price trap. At **$14.50** you are paying ~20× forward *adjusted* earnings for a business whose GAAP return on capital is ~2.75%, whose SBC (~$430M) consumes ~91% of net income, whose tangible book is **−$8.0B**, and whose founder controls ~62% of the vote. My conservative DCF (13.3% discount, 2% terminal growth) puts base value at **$5.50** — an expected return of about **−60%**. Rating: **C — Watch**. First interest only below **~$8**."

### Damodaran's position

> "This is a genuinely dominant platform whose value is a wide probability distribution. My FCFF DCF — treating SBC and acquired-intangible amortization as the real costs they are, at 9% WACC and 3% terminal growth, with an economic net-debt convention (~$2.3B corporate net debt) — gives a base of **$8.24**. Probability-weighted across five scenarios the workbook gives **$15.07** (+4%, ~+0.8% annualized). That is **not adequate compensation for the uncertainty**. The market at $14.50 is implicitly assuming RKT roughly doubles revenue to ~$20B at ~20% margins. Rating: **C — Hold/Wait**. Value becomes genuinely interesting only below ~$10."

### Druckenmiller's position

> "Macro first: the Fed held at 3.50–3.75% with three dissents voting for a hike, the 10Y is ~4.72% and rising, and the 30-year mortgage at ~6.67% is near a one-year high — the wrong tape for a mortgage-volume story, and Q3 is guided down for the first time since 2022. But the company-level tape is the opposite: record share, 28% adjusted-EBITDA margin, ~$1B/quarter servicing cash flow, and $320B of in-the-money MSRs. This is **a cheap call option on the 2027 refi wave, not a high-conviction long today** — 12-month price expectation ~**$15–15.50** (E[R] ≈ +6%). Rating: **C+ — Monitor**. A 1–2% starter is defensible only below **~$13.50** with a stop under **$11.50**."

### Soros's position

> "The rare company whose fundamentals and stock are moving in opposite directions. In the toughest spring housing market in years, Rocket printed a **record** quarter — record share, most profitable in four years, 28% adjusted-EBITDA margin, >70% recurring revenue — and the market responded by cutting price targets. The negative macro loop is consolidating the industry toward the one player with an investment-grade balance sheet, while $320B of >6% MSRs sit first in line to refinance. My probability-weighted 12-month **price** path is ≈ **$16.73** (+15%), with ~4.4:1 asymmetry. Rating: **B — asymmetric reflexive-reversal, scale-in** below $14.50. Not an A: the near-term data still validate the bear tape."

## Round 3: Points of Contention, Cross-Examination, and Resolution

### Contention 1 — Reference price: intrinsic value vs 12-month price

- **Buffett / Munger / Damodaran** frame their scenario values as **intrinsic value today** — what the equity is worth on normalized economics. **Druckenmiller / Soros** frame theirs as **12–18-month price expectations** — where the stock trades as macro, positioning, and expectations evolve (Druckenmiller weighted ~$15–15.50; Soros ~$16.73).
- **Cross-examination:** These are different objects. Consensus base intrinsic value (~$8) sits far *below* the $14.50 price, while both 12-month price expectations cluster at/above $14.50 — so a reader could be confused into thinking the "value" is higher than it is. All five anchor on the same $14.50 reference price, so the rating is insensitive to the framing — but the columns are not interchangeable.
- **Named concession:** **Druckenmiller** concedes, "my base is a price forecast under a stationary macro, not an intrinsic value — I am pricing the option, not the asset." **Soros** concedes his "B" is a reflexivity trade on expectations and positioning, not a claim that Rocket is fundamentally worth more than $14.50.
- **Resolution:** The three intrinsic-value models (Buffett, Munger, Damodaran) set the **consensus IV**. Druckenmiller and Soros inform the near-term price ladder and triggers only, and are labeled † (12-month price expectations) in the matrix.

### Contention 2 — Where does base-case intrinsic value actually sit?

| Buffett | **~$9.00** | Normalized owner earnings $1.1–2.4B at 10–12% discount, 15–18× cross-check |

- **Munger's challenge to Buffett/Damodaran:** "You are capitalizing adjusted, pre-SBC earnings. $430M of stock comp ≈ 91% of net income — it is a real cost to existing shareholders."

- **Damodaran:** extreme bull **$73.62** @ 10% (workbook); bull $19.46 @ 15%. **Munger:** extreme bull **$12.50** @ 5% — refuses to pay for the option. **Buffett:** bull **$17**, refuses to weight it heavily. **Druckenmiller:** extreme bull **$21–24** @ 10% (a 2027 call). **Soros:** extreme bull **$26** @ 8% (largest levered call on rate relief priced at $0).

Critically, the probability-weighted consensus value (~$14.86) is lifted almost entirely by the tail: the extreme-bull cell alone contributes ~$7.36 of the $14.86.
- **Named concession:** **Munger** concedes, "this is the most credible optionality in the industry — the counter-cyclical asset becomes hugely valuable exactly when the macro turns. I simply refuse to pay for it in advance."
- **Resolution:** Consensus bull ≈ **~$18**, extreme bull ≈ **~$73.62** (adopted from the workbook as the option proxy), combined probability ~25%. The right tail justifies *watching the story closely*; it does not justify paying for it at $14.50.

### Contention 6 — The net-debt / GAAP convention judgment

- **Damodaran:** ~**$2.3B** economic net corporate debt (0.9× EBITDA; $5,748M less $3,448M cash; excludes matched warehouse/MSR/advance and Ginnie buyout financing) — calls it "the single most important judgment in this valuation," noting a pure-GAAP corporate view (~$7.5B of senior notes net of corporate cash) would cut value by ~$1.8–2.0/share (~25%).

- **Named concession:** **Soros** concedes "the economic frame is right for a going concern — but a credit event could make the 'matched' debt less matched, and that tail is what negative tangible book leaves uninsured."
- **Resolution:** Consensus adopts the **economic net-debt convention (~$2.3B corporate net debt at 0.9× EBITDA), disclosed in every model**. A pure-GAAP corporate view would lower consensus base IV from ~$8 to ~$6–6.50 without changing the C rating.

# Part II — The Consensus Synthesis

## 1. The Business (What You Actually Own)

Rocket is the dominant US homeownership platform: it searches for the home (Redfin, 50M MAU), lends the money (Rocket Mortgage, the largest US lender), services the loan for decades (Rocket + Mr. Cooper, $2T UPB, the largest servicer), and re-serves the same client with the next mortgage, a home-equity loan, a personal loan, or a subscription (Rocket Money). ~70% of revenue is recurring or less rate-sensitive. It is the only integrated search→originate→service→recapture platform in the country, with an investment-grade balance sheet that no other public US mortgage company has.

**The consensus story:**

> **Rocket can create enormous per-share value if it converts the largest mortgage-servicing franchise in the world — a $2T UPB annuity throwing off ~$1B of cash per quarter — into durable, low-CAC share gains across origination, home equity, personal loans and brokerage, at a scale and cost advantage competitors cannot easily match — and if the returns on that growth exceed its ~9% cost of capital. The franchise is proven; the economics are not yet.**

## 2. The Economics (The One Variable That Matters)

Every profile, through different vocabularies, reaches the same place:

$$ \text{Value is created only if} \quad \text{Economic EBIT margin and incremental ROIC} > \text{Cost of Capital} \ (\approx 9\%) $$

- If Rocket holds **17→20% economic EBIT margins** (after treating SBC and acquired-intangible amortization as real costs) and earns **~30%→25% ROIC on tangible capital**, then growth creates value and the $2T servicing platform compounds — the Damodaran/Buffett base (~$8–9) is real.
- If economic margins compress toward GAAP's **4.6% net margin** (SBC ≈ 91% of NI; adjusted ~16% never converting to cash) and returns on new capital fall below ~9%, then record share gains **destroy value** — the Munger bear reading.
- The answer is **contested today** — which is precisely why the stock is a debate rather than an obvious buy or short.

**Consensus monitoring hierarchy:** `Economic EBIT margin (GAAP-vs-adjusted gap) → Incremental ROIC vs ~9% WACC → SBC as % of net income → Purchase/refi share (6.2% / 14.3%) → Servicing cash flow (~$1B/qtr) → 30-yr mortgage rate (<~6.25%)`.

## 3. Management

Strong, candid, operationally excellent execution (record share, 28% adjusted-EBITDA margin, integration ahead of plan, $11.2B liquidity, honest guidance) — but docked for founder control (~62% via Class L), SBC ≈ net income, minimal buybacks/dividend, and a $14B+ stock-funded M&A thesis with $31.5B of goodwill+intangibles. **6.5/10.**

The moat (scale × servicing recapture × distribution × AI × IG balance sheet) is real, widening, and moderate — not wide. The business quality is not in dispute.
