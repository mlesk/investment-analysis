03The Debate: Agreement, Contention, Resolution

Round 1: opening statements. Round 2: uncontested common ground. Round 3: six disputed points, cross-examined to resolution. Round 4: final concessions.

Uncontested consensus core

The business has changed identity. Bitcoin mining is the legacy cash engine; the value of the stock is the AI infrastructure buildout.
The scarce asset is power, not GPUs. GPUs are purchasable; grid interconnection, land, and permits are not. Speed ("time-to-compute") is the only plausible moat.
The moat is real but unproven. Grades: Narrow→Moderate (Buffett), Moderate (Munger), Moderate→potentially Strong (Damodaran). Nobody grants "wide."
Conventional metrics mislead. GAAP EPS, P/E, and EBITDA are unusable: GPU depreciation is economically real, and EBITDA ignores ~$2.6B/9-months of investing cash flow.
Owner earnings are negative today. FY25 FCF ≈ −$1.1B. The investment is a wager on future owner earnings, not current ones.
Execution brilliant; allocation unproven. Microsoft, NVIDIA, >$4B ARR, 5 GW — real. But +33% shares in 9 months and $820M co-CEO RSUs mean per-share economics are all that count.
One valuation-critical variable: incremental ROIC on new AI capital (Damodaran), new capacity (Munger), and FCF/share after maintenance CapEx (Buffett). ARR growth is meaningless if returns fall below the cost of capital.
Value is a distribution, not a point. All three produced scenario spreads spanning an order of magnitude ($5 → $166) and warned against manufactured precision.
The current price embeds successful execution. Margin of safety: "not enough" (Damodaran), 3/10 (Munger), 3/10 (Buffett).
Rating: C — Hold/Watch. Unanimous across three independent frameworks. No A, no B, no D, no E.

Contested points — and how they were resolved

Reference price: $38 vs $43.67 vs $45. Two weeks apart on the same stock; every rating is insensitive across the band. Resolved: working band ~$40–45.
Where does base value sit? Munger $35–38 (12%/2% — strictest), Damodaran $42, Buffett $73 (capitalizes unproven $1.3B owner earnings). Buffett concedes $73 is "proven-execution value," not today's value. Resolved: base ≈ $38–42; $73 becomes the upgrade target.
How much credit for the right tail? Bull estimates $75–80 (consensus) with an extreme tail at $140–165. The tail is real but not likely (30–35% combined). Resolved: it justifies watching, not paying.
What price compensates for uncertainty? Damodaran $30–37; Buffett $25–30; Munger $20–25. A dispute about sizing and temperament, not the stock. Resolved: composite ladder — start $30–35, build $25–30, conviction $20–25.
Management: 8 vs 7 vs "not proven." Munger concedes half a point on compensation; Buffett holds on dilution; Damodaran's "has IREN ever earned its cost of capital?" becomes monitoring metric #1. Resolved: 7/10.
Does positive expected value justify buying? All three compute positive EV (+27% Damodaran, +13% Munger) yet all rate C — EV is tail-driven, the median ≈ price, and the −50% to −70% left tail is uninsurable. Resolved: positive EV is not sufficient to act.

06Pros & Cons — At ~$40–45

FOR the investment
  • Scarce power moat — 4.5–5 GW secured where interconnection takes years
  • Contracted demand — $13B+ signed Microsoft/NVIDIA; ~85% of >$4B ARR under contract
  • Blue-chip counterparties validate the platform
  • Speed advantage — self-build capability, "time-to-compute"
  • Strong liquidity — ~$7.6B cash plus investment-grade GPU financing
  • Improving terms — customer prepayments cover ~45% of GPU CapEx
  • Fat right tail — $75–80 bull and $140+ extreme bull are real, not fantasy
AGAINST the investment
  • No margin of safety — price ≈ conservative base case
  • Negative ROIC and owner earnings today — the bet is entirely on the future
  • Reinvestment treadmill — $2.6B/9-months investing vs $289M operating cash flow
  • GPU obsolescence — infrastructure-like buildings, inventory-like chips
  • AI oversupply risk — capital floods in; pricing can compress faster than demand grows
  • Dilution machine — +33% shares in nine months; $820M co-CEO RSU grants
  • Complex capital structure — $6.7B+ convertibles, capped calls, forwards, GPU debt
  • Concentration and accounting opacity — FY26 10-K not yet filed

07What Changes the Rating

Upgrade C → B/A when:
  1. Incremental ROIC on new AI capacity demonstrated ≥15%
  2. FCF turns sustainably positive, heading toward $1B+ annualized
  3. Diluted share count stabilizes; revenue grows faster than shares
  4. AI Cloud margins hold after depreciation; CapEx/MW declines
  5. Utilization stays high; AI pricing holds
Downgrade C → D/E if:
  1. AI compute prices fall >20–25% or utilization disappoints
  2. Microsoft/NVIDIA contracts renegotiated downward
  3. CapEx remains enormous indefinitely — the capital treadmill
  4. Persistent 20–30% annual dilution with no per-share value creation
  5. Incremental ROIC settles below ~10–12%

08Final Consensus Judgment

“

At a price of roughly $40–45, the market is effectively assuming that IREN will successfully convert its scarce power portfolio into a multi-billion-dollar AI infrastructure business with strong margins and acceptable returns on capital. Three independent frameworks — fundamental DCF, multidisciplinary quality, and owner-earnings value — all land at a base intrinsic value of roughly $38–42 per share and a rating of C: Hold/Watch. We agree the business and the contracts are real, the moat hypothesis is plausible but unproven, current returns are negative, dilution is material, and the valuation already pays for success. The biggest risks to any purchase here are AI compute oversupply, GPU obsolescence, financing/dilution, and an incremental ROIC below the cost of capital. Therefore, at today's price we would wait: accumulate in stages below $35, buy with conviction in the $20–25 range, and let demonstrated return on capital — not ARR headlines — decide whether this becomes a wonderful compounder or merely a wonderful story.
