# IREN — Damodaran-Style Fundamental Valuation

**Valuation date:** August 16, 2026
**Working share price:** approximately **$45/share**. Because the web data available to me does not expose a clean official August 14 closing quote, I use $45 as the reference price rather than manufacture a more precise number.
**Diluted valuation share count:** ~**380 million**
**Primary framework:** FCFF DCF, supplemented by reverse DCF, scenario analysis, and relative valuation.

> **Bottom line:** IREN is no longer fundamentally a Bitcoin miner with an AI story attached. It is becoming a highly capital-intensive AI infrastructure company whose principal assets are **power, data-center capacity, GPUs, customer contracts and execution capability**. The opportunity is real, but so is the financing and execution risk. My base DCF produces approximately **$42/share**, while probability-weighted value is approximately **$57/share**. At ~$45, I view the stock as **roughly fairly valued to modestly attractive**, but not with enough margin of safety to warrant an aggressive purchase. My preferred entry range is approximately **$30–35**, with ~$37 representing a reasonable "fair risk-adjusted" price.

# 1. What IREN Actually Is

The simplest description is:

> **IREN buys/controls electricity and data-center capacity, puts computing hardware inside those facilities, and sells the resulting computing capacity to customers.**

Historically that computing capacity was overwhelmingly Bitcoin mining ASICs.

The company is now rapidly reallocating that infrastructure toward GPUs and AI/HPC workloads.

As of March 31, 2026, IREN reported approximately **$570 million of nine-month revenue**, of which $512 million was Bitcoin mining and only $58 million AI Cloud. But the direction of the business is changing dramatically. ([SEC][1])

IREN reported:

- ~4.5 GW of secured power
- ~99,900 GPUs installed or on order at December 2025
- ~46 EH/s installed Bitcoin mining capacity at December 2025
- seven data-center sites with approximately 4.5 GW of grid agreements
- a 750 MW Childress campus
- 200 MW of Childress being transitioned toward liquid-cooled AI infrastructure. ([SEC][2])

The strategic transformation is therefore:

**Bitcoin mining → power infrastructure → AI infrastructure**

That distinction is fundamental to the valuation.

---

# 2. The Fundamental Story

The IREN story can be expressed as:

> **IREN can create enormous value if it converts scarce, already-secured power and data-center infrastructure into long-duration, high-return AI compute contracts faster and more efficiently than competitors.**

The three Damodaran variables become:

### Cash flows

Potentially enormous growth in operating cash flow as contracted AI capacity becomes operational.

### Growth

Extremely high for several years.

Management was targeting:

- **$3.7B ARR by end-2026**
- ~480 MW AI capacity in 2026
- ~1.21 GW in build for 2027
- expansion across a secured 5 GW pipeline afterward.

### Risk

Extremely high.

The primary risks aren't ordinary stock volatility. They are:

- enormous capital requirements
- GPU obsolescence
- financing dependence
- construction/execution risk
- AI-cloud pricing compression
- customer concentration
- technology changes
- power availability
- dilution
- Bitcoin economics during the transition
- uncertain terminal returns on capital.

That makes IREN a **young/high-growth company**, not a mature data-center REIT.

# 4. The Most Important Change: AI Cloud

The key fact is that AI Cloud revenue went from:

**$3M → $16M → $58M nine-month FY26**

while Bitcoin mining remains much larger today.

But management has signed dramatically larger AI contracts.

### Microsoft

IREN signed a **$9.7B, five-year Microsoft agreement**, involving 200 MW of critical IT load and an expected approximately **$1.94B annualized run-rate revenue** when fully commissioned. ([IREN][4])

### NVIDIA

IREN subsequently signed a **$3.4B five-year AI Cloud contract with NVIDIA**, involving Blackwell GPUs and 60 MW of capacity, with deployment targeted from early 2027.

NVIDIA also established a strategic partnership supporting up to **5 GW** of NVIDIA-aligned AI infrastructure and received a five-year right to purchase up to 30M IREN shares at $70/share. ([NVIDIA Investor Relations][5])

This substantially changes the quality of the revenue story.

The question is no longer:

> "Can IREN find customers?"

It is increasingly:

> **"Can IREN build the infrastructure economically enough to earn an adequate return on the enormous capital being deployed?"**

That is the valuation-critical question.

# 5. Industry Economics

IREN sits at the intersection of three industries:

### Bitcoin mining

Low differentiation, commodity economics, high electricity sensitivity.

### Data centers

Scarce power and land can create meaningful competitive advantages.

### AI cloud

Potentially very attractive growth but intense capital requirements and rapid technology obsolescence.

The critical scarce resource isn't simply GPUs.

It is:

> **Power + grid connection + land + cooling + data-center construction + financing + customer contracts.**

This is why IREN can potentially earn attractive returns even though GPUs themselves are commoditized.

---

# 6. Competitive Advantage

I would rate IREN's current competitive advantage:

## **Moderate → potentially Strong**

But I would not call it exceptional yet.

The strongest advantage is **power infrastructure**.

IREN has accumulated approximately 4.5 GW of secured power and is building toward a 5 GW global pipeline.

That matters because obtaining power and grid connections can take years.

The second advantage is **execution experience**.

IREN has already built large-scale energy-intensive data centers.

The third is **vertical integration**.

IREN controls:

- land
- electrical infrastructure
- data centers
- GPUs
- software
- customer delivery.

IREN has also acquired Mirantis to strengthen compute deployment and management capabilities.

### But there is an important caveat

Power isn't automatically a moat.

If every MW ultimately earns commodity-like returns, owning power merely gives IREN an asset.

The moat only becomes economically meaningful if:

**Scarce power → higher utilization/pricing → superior ROIC**

That has not yet been demonstrated over a full cycle.

# 7. Historical ROIC

This is where I become much more cautious.

As of March 2026, IREN had:

- $2.21B cash
- $4.37B PP&E
- $3.69B convertible notes
- ~$274M finance leases
- $2.66B book equity. ([SEC][1])

Yet nine-month FY26 operating income was approximately **-$426M**. ([SEC][1])

Therefore:

> **Current ROIC is negative.**

This is not surprising for a company undergoing a massive buildout, but it means investors are paying today for **future ROIC**, not current ROIC.

That is a critical distinction.

---

# 8. Why EBITDA Is Dangerous Here

IREN reported $226.5M of adjusted EBITDA for the first nine months of FY26.

But adjusted EBITDA is not the same thing as economic earnings.

The company spent:

- **$1.67B on PP&E**
- **$686M on computer hardware**
- **$108M on intangible assets**

during the first nine months of FY26. ([SEC][1])

Operating cash flow was only **$289M** against more than **$2.6B of investing expenditures**. ([SEC][1])

This is perhaps the single most important accounting conclusion:

> **IREN's current EBITDA dramatically understates the capital required to build the business.**

Therefore I would **not** value IREN using an aggressive EBITDA multiple.

The economics must ultimately be judged through FCFF and ROIC.

# 11. Cost of Capital

Damodaran's July 1, 2026 framework gives a U.S. implied equity risk premium around **4.18%**, with a U.S. Treasury rate around **4.45%**. ([Stern School of Business][7])

For IREN, I would not use a normal mature data-center WACC.

The business has materially higher:

- operating risk
- financing risk
- technology risk
- customer concentration
- earnings uncertainty.

I therefore use:

### Base WACC: **11.5%**

Sensitivity:

| Scenario    |      WACC |
| ----------- | --------: |

This is deliberately higher than a mature infrastructure company.

Damodaran's January 2026 U.S. industry data show how much normal mature infrastructure valuations depend on lower risk and more stable capital structures; IREN does not yet possess those characteristics. ([Stern School of Business][8])

# 12. Primary DCF

I use a 10-year FCFF model.

The important thing is that I don't simply forecast revenue and slap a multiple onto it.

I explicitly connect:

**Revenue → EBIT → NOPAT → reinvestment → FCFF**

and tie reinvestment to expected growth and ROIC.

# 13. Why 40%+ EBIT Margins Matter

Suppose IREN eventually produces:

**$9B revenue × 42% EBIT = $3.8B EBIT**

At an 18% tax rate:

**NOPAT ≈ $3.1B**

If it can sustain a ~22% ROIC while growing at 3%:

**reinvestment ≈ 14% of NOPAT**

leaving roughly:

**$2.7B terminal FCFF**

That can support a very substantial valuation.

But if mature EBIT margins are only 30%, the economics change dramatically.

This is why **margin is one of the three most important variables in the entire valuation.**

# 14. Terminal Value

I use:

### Stable growth: 3%

### Stable ROIC: ~15%

### Stable WACC: ~11.5%

This is deliberately conservative.

Why 15% ROIC?

Because I do not believe a reasonable terminal assumption should be:

> "IREN will permanently earn 30%+ returns on capital."

That would imply a durable competitive advantage far beyond what has yet been demonstrated.

The terminal value represents approximately **50%+ of the DCF enterprise value**.

That is high enough that terminal assumptions deserve enormous scrutiny.

# 22. Why I Would Not Value IREN at CoreWeave's Multiple

CoreWeave currently deserves a premium because it has:

- greater revenue scale
- more established AI-cloud operations
- enormous customer commitments
- proven AI workload demand.

IREN is earlier in the transition.

Therefore I would **not** simply say:

> "CoreWeave is worth 6.5x sales, therefore IREN should be worth 6.5x sales."

That violates the central Damodaran principle.

The correct question is:

> **Does IREN deserve a similar multiple after adjusting for growth, margins, reinvestment and risk?**

My answer:

**Not yet.**

# 23. The Most Important Economic Question

The entire IREN thesis ultimately reduces to:

# **What return will IREN earn on each incremental MW of AI infrastructure?**

Suppose IREN spends $10M to create infrastructure capable of generating $3M annual revenue.

If mature EBIT margin is 40%:

**EBIT = $1.2M**

After tax:

**NOPAT ≈ $1M**

That is approximately:

**10% ROIC**

Not particularly exciting given IREN's risk.

But if that same $10M produces $4M revenue at 45% EBIT margin:

NOPAT ≈ $1.48M

ROIC ≈ **15%**

Better.

At $5M revenue:

ROIC could exceed **18%**.

This is why **revenue per MW and capital cost per MW** are far more important than headline ARR.

# 24. Growth Quality

I classify IREN's potential growth as:

## **High quality if monetization exceeds capital cost**

but

## **Low quality if revenue growth merely requires proportional capital expenditure.**

This is the crucial Damodaran distinction.

If:

**ROIC > WACC**

growth creates value.

If:

**ROIC < WACC**

growth destroys value.

A company can grow revenue 50% annually while destroying shareholder value.

IREN has not yet demonstrated that its future growth will consistently exceed its cost of capital.

---

# 25. Reinvestment Risk

This is probably the biggest hidden risk in the valuation.

IREN generated:

**$289M operating cash flow**

while spending:

**~$2.61B on investing activities**

in nine months. ([SEC][1])

The business therefore requires enormous external capital during its growth phase.

That creates a feedback loop:

**More AI demand → more construction → more GPUs → more capital → more financing → more dilution/debt → more capacity → more revenue**

If returns are high, this is wonderful.

If returns deteriorate, the same mechanism becomes destructive.

---

# 26. Financing Risk

This is why IREN cannot be analyzed solely as an operating business.

The company raised:

- billions through equity
- billions through convertible debt
- and continues to explore additional financing. ([SEC][1])

The capital structure is therefore part of the investment thesis.

If IREN's cost of capital rises substantially:

**DCF value falls sharply.**

If equity markets become unavailable:

**growth slows.**

If GPU financing becomes expensive:

**ROIC falls.**

If IREN must issue enormous quantities of stock:

**per-share intrinsic value falls even if enterprise value increases.**

# 28. Accounting Quality

I would classify reported earnings quality as:

## **Low/Medium**

Not because I see evidence of fraudulent accounting.

Rather because reported earnings contain enormous:

- depreciation
- impairment
- derivative gains/losses
- convertible accounting
- stock compensation
- noncash financing effects.

For example, nine-month FY26 included a **$534M unrealized gain on financial instruments**, while Q3 contained a $23.7M unrealized loss. ([SEC][1])

Those numbers can overwhelm GAAP earnings while having little relationship to normalized operating economics.

Therefore:

### Do not use:

- P/E
- trailing EPS
- GAAP net income

as primary valuation metrics.

### Prefer:

- normalized EBIT
- NOPAT
- FCFF
- ROIC
- capital/MW
- revenue/MW
- cash return on invested capital.

## Risk #2 — Capital intensity overwhelms returns
