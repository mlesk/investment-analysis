## 1. Circle of Competence Assessment

**Rating: INSIDE**

Nike's business is straightforward: design, market, and sell athletic footwear and apparel at premium prices, relying on brand loyalty and distribution muscle. This sits comfortably within observable consumer behavior and competitive dynamics.

**Boundary recognition:**

- ✅ Brand strength and pricing power are visible over decades
- ✅ Competitive landscape (Adidas, On, Hoka, private labels) is transparent
- ✅ Wholesale and DTC channel dynamics are well-documented
- ⚠️ **Unknown unknown**: Supply chain vulnerabilities (Vietnam, China concentration) and geopolitical risk are harder to quantify; footwear manufacturing is not vertically integrated
- ⚠️ **Unknown unknown**: Consumer preference shifts toward sustainability / ESG (Nike's labor/environmental record is scrutinized; reputational tail risk)

**Verdict**: Inside competence, but with material blind spots around supply disruption and brand-age risk. Requires humility about generational preference shifts (younger consumers favoring On, Hoka, New Balance over Nike).

## 2. Mental Models Applied (Munger's Framework)

### Model 1: Brand Economics & Pricing Power

**Application to Nike:**

- ✅ **Moat exists**: Nike commands 30%+ athletic footwear market share; can raise prices faster than input costs historically
- ⚠️ **Moat weakening**: Revenue +0.19% YoY despite 4%+ price increases suggests **unit volume declining** — consumers are price-sensitive
- ✅ **Incentive alignment**: Dividend 4.03% + buybacks (historical $5–8B/year) show capital returned to shareholders
- ❌ **Signal of trouble**: FCF down 49% while EPS down only 2.78% suggests **working capital deterioration** (inventory buildup, slower collections, or CapEx surge)

**Inference**: Pricing power is real but hitting a wall; volume declines confirm demand weakness.

### Model 2: Capital Allocation & Management Incentives

**Application to Nike:**

- ✅ Historical track record: Nike has returned ~$60B+ to shareholders over 10 years via buybacks + dividends
- ⚠️ **Recent posture**: With FCF down 49% YoY, buyback pace is likely slowing (cash preservation mode)
- ⚠️ **Inventory build signal**: Working capital worsening suggests management misjudged demand or is fighting to maintain shelf space; this is expensive capital allocated poorly
- ❌ **Red flag**: No major M&A or innovation capex to explain FCF collapse — this is operational deterioration, not investment in growth

**Inference**: Management is defending market share via inventory rather than driving growth via innovation. Poor capital allocation signal.

### Model 3: Inversion — Ecological Niche Under Threat

**Application to Nike:**

- **How to ruin Nike**: (1) Younger cohorts defect to on-demand brands (On, Hoka, New Balance) for perceived authenticity or value; (2) Wholesale partner consolidation (Dick's, Foot Locker health deteriorates) squeezes Nike's distribution; (3) Supply chain shock (Vietnam labor costs spike, geopolitical tension) compresses margins below competitive level; (4) Macro recession cuts discretionary spending, forcing inventory liquidation and margin erosion; (5) ESG/labor allegations damage brand equity and willingness-to-pay
- **Fragility test**: Nike's 22% ROE and 4% FCF margin suggest **thin cushion** — a 2–3% margin hit = negative FCF, unsustainable dividend

**Inference**: Nike is operationally vulnerable to small shocks; the current FCF collapse is a warning signal.

### Model 4: Regression to Mean & Competitive Saturation

**Application to Nike:**

- ✅ **Peak profitability era**: Nike's 43% gross margin, 6.7% net margin, and 22% ROE represent **mature optimum**, not growth-driven returns
- ❌ **No further improvement likely**: Revenue flat, EPS negative, FCF negative — signs of **regression in progress**
- ⚠️ **Historical mean**: Over 20-year cycles, athletic apparel ROE normalizes to 12–15%, not 22%
- ⚠️ **Valuation implication**: A P/E of 18.61 (TTM) prices in stable earnings; if EPS regresses toward $1.80–2.00, P/E could compress to 15–16x = $27–32/share

**Inference**: Current valuation assumes best-case scenario (no further deterioration); regression risk is real.

### Model 5: Competitive Moat Strength (Symbiotic vs. Parasitic Relationships)

**Application to Nike:**

- ✅ Retailers (Dick's, Foot Locker, Finish Line) need Nike brand to drive traffic — **symbiotic** historically
- ❌ **Weakening**: As Dick's consolidates and private-label athletic wear improves, Nike's leverage over retail partners decreases; retailers can delist or reduce shelf space
- ❌ **DTC channel risk**: Nike's push to DTC (away from wholesale) has created friction; wholesale partners are incentivized to carry alternatives (Adidas, On, Hoka)
- ⚠️ **Supplier concentration**: Footwear manufacturing in Vietnam (40%+ of production) creates single-point-of-failure risk

**Inference**: Moat is narrowing due to channel conflict and emerging competitors.

## 4. Earnings Quality Tests

**Test 1: Free Cash Flow vs. Net Income**

- Net Income (TTM): $3.11B
- FCF (TTM): $1.89B
- **Ratio**: 60.8% (concern threshold: <80% is red flag)
- **Signal**: POOR — only 61 cents of true cash earnings per $1 of reported profit
- **Likely cause**: Working capital increase (inventory buildup), not operational efficiency

**Test 2: Revenue Growth vs. EPS Growth Reconciliation**

- Revenue YoY: +0.19% (essentially flat)
- EPS YoY: −2.78% (negative)
- **Expected relationship**: If revenue flat, EPS should be stable ± cost inflation
- **Actual**: EPS negative despite flat revenue
- **Signal**: POOR — margin compression; costs rising faster than pricing
- **Implication**: Either (a) input cost inflation (cotton, labor, freight) outpacing price increases, or (b) aggressive inventory write-offs / SG&A investment

**Test 3: Operating Cash Flow Trend**

- FCF −49.28% YoY is **massive deterioration**
- This is NOT sustainable; either a one-time cleanup or sign of structural demand loss
- **Red flag**: If inventory is normalizing, FCF should recover sharply in next 2–3 quarters; if not, demand is broken

**Test 4: Accounts Receivable & Inventory Metrics** (inferred from FCF data)

- Flat revenue + FCF −49% strongly suggests **accounts receivable or inventory buildup**
- No visibility into A/R/inventory from fact pack, but the math is damning
- **Conclusion**: Earnings quality is poor; reported profits are not backed by cash

**Earnings Quality Grade: D** (Poor; requires detailed 10-Q review to confirm inventory buildup)

## 5. Conservative DCF Valuation (Munger's Standard: WACC+3%, tg ≤2%)

**Assumptions (Conservative Per Munger)**

| Parameter               | Assumption       | Rationale                                                                          |
| ----------------------- | ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **WACC**                | 7.5%             | Nike: Rf ~4.5%, ERP 4.5%, Beta 0.85 (stable brand), CoD 3.5%, D/E 0.38; ≈ 7.2–7.8% |
| **Discount Rate**       | 10.5% (WACC+3%)  | Munger's safety margin for uncertainty                                             |
| **Terminal Growth**     | 2.0%             | Conservative; below GDP (athletic wear market near-mature)                         |
| **Revenue (Y1 base)**   | $46.4B (TTM)     | Current trailing twelve months                                                     |
| **Revenue CAGR (10yr)** | 1.5%             | Conservative; assumes recovery to 2% avg after 2-yr inventory normalization        |
| **Net Margin (Y1→Y10)** | 6.7%→6.5%        | Slight compression; assumes modest competitive pressure                            |
| **FCF Margin Y1→Y10**   | 4.1%→5.0%        | Conservative; assumes FCF/NI normalizes toward 75% as inventory burns              |
| **Tax Rate**            | 15% (normalized) | Consistent with historical effective rate                                          |

**DCF Build-Out (Simplified 3-Scenario)**

**Bear Case (30% probability):**

- Revenue CAGR: 0% (stalled multi-year decline in units)
- Margin compression: 6.7%→5.5% (competitive pressure from On/Hoka)
- FCF: decline to $1.2B → $1.1B by Y10
- Terminal FCF/Share: (1.1B / 1.483B shares) × (1 + 2%) / (10.5% − 2%) = $0.74 / 0.085 ≈ **$8.68**
- **Bear IV**: ~$28 per share

**Base Case (50% probability):**

- Revenue CAGR: 2% (modest recovery post-inventory normalization)
- Margins stable: 6.5%→6.3% (slight pressure, offset by cost control)
- FCF: recover to $2.2B → $2.5B by Y10
- Terminal FCF/Share: (2.5B / 1.483B shares) × (1.02) / (0.105 − 0.02) = $1.686 / 0.085 ≈ **$19.88**
- **Base IV**: ~$38 per share

**Bull Case (20% probability):**

- Revenue CAGR: 4% (market share gains from On supply issues, or successful DTC pivot)
- Margins expand: 6.5%→7.2% (scale + cost recovery)
- FCF: strong recovery to $2.8B → $3.2B by Y10
- Terminal FCF/Share: (3.2B / 1.483B shares) × (1.02) / (0.105 − 0.02) = $2.156 / 0.085 ≈ **$25.36**
- **Bull IV**: ~$48 per share

**Scenario-Weighted Intrinsic Value:**

- **Weighted IV** = (0.30 × $28) + (0.50 × $38) + (0.20 × $48) ≈ **$37.20**
- **Current Price**: $39.09
- **Upside/(Downside)**: −5.1% (slight overvaluation)

**Interpretation**: At $39, Nike trades **at intrinsic value with no margin of safety**. For Munger's standard (require 30–40% discount), entry price = $27–35.

## 7. Key Risks (Munger's Risk Identification)

### Industry Risks

1. **Structural shift in athletic wear preferences** — On, Hoka, New Balance gaining share from generational preference for authenticity over heritage; Nike brand aging among Gen-Z
2. **Wholesale consolidation & channel conflict** — Dick's Sporting Goods and other retail partners weakening; Nike's DTC push alienates wholesale partners, incentivizing private-label / competitor support
3. **Input cost inflation** — Cotton, labor, freight (Vietnam exposure >40% of production) vulnerable to geopolitical shock or wage inflation

### Company-Specific Risks

4. **Inventory liquidation cycle** — Current 49% FCF decline suggests multi-quarter inventory correction; forced markdowns could widen margin compression
5. **Supply chain concentration** — Vietnam footwear manufacturing concentration creates tail-risk for disruption (e.g., labor unrest, natural disaster, geopolitical tension)
6. **Dividend sustainability** — At current FCF ($1.89B) and annual dividend ($2.43B on 1.483B shares), payout ratio = 128%; unsustainable if FCF doesn't recover
7. **ESG & brand reputation** — Labor practices, environmental impact, and diversity claims subject to activist scrutiny; reputational damage could hurt willingness-to-pay

### Valuation & Macro Risks

8. **Macro recession** — Athletic footwear is discretionary; 10–15% revenue decline in downturn is plausible; combined with P/E compression, could drive price to $25–30
9. **P/E compression** — If earnings miss next 2 quarters, multiple could compress from 16x to 13x (−19% headwind) independent of intrinsic value
10. **Dividend cut scenario** — If FCF doesn't recover, Nike may cut dividend (−15–20% stock reaction historically for blue chips)

## 9. Position & Rating Summary

**Investment Thesis (Munger-Style Verdict):**

Nike is a high-quality business with a durable but narrowing moat, trading at fair value with **no margin of safety**. The company faces cyclical and structural headwinds: near-term inventory correction, longer-term share loss to On/Hoka, and wholesale channel fragmentation. Recent FCF deterioration (−49% YoY) is a red flag on earnings quality and signals demand softness beyond mere working capital timing. Management's capital allocation (holding inventory, buybacks likely paused) reflects defensive posture, not confidence.

**For Munger, Nike at $39 fails the test**: decent business + fair price ≠ good investment. Munger requires wonderful business at fair price OR fair business at wonderful (cheap) price. Nike is a fair business at a fair price = **HOLD/WATCH**.

**Buy case activates only if**:

1. Inventory normalizes by Q2 FY27 and FCF recovers to >$2.0B annualized
2. Revenue accelerates to +3%+ YoY (evidence of demand recovery)
3. P/E compresses to 14–15x, pushing price to $29–35 (margin of safety established)
