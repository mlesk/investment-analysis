## I. BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING & FUNDAMENTAL STORY

### What Nike Is

Nike designs, markets, and sells athletic footwear, apparel, and equipment globally. It does not manufacture; instead, it outsources 100% of production to contract manufacturers (Yue Yuen, Smasung, Foxconn equivalents) and keeps gross margins at 43% while competitors like Adidas run 40–42%.

**Revenue mix (estimate):** 60% footwear / 25% apparel / 15% equipment & other. Geographic: ~50% North America, 20% EMEA, 15% APAC, 15% Rest of World. Distribution: ~25% DTC (company-owned, higher margin), 40% wholesale (wholesale partners, lower margin), 35% licensing/other.

**Why customers pay:** Nike owns the single strongest brand in athletic apparel and footwear globally. Consumers pay 20–50% premiums vs off-brand equivalents for Swoosh association, performance credentials, and fashion/lifestyle positioning. The brand has no true peer in athletic culture; even Adidas and Puma are significantly weaker.

### Economic Structure: Cash Flow Drivers

1. **Gross Margin: 43.25% (TTM).** Driven by product mix (premium footwear + DTC sales), contract manufacturing at scale, supply-chain efficiency, and currency hedging. Downside: wholesale channel pressure (lower margin, 20–25% vs 50%+ DTC); upside: DTC mix shift (+2–3% gross margin if DTC reaches 35% of revenue).

2. **Operating Margin: ~9.5% (TTM, SG&A at ~34% of revenue).** Governed by DTC labor (stores, digital), marketing spend, R&D (product development), and corporate overhead. SG&A scale is high due to large wholesale SG&A footprint and marketing requirements to defend brand.

3. **Capital Intensity: Low.** CapEx historically 2–2.5% of revenue ($1.0–1.2B annually). Working-capital model favorable: Nike receives payment from wholesale partners upfront or net-30, while paying factories net-90. This creates a ~$2–3B permanent working-capital float (cash conversion cycle is negative, which is rare and valuable).

4. **Reinvestment Needs: Moderate.** Athletic footwear cycles demand continuous product investment (new designs, innovation, sports sponsorships). R&D is ~4–5% of revenue but drives pricing power and brand strength.

5. **Financing Structure:** Debt $11.04B, equity $26B+, debt/equity 55%. Investment-grade credit (low default risk). Net debt $2B (after cash).

### Fundamental Value Drivers: Cash Flow + Growth + Risk

**Cash Flow:** Nike converts ~10% of revenue into free cash flow in normalized years ($4.6B revenue → $4–5B FCFF pre-growth). This is exceptionally high compared to most consumer goods (3–5% is typical). The working-capital float is a hidden cash engine.

**Growth:** Historical (5-year): Revenue CAGR ~5%, but recent inflection (flat YoY in 2026) reflects wholesale softness and China deceleration. Normalized forward growth: 3–6% in developed markets (U.S. = 70% of earnings) + upside optionality from China stabilization and DTC acceleration. The valuation story hinges on whether this is a cycle (2–3 year trough) or a structural decline.

**Risk:** Beta 1.12 suggests cyclical sensitivity above market. Key risks: (1) China exposure (~20% revenue, declining) and geopolitical decoupling, (2) wholesale partner health (Dick's, Foot Locker, etc. face pressure), (3) fashion/product cycle risk (trends shift), (4) labor cost inflation, (5) currency headwinds (strong $ hurts overseas earnings). WACC ~8.5–9% is reasonable for premium brand with wide moat.

### Business Lifecycle: Mature But Still Growing

Nike is **Mature + Growth = Late Growth stage.** ~$46B revenue places it in the top tier of consumer discretionary. But the company has significant growth vectors: (1) DTC digital penetration (from 10% of sales currently, could reach 20%+), (2) international emerging markets, (3) adjacent categories (bags, eyewear, apparel cross-selling). This is not a declining business, but it is not young/high-growth either (20%+ CAGR is behind it).

**Valuation Model:** Two-stage DCF (5-year explicit forecast, terminal value at stable growth).

## III. COMPETITIVE ADVANTAGE & ECONOMIC MOAT

### Moat Assessment

**Brand / Pricing Power (VERY STRONG):** Swoosh is the most recognized athletic brand globally. Consumers will pay 30–50% premiums vs generic athletic brands and 15–25% vs Adidas. This moat is durable for 10+ years (sports endorsements, product innovation, fashion cycles all reinforce it). **Durability: 8/10.**

**Scale & Manufacturing Relationships (STRONG):** Nike's scale with contract manufacturers (Yue Yuen 30%, Smasung 15%, others 55%) gives it better unit economics and supply-chain agility than Adidas, Puma, or Saucony. Working relationships span 25+ years in many cases. **Durability: 7/10.**

**Distribution Network (MODERATE):** Nike's DTC footprint (950 stores, ~$10B DTC revenue) and wholesale relationships (Dick's, Foot Locker, JD Sports, etc.) are valuable, but increasingly are going digital. The competitive advantage here is eroding as others build e-commerce. **Durability: 6/10.**

**Network Effects / Halo (VERY STRONG):** Sports marketing spend ($1.5–2B annually on athlete sponsorships, team partnerships) creates powerful network effects. LeBron, Serena, Messi, Ronaldo, Giannis—all Nike—reinforce aspiration and cool factor. This is self-reinforcing: winning athletes drive shoe sales, more sales fund more sponsorships. **Durability: 8/10.**

**Overall Moat Rating: STRONG. Score 7.5/10 (on 10-pt scale).** Nike has durable competitive advantages that justify premium pricing for 10–15 years. However, threats exist: Lululemon's ascent in lifestyle, emerging competition from China (Li-Ning, Anta), and luxury brands (Gucci, Louis Vuitton) poaching market share at the high end.

## XIV. MARKET EXPECTATIONS & REVERSE-DCF INTERPRETATION

The market is currently pricing Nike at $39.09, implying:

1. **China is not recovering in 5 years** (structural decline priced in).
2. **Gross margin stays compressed** (no recovery to 45.5%+).
3. **DTC penetration plateaus** (no operating leverage).
4. **Terminal growth is 1.5–2.0%** (vs. our 2.5%).

This is **too pessimistic** given:

- Nike's brand strength and pricing power (moat is intact).
- Historical evidence of margin recovery in past cycles.
- Early signs of China stabilization (Q2 FY2026 China down 6% vs. Q1 down 11%).
- DTC penetration still has runway (25–30% is achievable vs. peers at 35–40%).

The market has conflated **near-term cyclical weakness with long-term structural decline.** This is a classic valuation opportunity.

## XV. FIVE KEY CONCLUSIONS

1. **Nike's brand moat is durable and worth ~$79B** (base-case equity value) vs. the $58B market cap. The Swoosh commands premium pricing, drives loyalty, and justifies operating margins 2–3x the industry average.

2. **Margin recovery is the hidden upside.** Gross margin fell from 47.5% → 43.25% due to wholesale mix and inventory clearing. Normalized recovery to 45–46% is reasonable by Year 2–3, driving 250+ bps EBIT margin expansion. This alone is worth $15–20B of value.

3. **China is the key variable, not a terminal decline.** If China stabilizes (realistic 1–2% growth by Year 3), Nike's 3–4% organic growth is achievable. Current pricing assumes China is dead; that's wrong.

4. **DTC digital acceleration is underappreciated.** Nike's penetration of DTC is 25% of revenue; peers (Lululemon) are 90%+. Moving to 30–35% DTC would drive 200+ bps gross margin expansion and scale SG&A, unlocking 15–20% value.

5. **The risk/reward is asymmetric to the upside.** Downside: $2–15 (Severe Bear / Bear, 30% risk). Base: $53 (realistic). Upside: $96–163 (Bull / Extreme Bull, 35% probability combined). Expected return is positive at current price.
