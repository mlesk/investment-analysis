# Part II — The Consensus Synthesis

## 1. The Business (What You Actually Own)

Nike designs, markets, and distributes athletic footwear (~2/3 of sales), apparel, and equipment globally under the Nike, Jordan, Converse, ACG, and NikeSkims brands, selling through wholesale (~18,000 accounts, incl. ~5,500 franchised China stores), own-retail, and e-commerce in 40+ countries — production nearly fully outsourced, an asset-light brand-and-IP model.

**The consensus story:**

> **Nike is a genuinely great consumer brand — simple, durable-category, fortress balance sheet, #1 global share and premium pricing intact — whose moat is eroding and whose earnings power has roughly halved, at a 12-year low just above its 52-week low. You own a wounded-but-not-dead franchise whose entire value hinges on whether the current reset is a recoverable cycle or a permanent structural decline.**

## 2. The Economics (The One Variable That Matters)

Every profile, through different vocabularies, reaches the same hinge:

$$ \text{Value is created only if} \quad \text{China stabilizes} \times \text{Margins normalize} > \text{Cost of capital} \; (\approx 8\text{–}9\%) $$

- If China stabilizes (from −11% toward flat/+) **and** gross margin normalizes toward ~45.5% with EBIT toward ~12%, Nike re-earns its old economics and Damodaran's **$53.33** base (weighted $62.22) is the relevant value.
- If the share loss (China −590 bps; On/Hoka/Brooks ~17% combined) is structural, margins settle lower and the **$34–40** fundamental cluster (Buffett / Munger / Soros) caps value — and $39.09 is fair, not cheap.

**Consensus monitoring hierarchy:** `Greater China revenue growth > EBIT margin recovery > FCF/diluted share > DTC vs wholesale mix > dividend policy`.

## 3. Management

Elliott Hill's turnaround is credible, candid, and insider-aligned (CEO and Tim Cook bought at $42–43 in April 2026), and the balance sheet buys time — but it is entirely unproven: revenue is flat-to-down, EPS is guided flat for three quarters, buybacks are paused, the dividend payout is ~104%, the CFO was recently replaced, and JPM sees self-inflicted headwinds into FY28. **6/10.**
