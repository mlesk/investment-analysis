# Cipher Digital Inc. (NASDAQ: CIFR) — Buffett-Style Value Investment Analysis

_Prepared: August 16, 2026 · Reference price ~$17.86 (Aug 14, 2026 close) · Market cap ~$7.4B · 415M shares outstanding_

---

## Bottom Line First

Cipher Digital is executing one of the more credible transformations in the AI-infrastructure space — a pivot from **self-mining Bitcoin** to **owning and leasing turnkey HPC data centers to hyperscale tenants** under long-term contracts. The company has signed three data-center campus leases (Amazon, Fluidstack backed by Google, and one undisclosed investment-grade hyperscaler) that management says will generate approximately **$793M of average annualized net operating income from October 2026 through September 2036**, and it has assembled a **5.3 GW portfolio across 11 sites**. Execution has been genuinely impressive: Black Pearl capacity was delivered two months early, three project-level bond financings closed at progressively lower coupons (7.125% → 6.125% → 6.0%), and the team has replaced most of its mining business with contracted, investment-grade landlord economics.

**But this is not currently a classic Buffett value investment.** The catch is in the capital structure: the contracted campuses are funded **~98% with non-recourse project debt that amortizes during the base lease terms**, meaning the near-term cash flows of the landlord business largely accrue to **debt holders, not equity holders**. The equity's cash flows are thin and back-loaded, leverage is extreme (debt/equity ~9.5x, total debt principal >$6B), the company has diluted shareholders ~13% in the past year alone, and the enormous 4.4 GW development pipeline — the source of most of the stock's optionality — remains un-leased and is subject to ERCOT's evolving Texas interconnection process, which is itself under new political scrutiny.

My conclusion using a **Buffett-style framework**:

> **Rating: C — Watch / Speculative Value**
>
> **Not a Buy under a strict Buffett framework at ~$17.86/share.**
>
> I would become substantially more interested around **$10–12**, and I would regard **$7–9 as potentially compelling** if the contracted leases continue to deliver and the pipeline signs new tenants. I would treat **<$7** as a high-conviction value zone under a debt-aware, cash-flow-to-equity framework.

The central question is not whether Cipher can grow — it almost certainly can. The question is:

> **How much of that contracted NOI will actually reach equity holders per share, after project debt service, corporate costs, warrant and convertible overhangs, and the capital required to keep building?**

That distinction — between a _landlord whose rent first repays its mortgages_ and a _landlord who distributes cash to shareholders_ — is the entire investment question.

# 2. First: What Exactly Is Cipher?

The most important thing to understand is the business-model shift.

Cipher was founded in 2021 (via a SPAC merger with Good Works Acquisition Corp.) as **Cipher Mining**, a pure-play Bitcoin miner. ([SEC][3], [SEC][11], [9], [10], [12]) It still mines Bitcoin at its Odessa facility (207 MW, ~11.6 EH/s at ~17.2 J/TH). ([2])

But beginning in late 2025, management pivoted the company into a **data-center landlord (developer/owner/operator)** model:

> **Cipher sources land and power, designs and builds industrial-scale HPC data centers, and leases them turnkey to hyperscale tenants on long-term contracts — then finances the construction at the project level with non-recourse debt.**

In simple terms, Cipher is becoming **a build-to-suit landlord for hyperscale compute**, with Bitcoin mining retained as an interim use of power and a conversion candidate. ([3], [2])

The three contracted campus leases are:

| Campus                              | Tenant                                   | Capacity                                 | Lease                    | Key terms                                                                                                  |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Barber Lake** (Colorado City, TX) | Fluidstack (backstopped by **Google**)   | 300 MW gross (244 MW Ph I + 56 MW Ph II) | 10-yr + two 5-yr options | Ph I delivery by Sep 2026, rent Oct 2026; Google backstop via Recognition Agreement + 24.2M warrants ([3]) |
| **Black Pearl** (Wink, TX)          | **Amazon (AWS)**                         | ~300 MW                                  | 15-yr                    | First capacity delivered Aug 2026 (2 months early), rent commenced; fully ramped by Q1 2027 ([3], [2])     |
| **Stingray** (West TX)              | Undisclosed investment-grade hyperscaler | ~100 MW                                  | long-term                | $810M bond financing completed; delivery H1 2027 ([2])                                                     |

Together these three leases are expected to generate **~$793M of average annualized net operating income from October 2026 through September 2036**. ([2]) That is the heart of the equity story — roughly **$7.9B of contracted NOI over a decade** from three investment-grade counterparties, on top of the 207 MW Odessa mining site.

Beyond the contracted campuses sits the **4.4 GW pipeline** (Apollo 900 MW, Colchis ~1 GW, Mikeska 500 MW, McLennan 500 MW, Milsing 500 MW, Reveille 70 MW, Ulysses 200 MW, plus Barber Lake +500 MW and Stingray +200 MW expansions) — total portfolio **~5.3 GW across 11 sites**. ([2])

# 3. Circle of Competence

## Is the business understandable?

**The mining business (Odessa):** Very simple — buy cheap power (~2.7–2.8 c/kWh), run ASICs, earn Bitcoin, sell it. ([3]) This is a commodity business in which no one controls the price of Bitcoin. Buffett-grade moat: none.

**The HPC landlord business:** Conceptually simple — own land and power rights, build a data center, sign a 10–15 year lease with a hyperscaler, collect rent, and let project debt amortize against that rent. That is a real-estate/build-to-suit model, not a technology model. Understandable.

**The complication** is the **capital structure and cash waterfall**:

1. Construction is funded ~98% by **project-level senior secured notes** that "amortize during the base lease terms." ([2], [3])
2. During the amortization window, a large share of the NOI services that debt.
3. The equity residual is **back-loaded** — it grows as the debt amortizes and only fully "flips" to equity cash flow when the notes are repaid (or refinanced).
4. On top of the project debt sit **$1.47B of corporate convertibles** (2030 1.75% and 2031 0.00%), a **$632M Google warrant liability** (24.2M shares at $0.01), and a **$600M ATM equity program**. ([3], [1])

So while the _business_ is simple, the _economics to equity_ are genuinely hard to estimate. A $793M NOI headline sounds wonderful; the question is what fraction of it ever reaches shareholders.

### Circle-of-competence score: **5/10**

I understand the model. I do **not** yet trust the long-term per-share economics enough to put Cipher in the same mental bucket as a toll road or See's Candies.

# 4. Will This Business Still Be Needed in 10 Years?

Almost certainly **yes**, at the portfolio level.

The contracted tenants are among the most sophisticated companies on earth (Amazon, Google). AI/HPC compute demand continues to grow, and the scarce inputs — **megawatts of reliable power, land, grid interconnection, and construction capacity** — are exactly what Cipher controls. ([3], [2])

The question is not whether data-center capacity will be needed in 2036. It almost certainly will be. The question is:

> **Who captures the economic profit — the landlord, the tenant, the lender, or the next developer?**

That is much harder, and it depends on the lease terms (which are largely confidential), the cost of capital over the next decade, and whether the rent escalators outpace power/inflation. ([3])

# 5. Will Technology Fundamentally Change the Industry?

**Yes — this is a real risk, but differently than for a GPU owner.**

Because Cipher is a **landlord rather than a GPU operator**, it is largely insulated from GPU obsolescence (unlike IREN, which owns and depreciates the compute). Cipher's tenants bring their own GPUs and IT. That is an important structural advantage of the colocation/lease model. ([2])

However, technology can still hurt Cipher in three ways:

1. **Power density and design:** if rack densities or cooling requirements shift materially, today's shell-and-core designs could need retrofits — a capital cost that may land on the landlord.
2. **AI demand cyclicality:** if the AI capex cycle turns, hyperscalers may slow new site commitments; existing leases are sticky, but the **pipeline** (4.4 GW) is where the value sits, and that depends on continued tenant appetite. ([3])
3. **Efficiency gains:** if compute becomes dramatically more efficient per watt, the _power bottleneck_ that makes Cipher's sites valuable could loosen. The 10-K itself flags that improved AI model efficiency "could potentially adversely affect data center usage by significantly reducing the computational power needed." ([3])

None of these is a near-term threat to the three signed leases. All of them matter enormously to the **pipeline valuation** that underpins much of today's stock price.

# 6. Economic Moat

The moat — if it exists — is **not "AI."** It is:

> **Power + land + grid interconnection + construction capability + speed + capital access.**

That is the same hypothesis the market applies to IREN, CORZ and the other miner-to-landlord converts, and it is a legitimate one. Let me score each component.

## 6.1 Intangible Assets

- **Brand:** ~1/10. No consumer brand. What matters is reputation with hyperscalers, and there Cipher is building credibility (early delivery at Black Pearl; 95–100% equipment procurement; ex-hyperscaler leadership hires). ([2])
- **Patents/licenses:** ~2/10. Four US patents (expiring 2043–44) plus proprietary data-center software. Not moat-relevant. ([3])
- **Regulatory/interconnection barriers:** this is the real "license." Texas SB 6, ERCOT's new **batch interconnection process** and required security deposits create genuine friction for new entrants, and Cipher has advanced sites already in the process (Apollo, Colchis, Mikeska, McLennan, Milsing submitted for Batch Zero/Batch 1). ([3], [2]) A competitor cannot simply wake up tomorrow and plug a 1 GW site into ERCOT. This is valuable — but note it also works _against_ Cipher's un-energized pipeline: the August 2026 letter from Texas Governor Abbott calling for an **audit of the interconnection queue** shows the process can be delayed and re-architected at any time. ([2])

**Intangible-asset score: 3/10** (mostly a regulatory/power moat, not a brand/IP moat).

## 6.2 Switching Costs

**Moderately high — but tenant-favorable.**

Once a hyperscaler occupies a 300 MW campus custom-built to its specifications for 10–15 years, it is not trivial to relocate. But the tenant — Amazon, Google — is the one with the leverage and alternative options (build their own, use CoreWeave/Equinix/Digital Realty/Vantage, etc.). ([3]) The switching costs protect Cipher's _revenue stream_ more than they give Cipher _pricing power_.

**Score: 6/10** — the switching costs protect the revenue stream more than they confer pricing power on Cipher.

## 6.3 Network Effects

**None.** More tenants do not make Cipher's campuses more valuable to other tenants. This is not Visa.

**Score: 1/10** — no network effects exist in a build-to-suit landlord business.

## 6.4 Cost Advantage — The Most Important Moat

Cipher's strongest potential advantage:

- **Power-first site sourcing:** Odessa's Luminant PPA is ~2.7–2.8 c/kWh (take-or-pay on 66.7% of 207 MW) — low-cost power secured years ago. ([3])
- **Texas scale:** the majority of a 5.3 GW, 11-site portfolio sits in Texas, where Cipher has interconnection and land advantages. ([2])
- **In-house procurement and construction:** Cipher manages supply chain in-house (vs. competitors who outsource), has ~95–100% of equipment secured for current builds, and partners with Quanta Services. ([2], [3])
- **Speed:** delivered Black Pearl two months early at the tenant's request — a credibility point with hyperscalers. ([2])
- **Capital access:** three project financings (7.125%, 6.125%, 6.0% coupons) with repeated oversubscription — a genuine structural advantage in a crowded market. ([2])

**Cost-advantage score: 7/10** — potentially excellent, but **not yet proven at operating scale**. As of August 2026, Cipher has delivered its _first_ HPC capacity; no campus has operated a full year. The $9–11M/MW build-cost guidance is already trending up with inflation, and management concedes costs are "creeping higher." ([2])

## 6.5 Overall Moat

> **Current: Narrow. Potential: Moderate.**
>
> Not Wide — because the economics depend on scarce power/land/capital and on execution, all of which competitors (CoreWeave, Digital Realty, Equinix, Vantage, Aligned, and miner peers IREN/CORZ/HUT/WULF/APLD) are pursuing with equal vigor. ([3])

**Moat: Narrow, with a credible but unproven path to Moderate.**

# 7. Management Quality

## 7.1 Integrity & Shareholder Orientation

**Generally positive, with a caveat on compensation.**

- **Candor:** Management's calls are unusually candid about both execution (early delivery, financing tightness) and risks (ERCOT process, credit markets, tenant terms). ([2])
- **No fraud or related-party red flags found.** The Bitfury board-observer arrangement was terminated in July 2025; the audit committee reviews related-party transactions. ([4])
- **Internal controls:** a 2024 material weakness (IT general controls) was **remediated and cleanly attested as of Dec 31, 2025** by auditor CBIZ (which acquired Marcum's attest business — a firm change, not a disagreement). ([3])
- **Skin in the game:** CEO Tyler Page owns 8.5M shares (~2.1%); all directors + officers hold ~3.1%; Bitfury affiliates (founder lineage) own ~15.1%. ([4])
- **Insider sales:** Page adopted a 10b5-1 plan (Dec 2025) covering up to 1.5M shares through Dec 2026. Not alarming, but notable. ([3])

**Compensation is the concern.** Page's FY2025 reported total compensation was **$14.97M** (salary $825K, bonus $1.65M, stock awards $12.5M), and SEC "compensation actually paid" for the CEO was **$155.5M** in 2025, driven by PSUs vesting on the $5B and $7.5B market-cap milestones. ([4]) The company paid **200% of target annual bonuses** in 2025, and the CEO still holds the final tranche of market-cap PSUs vesting at a **$10B** market cap. Pay ratio was 24.6:1. ([4]) Say-on-pay passed with only **72%** support. ([4])

There is a legitimate alignment argument: awards are equity, multi-year, market-cap-linked, and the CEO realized ~$99M on vesting only after the stock went from ~$0.56 (2022) to ~$14.76 (2025). ([4]) But a Buffett lens notes that **management was heavily rewarded (a) while shareholders were simultaneously being diluted, and (b) on market-cap milestones that can be met by capital structure, not by owner earnings.** This prevents a top management score.

## 7.2 Capital Allocation

This is the crux.

**The good:**

- Raised **~$3.2B** in 2025 (convertibles + project notes) and completed three project financings at improving terms. ([3], [2])
- Structurally smart: non-recourse project debt, amortizing to match lease cash flows, preserving parent flexibility; a $200M revolver (with $50M accordion) is undrawn. ([2])
- Pivoted decisively and early into the landlord model, converting Black Pearl's mining site into an Amazon lease. ([3])

**The concerning:**

- **Persistent dilution.** Shares outstanding: ~249M (2021) → 291M (2023) → 351M (2024) → 405M (2025) → **414.3M (Jun 2026)**, +13.3% YoY. ([3], [6]) The ATM raised $195.5M at an average of **$5.88/share** in 2025 — selling equity near multi-year lows to fund the transition. ([3])
- **Equity as deal currency:** Google received **24.2M warrants at $0.01** (fair value $544M at issuance) for the Barber Lake backstop — a substantial equity cost embedded in deal terms. ([3])
- **Cash-flow capture by debt:** the model funds ~98% of project cost with debt; near-term NOI services that debt. The equity is buying a **back-ended residual**, not current cash. ([2])
- **Big non-cash noise:** 2025 included a $450M embedded-derivative loss, a $737M reclassification, a $544M warrant issuance, $96M miners-held-for-sale write-down, and $45M impairments. ([3])

**Capital-allocation score: 5/10** — brilliant at accessing capital and structuring deals, but the shareholder is funding a machine whose first claim on cash belongs to lenders and to future issuance.

## 7.3 Operational Capability

- **Impressive execution so far:** Black Pearl delivered early; equipment 95–100% secured across current builds; three successful financings; Odessa fleet at 17.2 J/TH. ([2])
- **Team:** ex-hyperscaler construction/ops hires; new CFO (Greg Mumford, ex-KBW) and new Head of Grid Strategies (ex-ERCOT) and ex-Google data-center lead. ([2], [4])
- **Caveat:** no HPC campus has yet produced a full year of contracted rent. Barber Lake rent begins Oct 2026; Black Pearl rent began Aug 2026. The operating proof is **just beginning**.

**Management score: 6/10** — strong execution and capital access, founder-led, candid; docked for heavy dilution, rich equity comp, and an unproven (at scale) operating record.

# 9. Owner Earnings — The Buffett Test

> Owner Earnings = Net Income + D&A − CapEx − Working Capital Change

For Cipher this is **deeply negative today**:

- **TTM owner earnings ≈ −$1.5B** (OCF −$256M less capex −$1.24B). ([7])
- FY2025 owner earnings ≈ **−$700M**. ([7])

That is not because the business is worthless; it is because the company is in an enormous **investment phase** — building campuses whose revenue and rent begin landing in 2026–2027.

**The forward question** — the one that determines whether Cipher is ever a Buffett holding:

> **After the project debt amortizes, how much cash actually reaches shareholders?**

My estimate (labeled clearly as an estimate, given confidential lease terms):

- Contracted NOI: **~$793M/yr** (management guide, 2027–2036). ([2])
- Project debt service (interest + amortization on ~$4.5B of project notes): **~$500–600M/yr** declining over the decade. ([2], [3])
- Corporate cash costs (G&A, comp ex-SBC, insurance): **~$60–80M/yr**. ([3])
- Net **equity** cash flow from the three contracted campuses at full ramp: roughly **+$150–300M/yr**, **back-loaded** (thin early, growing as debt amortizes), before any pipeline economics.

That back-loaded profile — cash first to lenders, then to equity — is the single most important reason this fails the classic Buffett owner-earnings test today.

**Current owner earnings: ≈ −$1.5B (TTM, actual). Forward normalized equity owner earnings: ≈ +$150–300M/yr at full contracted ramp (my estimate).**

# 11. Intrinsic Value & Margin of Safety

## 11.1 DCF — The Right Way to Value Cipher

I would **not** use 2025/2026 FCF. I value Cipher on **normalized future equity free cash flow** (cash to shareholders after debt service, corporate costs, and the maintenance/re-investment needed to keep the platform competitive), with the cash-flow-to-equity profile explicitly back-loaded.

Model inputs: 10-year horizon, terminal growth 2.5%, fully diluted share base ~**440M** (415M outstanding + ~24M Google warrants + conversion overhang). Discount rates reflect a 10–12% required return (Buffett-standard) on a levered, cyclical-adjacent asset.

### Bear case (conservative)

- Leases deliver **~75% of guided NOI** (~$600M/yr) — execution slippage, power-cost inflation, tenant friction.
- Debt service and corporate costs absorb most of it; **equity FCFE averages ~$150M/yr**, back-loaded.
- **Pipeline largely does not materialize**; treated near-zero.
- Discount 12%.
- **Approx. intrinsic value ≈ $6–9/share.**

### Base case (neutral)

- Leases deliver guided **~$793M/yr NOI**; debt amortizes on schedule; residual campus value accrues to equity.
- **Equity FCFE ramps from ~$50M (2027) to ~$350–400M/yr (2035–36)**, averaging ~$200–300M/yr over the decade; residual value of fully-amortized, leased campuses at year 10.
- **Partial pipeline success:** Odessa conversion + Reveille + Ulysses (477 MW) leased by 2027–28; partial build-out of Colchis/Mikeska/McLennan later.
- Discount 11–12%.
- **Approx. intrinsic value ≈ $10–14/share.**

### Bull case (optimistic)

- Leases deliver NOI **above guide** with rent escalators; all 907 MW ramped by 2027.
- **4.4 GW pipeline largely leased and built** 2028–2033 (Amazon/Google + more), each tranche project-financed; owner earnings compound from ~$300M to **>$1.2B/yr** by 2035.
- Discount 10–11%.
- **Approx. intrinsic value ≈ $24–30/share.**

### DCF Summary

| Scenario | Sustainable equity FCFE | Growth | Discount | Approx. value/share |
| -------- | ----------------------: | -----: | -------: | ------------------: |
| Bear     |                  ~$150M |     5% |      12% |           **~$6–9** |
| Base     |              ~$200–300M |     8% |   11–12% |         **~$10–14** |
| Bull     |             ~$900M–1.2B |    12% |   10–11% |         **~$24–30** |

The range tells the whole story: **Cipher is not hard to value because the math is hard — it is hard because the correct "cash to equity" assumption is radically uncertain**, and because a 12% required return on a back-loaded, heavily levered cash stream demands a much lower price than the NOI headline suggests.

> Note on the Street: the 17-analyst consensus average target is **$32.18** (range $22–$69), with recent targets of JPM $22, Canaccord $27, KBW $28, Bernstein $32, Needham $25, and Morgan Stanley $44. ([5], [8], [13], [14], [15], [16], [17], [18]) That consensus implicitly assumes lower discount rates and far more pipeline credit than my debt-aware base case. My disciplined-Buffett base sits well below the Street — that is a feature of the framework, not a claim that the Street is wrong.

## 11.2 Relative Valuation

- **P/E:** meaningless (GAAP losses; TTM EPS −$2.79). ([6])
- **P/S:** 38.8 (TTM) / 12.8 forward — extremely high, reflecting revenue still in the mining-liquidation trough. ([6])
- **P/B:** ~13x on book value of $1.36/share — expensive, though book understates land/interconnection value and overstates nothing on the debt side. ([6])
- **EV/EBITDA:** ~127x (TTM) — EBITDA is not yet a meaningful metric for a pre-revenue-transition landlord. ([6])
- **EV/Sales:** ~64x. ([6])

Relative valuation is **not informative** at this stage. The DCF and the equity cash-flow question are what matter.

## 11.3 Margin of Safety

At **$17.86**:

- vs. Bear (~$6–9): you are overpaying by **~2–3x**.
- vs. Base (~$10–14): you are paying **~30–80% above**.
- vs. Bull (~$24–30): there is **~35–65% upside**.

The expected value may be positive, but Buffett does not invest on expected value alone; he wants **a high probability of being approximately right**. Given the leverage, the back-loaded cash flows, the dilution, and the unproven operating record, I do not believe the probability distribution is narrow enough yet.

**Margin of safety at ~$17.86: 3/10 — insufficient.**

# 12. Major Risks

## Risk #1 — The Debt Comes First (cash-flow capture)

The contracted NOI is not equity cash flow. Project notes amortize during the base lease terms; equity is back-loaded. If leases deliver _at_ guidance, equity cash flow is modest for years; if leases underperform, the equity cushion (book equity just $562M on >$6B of debt) is thin. ([1], [2])

## Risk #2 — Tenant Concentration

Three single-tenant campuses (Amazon, Fluidstack/Google-backstop, one undisclosed hyperscaler). Tenant guarantees generally only activate **after rent commencement** and are subject to caps; a tenant bankruptcy or lease termination would hit the most important assets. The company explicitly flags this. ([3])

## Risk #3 — Dilution & Overhangs

Shares +13% YoY; ~162M potential shares from convertibles (at Dec-25), 24.2M Google warrants, a $600M ATM, and equity-for-deals. Per-share value is far more important than corporate value. ([3], [6])

## Risk #4 — Texas Regulatory / Interconnection

The 4.4 GW pipeline depends on ERCOT's batch process, which is being re-audited (Governor Abbott letter, Aug 2026). Delays or denials would push pipeline energization out and re-rate the stock. ([2])

## Risk #5 — Construction Cost & Execution

Build costs are creeping above the $9–11M/MW band with inflation; transformers and equipment are constrained; any delay in the three campuses reduces contracted NOI timing. ([2], [3])

## Risk #6 — Bitcoin Legacy

Odessa (207 MW) is the only operating revenue today; its Luminant PPA runs to ~July 2027 and a lease/PPA renegotiation is needed for an HPC conversion. Bitcoin price and the April 2028 halving matter to the mining tail. ([3])

## Risk #7 — AI Demand Cyclicality & Technology

If hyperscaler capex cycles down or compute efficiency improves dramatically, pipeline demand could soften and rent escalators could disappoint. ([3])

## Risk #8 — Accounting & Valuation Volatility

Warrant/convertible mark-to-market creates large GAAP swings (e.g., $150.5M Q2-26 warrant loss); the stock is volatile (beta 3.22), 13.4% short interest, and is down ~41% from its $30.14 high. ([6])

# 16. Final Investment Judgment

## **CIFR = C / WATCH**

I would **not** classify Cipher as a Buffett-style "Buy" at ~$17.86. That does not mean I think it is a bad company — quite the opposite. Cipher has the makings of a genuinely valuable HPC real-estate platform, with credible tenants, credible execution, and a credible path to scale.

The problem is that **the market already recognizes the transformation**, and the equity economics are far less clean than the NOI headlines:

> At $17.86 you are not buying "a cheap Bitcoin miner." You are buying **a leveraged, back-ended claim on the residual value of three leased data-center campuses plus an un-proven 4.4 GW development pipeline**, whose near-term cash flows are contractually committed to lenders first.

That is an interesting company — but a difficult Buffett investment.

## 16.1 My Valuation Framework (debt-aware, cash-to-equity)

| Price      | Buffett-style interpretation                                                       |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **>$20**   | Too much pipeline success priced in                                                |
| **$14–20** | Speculative / growth valuation (current zone)                                      |
| **$10–14** | Interesting; near my base case, still thin MOS                                     |
| **$7–10**  | **Attractive risk/reward if leases hold**                                          |
| **<$7**    | **Potential high-conviction value opportunity**, assuming contracted leases intact |

These are not rigid targets — if the equity cash-flow economics improve (leases deliver above guide, debt amortizes, pipeline signs), intrinsic value rises with them.

## 16.2 What Would Change My Rating from C → B/A

1. **Contracted rent actually converts to shareholder cash** — several quarters of positive, growing **FCF to equity** (after debt service), not just NOI.
2. **Share count stabilizes** while contracted NOI grows.
3. **Pipeline leases are signed** at the 477 MW near-term sites (Odessa/Reveille/Ulysses) and beyond, validating the 4.4 GW value.
4. **Project debt amortizes on schedule**, converting NOI into distributable equity cash flow; parent cash turns positive without new issuance.

## The single most important conclusion

**Do not reject Cipher because it used to be a Bitcoin miner — that is looking backward. Do not buy Cipher just because Amazon and Google signed leases — that is looking at revenue rather than equity economics.**

The real question is:

> **Can Cipher convert $793M/yr of contracted NOI into durable per-share owner earnings after debt service, dilution, and the capital required to keep building?**

If yes — the pipeline and the residual campuses could make today's price look cheap.
If no — the leverage and back-loaded structure mean equity holders may wait a long time for the cash the headlines promise.

That is why, under a genuinely conservative Buffett framework, I land at **C — Watch**, rather than A or B.
