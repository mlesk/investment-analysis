# Reddit (NYSE: RDDT) — Buffett-Style Value Investment Analysis

**Analysis date:** 2026-08-20 · **Reference price:** $151.71 (2026-08-19 close) · **Market cap:** ~$29.2B · **Shares:** 192.4M basic / 207.0M fully diluted

**Bottom line first:** Reddit is a genuinely rare thing — a social platform whose moat I would call **wide and widening**, with 91% gross margins, near-zero capital intensity, ~$2.8B of net cash, and 8 consecutive quarters of >60% revenue growth. This is the closest thing to a "toll booth on human conversation" that the internet has produced. **But at $151.71 the price has already priced in a great deal of the future.** On TTM numbers the stock trades at ~35× GAAP earnings, ~10.5× sales, ~29× FCF — and ~43× FCF after treating stock-based compensation as the real economic cost it is. My Buffett-style DCF gives bear/base/bull intrinsic values of roughly **$65 / $123 / $251** per share, a probability-weighted **~$131**. There is **no margin of safety at the current quote**; the market is implicitly underwriting ~18% FCF growth per year for a decade (on SBC-adjusted cash flow) even while the company's largest traffic acquisition channel (Google search) is demonstrably weakening. The business is a "buy at the right price" business, not a "buy at any price" business.

> **Rating: C — Watch. Wonderful business; price too high for a Buffett-style Buy at $151.71.**
>
> I would become a buyer in the **$85–95 zone** (a ≥30% discount to base-case value) and would view **<$75 as compelling** provided the community moat and ad monetization remain intact. I would be reducing above ~$180 and selling above ~$215 (near the consensus price target).

## 2. Business Understanding & Circle of Competence

### What the business is, in plain English

Reddit is the world's largest network of interest-based discussion communities ("subreddits"). Users post and vote on content; the most valuable posts surface to the top. Reddit makes money three ways:

1. **Advertising** — $762M of Q2'26 revenue (95% of the total), growing 64% y/y. Ads are sold against high-intent conversations ("Should I buy X?") ([SEC][2]).
2. **"Other" revenue** — $43M in Q2'26 (+24% y/y), primarily data-licensing deals that let AI labs train on Reddit content (Google, OpenAI) ([SEC][2]).
3. Long-term optionality — premium subscriptions, e-commerce (Shopify integration launched to GA in Q2'26), and future AI-data licensing at higher prices.

The flywheel is simple: **more users → more conversation content → better search ranking & more advertiser value → more revenue → more investment in product**. It is an understandable business — far closer to a See's Candies than to a GPU data-center lease book. I score circle of competence **8/10**: the product and monetization logic are within ordinary comprehension; the one genuinely hard question is external (how Google/AI interfaces will route traffic to Reddit over time).

### Will this still be needed in 10 years?

Almost certainly **yes**. The underlying demand — "ask real people for real opinions before you buy, decide, or believe" — predates the internet (Usenet, forums) and has survived every platform shift of the past 20 years. Huffman's Q2'26 letter frames Reddit as "the antidote to an automated web": as AI floods the internet with synthetic content, trust in human conversation becomes scarcer, not less valuable ([SEC][3]). The principal risk is not demand but **distribution** — whether users keep arriving via Google.

### Will technology fundamentally change the industry?

**Possibly, and in both directions.** AI could hurt (AI answers replace the "site:reddit.com" search habit that today drives roughly half of Reddit's traffic) or help (Reddit is the canonical training corpus; AI companies are Reddit's second customer class). Reddit's own 10-Q lists both: "potential harm caused by changes in internet search engines' methodologies" **and** competition from LLMs that answer questions directly ([SEC][1]). This two-sidedness is exactly why I cannot put a wide moat and a 35× multiple in the same sentence without flinching.

## 3. Economic Moat

### 3.1 Intangible assets — **9/10**

There is no patent moat worth mentioning. The moat is the **corpus**: 20+ years and 26B+ posts & comments across 100k+ active communities, generated by users for free ([SEC][3]). You cannot buy, fund, or engineer your way to 20 years of authentic conversation — every would-be competitor (Voat, Lemmy, federated clones) discovered this. The brand is powerful in a specific sense: "read it on Reddit" is a trust signal in purchase decisions; Reddit's own cited surveys report ~50% of US shoppers verify AI recommendations on Reddit before buying ([SEC][3]).

### 3.2 Switching costs — **7/10**

Moderate and rising. Users accumulate identity (karma, history, community standing, moderator roles) that does not port elsewhere. But Reddit is free to leave, and single-user exit costs are low. The real switching cost sits with the **community as a whole**: a subreddit's value lives in its accumulated archive and membership, which cannot be relocated. Retention shows it — new app-user retention improved 50% y/y in Q2'26 ([SEC][3]) — but logged-out users (now growing 27% y/y, 4× the logged-in rate) have essentially zero switching costs ([SEC][3]). That asymmetry is a real moat weakness.

### 3.3 Network effects — **9/10**

This is the heart of the moat and it is genuinely two-sided. More users → more questions asked → more answers posted → better search visibility (more users) — while simultaneously more content → more ad inventory → more advertisers → better monetization → more product investment. WAUq crossed **514.6M** in Q2'26 (+24% y/y) and DAUq reached **130.3M** (+18% y/y) ([SEC][2][3]). Competitors cannot easily replicate this: Meta tried with Facebook Groups, X with Communities, and none displaced the canonical "forum of record." The catch: the network effect compounds **through** Google. If AI answers intercept the search pathway, the network still exists but its acquisition funnel narrows. The community itself is defensible; the *traffic acquisition* layer is not.

### 3.4 Cost advantages — **7/10**

Extraordinary unit economics that are structural, not cyclical: users create the product (content), so cost of revenue is tiny — gross margin **91.3%** in Q2'26, 91.4% TTM ([SEC][2][6]). CapEx was **$1M** in Q2'26 (0.1% of revenue) and **$7.5M** over the trailing year ([SEC][2][5]) — the business requires essentially no physical reinvestment. Revenue per employee crossed **$1.0M** TTM ([SEC][3]). The one cost "leak" is stock compensation, addressed below. Scale economies in ad delivery, moderation (volunteer moderators + AI), and hosting all point the same direction.

### Moat rating: **Wide** — on the community side; with a **rented-distribution caveat** on the acquisition side.

## 4. Management Quality

### 4.1 Integrity & shareholder orientation — **7/10**

Steve Huffman (co-founder, CEO since 2015, u/spez) is candid in a way most CEOs are not: the Q2'26 letter openly concedes that "search referrals were choppy in the quarter, and traffic was more volatile later in the quarter," then states the strategic answer ("we're not building for drive-by traffic") ([SEC][3]). The company answers investor questions in a public subreddit (r/RDDT) ([SEC][2]). Governance caveats: Huffman votes **~74% of voting power** through a voting agreement with Advance (Reddit is a NYSE "controlled company") ([SEC][1]); major outside owners include Advance (~30%), Tencent (~11%), and Sam Altman (~9%) ([Wikipedia][8]). A controlled company with a founder at the helm is tolerable when performance is this good — but minority shareholders are passengers. Separately, the company faces securities litigation alleging inadequate disclosure about Google Search / AI Overviews impact ([SEC][1]) — an integrity-cloud to monitor rather than a proven fraud.

### 4.2 Capital allocation — **8/10**

The record is short (IPO March 2024) but unusually clean for a growth company:

- **A $1B buyback program** announced and actively used: 1.5M shares repurchased in Q2'26 for **$235M at ~$157.57** average, with $760M remaining authorized ([SEC][1][3]). Reddit is the rare hyper-growth platform *retiring* shares.
- Share count discipline is real: **fully diluted shares of 207.0M grew only +0.2% y/y** as of June 30, 2026 ([SEC][3]); Class A + Class B outstanding were 192.4M on July 29, 2026 ([SEC][1]).
- No dividend (appropriate at this growth rate); no value-destroying M&A of note; net cash simply accumulates.
- Counter-cyclical behavior: buying back stock at $157 during the post-earnings selloff is exactly the "be greedy when others are fearful" pattern.

### 4.3 Operational capability — **8/10**

The operating leverage is exceptional: operating margin went from **-43% (FY2024) to +20% (FY2025) to +28.3% TTM**; net margin 31.4% TTM ([StockAnalysis][6]). GAAP profitability (not just EBITDA) was reached and is expanding — Q2'26 net income $252.8M, up 2×+ y/y ([SEC][2]). ROE is **30.7%** on TTM GAAP equity — and far higher on operating capital, since ~$2.8B of the balance sheet is idle cash ([StockAnalysis][5]). Headcount discipline is visible: revenue/headcount crossed $1.0M ([SEC][3]).

### Management score: **7/10** — excellent operators; deductions for controlled-company governance, litigation noise, and an SBC bill that (while shrinking as a % of revenue) remains ~12% of revenue.

| Revenue growth | +37% | +21% | +62% | +69% | **+66.6%** |
| Gross margin | 84.3% | 86.2% | 90.5% | 91.2% | **91.4%** |
| Operating margin | -26% | -17% | -43% | +20% | **+28.3%** |
| Net income ($M) | -159 | -91 | -484 | +530 | **+871** |
| FCF ($M) | -100 | -85 | +216 | +684 | **+1,019** |

Source: [StockAnalysis][6]. The FY2024 loss was dominated by IPO-era stock awards; the underlying economics were already inflecting.

### 5.2 Cash flow quality

This is a Buffett-quality cash machine in structure: **OCF/Net income = 1.18×** TTM ($1,026M vs $871M); **FCF margin 36.7%** TTM ([StockAnalysis][5]). CapEx is nearly invisible ($7.5M TTM, 0.3% of revenue) — this business needs no treadmill. No receivables or inventory to go bad. The single earnings-quality blemish is **stock-based compensation**:

- FY2024 SBC: **$801.6M** (IPO-era mega-grants) → FY2025: **$343.2M** → **TTM: ~$338M** ([SEC XBRL][4]).
- Q2'26 SBC of **$100.95M** was roughly flat y/y ($89.1M a year earlier) despite +61% revenue ([SEC][1]).
- TTM SBC = **12.2% of revenue and ~39% of net income** — i.e., GAAP EPS overstates per-share economics by roughly that fraction, though the ratio is trending down sharply.

### 5.3 Financial safety

Flawless. **Cash & investments $2,786M vs total debt $20.9M** → net cash **~$2.77B (~$13.4–14.4/share)** ([StockAnalysis][5]); current ratio 10.5; interest coverage effectively infinite; no off-balance-sheet concerns beyond ordinary operating leases. The Altman Z-score is 62.8 ([StockAnalysis][5]). There is no financial distress scenario on this balance sheet.

### 5.4 Accounting prudence

Clean: no policy churn, no goodwill of consequence, revenue recognition conventional (deferred revenue is tiny — $38.6M at Q2'26, since ads bill as delivered) ([SEC][1]). The one normalization Buffett would demand is treating SBC as an expense in owner-earnings math — which I do below.

## 6. Long-term Growth Prospects

### 6.1 Industry

Digital advertising grows roughly with GDP-plus over a decade; within it, the shift toward "conversational commerce" (search-plus-social intent) favors Reddit. AI-search disruption is the wildcard: Reddit's 10-Q names competition from "Google (including YouTube, Search, Maps…), Meta, Snap, X, Pinterest, TikTok, Discord…" and from LLMs directly ([SEC][1]). The structure is oligopoly-with-scale-advantages — favorable for incumbents with differentiated inventory, which Reddit's intent-rich conversations are.

### 6.2 Company position and unit economics

- **DAUq 130.3M (+18% y/y); WAUq 514.6M (+24%)** ([SEC][2]).
- **ARPU $6.18/quarter, +36% y/y** — the monetization engine is what is compounding fastest ([SEC][1][2]).
- US revenue +56% y/y; **international +84%** — and international DAUq +28% vs US +6% ([SEC][3]). Growth is increasingly international and increasingly from logged-out users (+27% y/y).
- The honest concern: **US DAUq growth of +6%** is the weakest ever reported, and management attributes part of it to Google search-referral weakness ([SEC][3]). Management's own goal is 1B DAUq globally / 100M US — today it is at 13% of that ([SEC][3]).

### 6.3 Second growth curve

Real and demonstrated, not aspirational: **data licensing** (Google ~$60M/yr since Feb 2024; OpenAI partnership May 2024 with API access) ([Wikipedia][8][OpenAI][9]), **e-commerce** (Shopify GA, Shopping Listing Ads alpha, "7× average ROAS for retail advertisers in EMEA" per company data) ([SEC][3]), and **video** (Video in Comments, a reimagined video experience) ([SEC][3]). None is individually decisive today; together they diversify away from pure ad dependence.

### Growth score: **8/10** — but the next phase depends on international monetization offsetting a maturing US market and a fading search tailwind.

## 7. Owner Earnings & DCF

### 7.1 Owner earnings (Buffett's formula)

Owner earnings = Net income + D&A − CapEx − Δ working capital:

- TTM: **$871.1M + $16.5M − $7.5M − ~small ΔWC ≈ $880M** — about **$4.25/share** (diluted).
- **SBC-adjusted owner earnings ≈ $542M** — about **$2.62/share** diluted. Buffett would use this lower number: stock comp is real dilution of ownership, and Reddit still issues ~$338M/yr of it ([SEC][4]).

The honest read: on a fully loaded per-share basis the business earns **$2.6–4.3/share** today, versus a $151.71 share price — a **35–58×** multiple on true current owner-earnings.

### 7.2 DCF scenarios (10-yr owner-cash-flow model, terminal growth ≤3%, 10–12% discount)

I use "economic FCF" = reported FCF **minus SBC** as the starting cash-flow base, then add net cash:

| Scenario | Starting FCF | Growth (10y) | Discount | Terminal g | IV/share (dil.) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **Bear** | $680M (FCF−SBC) | 8% | 12% | 2.5% | **~$65** |
| **Base** | $800M | 14% | 11% | 3.0% | **~$123** |
| **Bull** | $950M | 20% | 10% | 3.0% | **~$251** |
| **Weighted (30/50/20)** | — | — | — | — | **~$131** |

Model notes: FCF base of $680M = TTM FCF $1,019M less TTM SBC $338M ([StockAnalysis][5][SEC][4]); shares = 207.0M fully diluted ([SEC][3]); net cash $2,765M added to equity value ([StockAnalysis][5]). All figures are estimates.

### 7.3 Reverse DCF — what the price assumes

At a $29.2B market cap (EV ~$26.4B after net cash), with an 11% discount rate and 3% terminal growth, the current price implies **~18% annual FCF growth for ten years on SBC-adjusted FCF** — or ~13% on reported FCF. That is not impossible (revenue just grew 66%), but it is exactly the kind of expectation that leaves no room for the Google-traffic scenario to play out badly.

## 9. Margin of Safety

- Weighted intrinsic value **~$131**; base case **~$123**; price **$151.71** → **the stock trades ~16–23% ABOVE base/weighted value.** Margin of safety: **negative**.
- Buffett requires a ≥30% discount to buy. That means an entry **≤ ~$86** against the base case.
- Downside if the Google scenario is structural rather than transient: in the bear case ($65), today's buyer would need ~5 years of ~13% value compounding just to get back to purchase price — with a beta of 2.03 amplifying the ride ([StockAnalysis][5]).

## 10. Major Risks

1. **Google/AI-search distribution risk (largest):** ~50% of traffic arrives via Google search; Q2'26 letter admits choppy, more volatile referrals; reports say the ~$60M/yr Google data-licensing deal may not be renewed; one analyst calls the search-traffic issue "likely permanent"; shares fell ~21% post-earnings on this theme ([SEC][3][Google News][10][Wikipedia][8]). If AI answers permanently intercept "site:reddit.com" searches, DAUq growth could stall even as revenue keeps compounding for a while.
2. **Concentration of user growth in low-switching-cost cohorts:** logged-out users +27% y/y vs logged-in +7% — the fastest-growing users are the least sticky ([SEC][3]).
3. **SBC burden:** ~$338M/yr (12% of revenue) is improving but still consumes ~39% of GAAP net income ([SEC][4]).
4. **Litigation & regulation:** securities class action + derivative suits over Google/AI-Overview disclosures; Australia under-16 social-media ban lawsuit; UK Online Safety Act age-verification obligations ([SEC][1][Wikipedia][8]).
5. **Governance:** Huffman controls ~74% of votes via the Advance agreement; NYSE "controlled company" status ([SEC][1]).
6. **Valuation/expectations:** at 10.5× sales, any growth disappointment compresses the multiple and the estimate simultaneously; short interest is ~10% of shares out ([StockAnalysis][5]).

## 11. Final Investment Judgment

Reddit passes almost every **qualitative** Buffett test — understandable business, wide community moat, network effects, 91% gross margins, zero-capex FCF conversion, net cash, founder-operator with real skin in the game, disciplined buybacks, honest shareholder letters. It fails the **price** test — which for Buffett is the whole game. At $151.71 you pay 35× TTM GAAP EPS, ~43× SBC-adjusted FCF, and a premium **above** even my probability-weighted intrinsic value of ~$131. There is no margin of safety, and the dominant exogenous risk (Google's AI search interface) is exactly the kind of thing a margin of safety exists to protect against.

> **Final rating: C — Watch.** A wonderful business at a fair-to-rich price is a *hold*; a wonderful business at $85–95 would be a *buy*. If the stock returns to that zone without evidence of community decay (DAUq still growing, ARPU compounding, SBC % falling), the asymmetry becomes genuinely attractive. Until then, patience is the position.
