# Part I — The Debate

## Round 1: Opening Statements

### Buffett's position (C — Watch)

But it fails my two hardest tests at $80.25: **(1)** it produces **zero owner earnings** — TTM free cash flow is **−$371M** and it has lost money every year since inception — and **(2)** there is **no margin of safety whatsoever** — the stock trades at ~62× trailing sales and roughly **5–6× my base-case intrinsic value of ~$15** (bear ~$6, bull ~$26, weighted ~$14). Price is what you pay, value is what you get, and at $80 you are paying for a SpaceX-scale outcome that even an optimistic DCF does not support. Great company, terrible entry price. **Rating: C — Watch. I become interested only below ~$10.**"

### Munger's position (D — Avoid)

> "I respect the operator — Peter Beck has built the West's leading small-lift rocket, a private pad in New Zealand, 87 successful missions, and a $2.36B backlog. But 'respect the operator' is not a substitute for 'the price is fair.' This is a stock priced for a flawless 10-year execution of the most difficult program in the industry, sold by a company that is burning **~$370M/yr**, diluting shareholders **~16% a year**, paying no dividend, generating no GAAP earnings, and — as I write this — about to lever up and issue a large block of stock to buy a profitable satellite operator for **$8B**. My conservative DCF — a **21.7% discount rate** (WACC +3%) with 0–2% terminal growth — values this stock at **$3.44–$8.38** (bear $3.44 / base $6.12 / bull $8.38, weighted **$5.63**). Even in my bull case the stock is worth less than a tenth of its price. The market is not pricing the business; it is pricing a lottery ticket on Neutron and Iridium. **Rating: D — Avoid. Buy only below ~$20.**"

### Damodaran's position (D — Avoid / Significantly Overvalued)

> "Rocket Lab is a genuinely excellent space company, but at $80.25 the market is paying **62× trailing sales** for a company that lost **$198M** in FY2025, burned ~$321M of free cash flow, and carries a 5-year beta of 2.63 — and the price embeds outcomes the fundamentals do not support. ## Round 3: Points of Contention, Cross-Examination, and Resolution

### Contention 1 — Reference price: intrinsic value vs 12-month price (the fundamental-vs-macro split)

- **All five** anchor on **$80.25** (Aug 14, 2026 close). But the debate runs on two different time-frames that were initially conflated.
- **Buffett / Munger / Damodaran** frame their scenario values as **intrinsic value today** — what the business is worth held to a long horizon: Buffett ~$14 weighted, Munger **$5.63**, Damodaran **$16.82**. Price is ~5–13× that IV.
- **Druckenmiller / Soros** frame their scenario values as **12-month price expectations** — where the stock _trades_ in a year: Druckenmiller bear $55 / base $103 / bull $135, weighted ~$90; Soros $58 / $88 / $115 / $135 / $40, weighted ≈$89. These answer "what happens next," not "what is it worth."
- **Cross-examination:** Soros to the IV camp: "Your $11–16 'intrinsic values' tell me nothing about the next quarter; the stock can run to $95 on a Neutron flight while you wait for a margin of safety that never comes." Buffett to the price-path camp: "And your $88–103 base is roughly the consensus price target — it is what the market already believes, not a mispricing. You are describing the path, not the value, and your path still only pays you +11–12% for a 2.63-beta, cash-burning, binary story."
- **Named concession:** Druckenmiller concedes his scenario prices are **12-month path projections, not intrinsic values** ("I am trading expectations, not fundamental value — on an IV basis I am as bearish as the fundamental lenses"). Soros concedes his scenarios are **12-month reflexive price targets**, not IV.
- **Resolution:** The consensus treats **Buffett, Munger, and Damodaran as the intrinsic-value anchors** (they set the consensus IV) and **Druckenmiller and Soros as 12-month price-path checks** (they inform the near-term ladder and the upgrade/downgrade triggers). In the valuation matrix below, the Druckenmiller/Soros columns are explicitly labeled as price expectations, not IV. **Both frames converge on the same action: do not initiate at $80.25.**

### Contention 3 — How much credit for the right tail (Neutron + Iridium)?

- **The fundamental lenses (IV):** Damodaran Bull **$25.09** (25%) / Extreme Bull **$50.22** (10%); Buffett bull ~$26; Munger bull $8.38. Even the _most favorable_ fundamental scenario ($50.22) is **−37% below the current price**.
- **The macro lenses (12-mo price paths):** Druckenmiller bull $135 (+68%, ~20–25%) / base $103; Soros bull $115 (+43%, 25%) / extreme bull $135 (+68%, 10%).
- **Cross-examination:** Damodaran to the macro camp: "Your $135 is the consensus price target — the market's _expectation_, not value. My extreme-bull IV of **$50.22** is what the business is _worth_ even if every catalyst lands: Neutron at a 1-3-5 cadence, Iridium closed and accretive, applications scaling. That is the entire upside, and it is below today's price." Druckenmiller to the IV camp: "You are pricing the company as if the binary fails. The market trades the _binary_ — if Neutron flies, the stock re-rates toward $95+ on momentum and NSSL task orders before your IV ever catches up. That is the opportunity, and it is real."
- **Named concessions:** Druckenmiller concedes the right tail is "real but already partially in the price" — the stock has already rallied ~41% off the July low on exactly that story, and his 1.8:1 asymmetry is below the bar for concentrated capital. Soros concedes the bull tail (~$115–135) requires _both_ Neutron success _and_ Iridium close — "at least three independent assumptions, each itself uncertain; if one fails, the compound narrative breaks." Buffett concedes the tail is "the most interesting part of the story" but refuses to pay for it.
- **Resolution:** The right tail justifies **watching closely and precludes a short** — but it does **not** justify buying at $80.25, because even the bull _intrinsic value_ (~$25–50) is below the price. The market is paying for the **price-path tail**, not the **value tail**. This is the single clearest demonstration that the fundamental value and the 12-month price are different instruments.

Buffett concedes the point is fair but counters that the gross-margin inflecting and the backlog visibility make this a different (better) risk than a fading story.
- **Named concessions:** Druckenmiller concedes there is **no cash-flow valuation floor today** (TTM FCF −$371M) — "the market will not reward earnings until Neutron de-risks the P&L; the stock needs a positive inflection to catch a falling multiple." - **Resolution:** This is a **real fragility, not an artifact** — a pre-owner-earnings company whose entire value sits in a heavily discounted terminal value. It is exactly why the fundamental IV (~$11–17) is so far below the price, why a margin of safety is impossible at $80.25, and why the FCF/EBITDA inflection after Neutron is the single valuation-critical variable.
