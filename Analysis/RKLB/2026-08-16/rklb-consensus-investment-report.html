Uncontested consensus core

This is an exceptional, real business. Founder-run, vertically integrated — one of only two self-launching space powers on earth; real moat (flight heritage, private Mahia pad + US–NZ treaty, vertical integration, Iridium's scarce L-band spectrum); record Q2'26 revenue ($234M, +62% y/y); $2.36B record backlog (45.5% converts in 12 months).
The business is not in dispute; the price is. No profile buys at $80.25; no profile recommends a short. Five frameworks, five refusals at the current price.
Neutron is THE swing factor. Unflown, slipped ~five times, Stage-1 tank ruptured January 2026, "window narrowing"; ~14% probability of a 2026 launch (prediction markets). A successful first flight re-rates the entire story; a static-fire/first-flight failure flips the narrative from "when" to "if."
Iridium is transformative but risky. $8.0B EV ($54/share cash+stock), funded by a $3.6B committed bridge plus assumption of Iridium's $1.775B term loan; close mid-2027; financing and integration unbuilt; converts a net-cash fortress into a levered balance sheet.
Valuation is extreme. 62× TTM sales / ~42× forward with no GAAP earnings through FY27 (consensus adj EPS −$0.05 FY26 / +$0.05 FY27); the price embeds a "second SpaceX" outcome the cash-flow math does not support.
Dilution is structural. Shares +15.7% YoY (598.5M → ~641M guided); $1.08B ATM Q2'26 plus $1.12B FY25; $3.0B ATM authorization; 141.8M shares reserved (~24% overhang); evergreen plan +5%/yr. Growth is funded by shareholders, not by the business.
The cash bridge is real but finite. Net cash ~$2.25B ($3.77/share) cushions a TTM FCF of −$371M; management guides adjusted-EBITDA-positive the quarter after a successful first Neutron flight and FCF-positive 18–24 months later.
The single valuation-critical variable is the FCF/EBITDA inflection after Neutron's first successful flight — the hinge that reconciles the fundamental lenses (IV ~$6–16) with the macro lenses (12-mo price ~$88–103). If Neutron flies and the P&L inflects, the equity-funded reinvestment turns value-creating; if it fails or slips, value converges toward net cash.
The macro is a headwind. 10Y ~4.69% near its 52-week high, a hawkish Fed hold under Chair Warsh with three dissents for a hike, and an energy-driven inflation shock — a discount-rate headwind for a 2.63-beta, long-duration, cash-burning growth stock.

09The Five Consensus Conclusions

The Business. Rocket Lab is an exceptional, founder-run, vertically integrated space company — one of only two "self-launching" space powers on earth (build, launch, operate its own satellites) — with a real moat (flight heritage, private NZ pad + US–NZ treaty, vertical integration, Iridium's scarce L-band spectrum), a record $2.36B backlog, and extraordinary growth optionality in Neutron and Iridium. The business quality is not in dispute; the price is.
The Economics. The sole determinant of long-term per-share value is the FCF/EBITDA inflection after Neutron's first successful flight — whether an unflown medium-lift rocket and a levered $8B acquisition convert the equity-funded reinvestment (ROIC −5.5% vs ~12% WACC) into positive free cash flow inside a credible window. If Neutron flies and the P&L inflects, growth turns value-creating; if it fails or slips, value converges toward net cash (~$3.77/share) and the backlog. The per-share tax is dilution (shares +15.7% YoY, ~24% issuance overhang), not interest.
The Market's Expectations. At $80.25 the market is implicitly assuming a successful Neutron first launch, an accretive Iridium close, ~40%+ revenue growth for years, a 10-year that does not break out to 5%+, and no further heavy dilution — a "second SpaceX" outcome requiring ~$25–30B terminal revenue at 22–25% operating margins, at ~5× the consensus weighted IV and 62× trailing sales / ~42× forward. Aggressive but not impossible; the consensus "Buy / $112.94 PT" reflects it. The Street's uniformity is itself a risk if Neutron slips.
The Valuation. Consensus base intrinsic value ~$11/share; probability-weighted ~$16.82/share (verified workbook anchor); bear ~$5–7; bull ~$25; extreme bull ~$50 (Damodaran workbook: $4.91 / $5.94 / $11.00 / $25.09 / $50.22, weighted $16.82, upside −79.04%, 5-yr annualized −26.84%). Confidence: Low/Medium — the direction of overvaluation is robust (the entire fundamental distribution sits below the price), the magnitude is uncertain (the Neutron/Iridium binary). The 12-month price-path lenses (Druckenmiller ~$90, Soros ≈$89, +11–12%) are balanced-to-positive but not exceptional (~1.1×–1.8× asymmetry). Both the base and weighted fundamental values are far below the $80.25 price — expected upside ≈ −79%, 5-year annualized ≈ −27%.
The Investment Decision. C — Hold/Watch at ~$80.25; do not initiate above ~$72. Tactical starter $62–72 (Soros), tactical accumulate $50–62 (Druckenmiller, on dislocation + confirmation), fundamental accumulate $20–30, high-conviction <$20 (Damodaran/Munger conviction; Buffett <$10), provided the monitoring hierarchy (Neutron first-flight status > FCF/EBITDA inflection > Iridium close & financing > dilution/share count > backlog & margin) stays intact. Upgrades require proof — a successful Neutron first flight, a confirmed FCF inflection, a low-dilution Iridium close, and/or a margin of safety — and a 10Y roll below ~4.3%. The binary catalyst and the operating data, not the stock price, will deliver the verdict.

10Final Consensus Judgment

“
