# Berkshire Hathaway Inc. (BRK) — Warren Buffett Lens Analysis

**Date:** 2026-08-16 · **Reference price (BRK.B, 2026-08-14):** $504.03 · **Market cap:** ~$1.08T [12]

---

## Bottom Line First

**Rating: C — Watch / Hold.** Berkshire is a _wonderful_ business at a _fair_ price. My base-case intrinsic value of ~$500 per Class B share (SOTP: operating-franchise DCF + investment portfolio at tax-affected market + net cash) is essentially **equal to the current $504 market price** — there is **zero margin of safety**. The franchise (negative-cost insurance float of $177.5B, BNSF/BHE/MSR cash engines, a $323.8B equity portfolio carried at cost of $106.5B, and $365.5B of cash) is about as high-quality as American capitalism offers, and Greg Abel's first letter confirms the culture and discipline are intact. But at 1.45× book value — near the top of Berkshire's historical range — and with base intrinsic value roughly at market, the price is not a Buffett-style bargain. A patient investor should wait for the low-$400s (a ~15–20% discount to base IV, near my bear case) or a market/portfolio drawdown to build a position.

## 1. Business Understanding & Circle of Competence

**How Berkshire makes money (in simple terms).** Berkshire is a holding company whose core engine is **insurance**: it collects premiums up front (float) and invests that money — at a _negative cost_ because underwriting has been profitable — in a concentrated portfolio of equities and U.S. Treasuries. Float was **~$177.5B at June 30, 2026** ($176B at year-end 2025) [1][2]. That "free money" funds GEICO (auto), the Primary Group (commercial lines), and Berkshire Hathaway Reinsurance Group, plus the investment portfolio. Around that engine sit four other durable businesses: **BNSF** (one of six North American Class-I railroads), **BHE** (utilities/energy), **Manufacturing, Service & Retailing** (Precision Castparts, Lubrizol, Clayton, NetJets, Pilot, See's, etc.), and a growing set of **equity investments** (Apple, AmEx, Coca-Cola, and now Alphabet).

**Simplicity.** The individual pieces are comprehensible to an ordinary investor; the _consolidated_ complexity (insurance accounting, GAAP mark-to-market noise) is the one genuine discount. Berkshire itself says GAAP net earnings "must be assessed with caution" and that **operating earnings is the best measure of business performance** [2][7].

**Will it be needed in 10 years?** Yes — insurance is mandatory, freight rail and energy are infrastructure, and consumer staples/industrial demand persists. Nothing in Berkshire's portfolio is exposed to a single technological disruption; its AI-adjacent exposure (BHE datacenter power demand, Alphabet stake) is additive, not existential.

**Will the commercial essence change?** The _industry_ (P&C underwriting, regulated utilities, rail) changes slowly; the main technological shifts (telematics at GEICO, AI-driven load forecasting at BHE) are cost _improvements_ Berkshire is adopting, not disruptions to its moat. Circle of competence: **within — high confidence.**

## 2. Economic Moat Analysis (4 types)

**Intangible assets — Strong.** The Berkshire _brand_ itself is a moat: counterparties, sellers, and regulators trust it to pay claims "without hesitation" and to close large, complex reinsurance deals others cannot [7]. GEICO has a genuine low-cost brand; See's, Clayton, and NetJets carry pricing power in niches. Regulatory barriers (state insurance licensing, utility rate compacts, rail regulation) are high and hard to replicate.

**Switching costs — Moderate.** Auto insurance churns on price (GEICO's retention fell after rate hikes [7]); railroads have meaningful but not absolute switching costs; utilities have captive customers with rate regulation. Net: moderate, and mostly an _industry_ feature rather than a Berkshire-specific edge.

**Network effects — Weak.** Not a network business. Two-sided/one-sided network economics do not apply; the "network" that matters is Berkshire's internal capital-allocation network (float → investments → operating cash flows), which is a _system_ advantage, not a classic network effect.

**Cost advantages — Strong (the core moat).**

- **Float at negative cost:** combined ratio of **87.1% in 2025** (5-yr avg 90.7%, 10-yr 93.0%, 20-yr 92.2%) means policyholders _pay Berkshire to borrow_ ~$176B [7]. No competitor can match this funding.
- **GEICO low-cost position:** industry's low-cost auto insurer, now layering in technology to defend it [7].
- **Rail oligopoly:** six Class-I railroads in North America; BNSF's operating margin improved to 34.5% in 2025 (from 32.0%) though still below the best peer [7].
- **Statutory surplus as a barrier:** Berkshire's insurance statutory surplus is itself a competitive advantage in winning large, unusual risks [3].
- **Regulated utility economics:** BHE rates average ~24% below national retail, a structural cost advantage [7].

**Moat rating: WIDE.** The moat is wide and, for the float and rail franchises, durable over decades. The soft spots (auto retention, BHE wildfire liability, MSR competitive pressure) erode only the margin, not the structure.

## 3. Management Quality Assessment

**Integrity & shareholder orientation — Excellent.**

- Greg Abel's first letter (the FY2025 letter) openly flags mistakes: the **Kraft Heinz investment has been "disappointing… well short of adequate"**; the **Pilot contract structure ("That mistake will not happen again")**; **Shaw's self-inflicted quality problems**; and BNSF's 2025 resolution of a past failure with an apology [7].
- Compensation is modest and equity-oriented; Buffett's shares go to philanthropy; Abel and Jain run lean corporate overhead [7]. No options-driven aggression; no related-party red flags surfaced in the filings reviewed.
- Berkshire does **not** pay a dividend so long as retained earnings create >$1 of market value per $1 retained — a rigorous shareholder test [7][10].

**Capital allocation — Very strong, one caveat.**

- Track record: float $88B (2015) → $176B (2025) [7]; book value $649.4B (2024) → $717.4B (2025) [3]; OxyChem ($9.4B) and Bell Laboratories added in 2025/early-2026, Taylor Morrison (~$6.8B/$72.50 per share) closed July 2026 [5].
- Buyback discipline: repurchases only below conservatively-determined intrinsic value, with a **$30B cash-floor safeguard** [5][10]. Buybacks were light in 2025 (treasury stock flat at $78.9B) and resumed at **~$4.5B in Q2 2026** ($4.8B H1-26) [1][3].
- Abel is now _deploying_ the cash: **~$20B net added to equities in Q2-26**, a $10B Alphabet private placement, a new Delta position, plus BNSF/BHE/MSR organic capex [10][11].
- Caveat: ~$365B in T-bills earning ~4% is a real **cash drag / deployment risk** — the single most-cited concern (Michael Burry argues Abel lacks Buffett's "patience for the fat pitch") [10]. Mitigated by the $10B Alphabet placement being negotiated directly at a discount to market [10].

**Operational capability — Strong.**

- BNSF operating margin 32.0% → 34.5% (2024→2025), each 1pt ≈ $230M cash flow [7].
- Precision Castparts OCF $0.9B avg (2021–22) → **$2.4B (2025)**; Pilot $1.7B (2025); BHE $8.4B (2025); BNSF $8.1B (2025) [7].
- GEICO restored underwriting margins via rate actions and is investing in tech to rebuild retention [7].

**Management score: 9/10** (integrity 10, allocation 9, operations 8–9).

## 4. Financial Health & Earnings Quality

**Profitability (10-yr trend).**

- FY2025: total revenues **$371.4B**; net earnings attributable **$67.0B** (2024: $89.0B; 2023: $96.2B) [3][2]. TTM revenue $384.7B, TTM GAAP net income $85.8B (fact pack / Yahoo) [12].
- **Operating earnings** FY2025 **$44.5B** (vs $47.4B in 2024; five-year average $37.5B); **TTM through Q2-26 ≈ $48.0B** (computed: 44.486 − 20.801 + 24.329) [2][1].
- ROE: GAAP FY25 **~9.8%** (66.97 / avg equity 683.4, computed); TTM ~12.1% (fact pack) [12]. Operating ROE on book ~6.5% (estimate) — modest because $365B of low-yield cash sits in book equity. This is a _quality_ issue (leverage-free), not a red flag.
- ROIC vs WACC: comfortably above cost of capital for the operating businesses; the equity portfolio historically compounds well above its cost basis (unrealized gains $217B at Q2-26) [5].

**Cash flow quality — excellent.**

- Operating cash flow: FY2025 **$46.0B** (five-year average >$40B) [7]; capex (PP&E + equipment held for lease) FY2025 **$20.9B** [3]; computed FCF ≈ $25B.
- OCF/net income ≈ 0.7 on GAAP (net income inflated by non-cash investment gains) but ≈ 1.0+ on operating earnings — **earnings quality is high when measured against operating earnings**, which is the right metric for an insurer [3][7].
- CapEx is lumpy (BHE investment cycle) but each segment self-funds and returns cash upstream ($4.4B BNSF dividend to Berkshire in 2025; $29B returned by insurers, up to a $31B statutory limit) [7].

**Financial safety — fortress.**

- Cash + U.S. T-bills at 6/30/26: **$365.5B** (YE25: $369.2B) [5][3]; fixed maturities $17.0B; borrowings **$128.6B** → **net cash ≈ $254B** (computed) [5].
- Debt/equity **~17%** (computed from filings; fact pack 17.1%) [12][5].
- Interest coverage: pre-tax earnings $82.5B (incl. investment gains) / interest $5.1B ≈ 16×; on operating earnings ~10× (estimate) [3].
- Float $177.5B carried at negative cost [1][7]; no refinancing risk at these levels.

**Accounting prudence — high.** No aggressive revenue recognition; the main "noise" is GAAP mark-to-market of equities (unrealized gains flow through net income by rule), which management explicitly disclaims [2][3]. Goodwill $83B (~6.8% of assets) with only modest 2020-era impairments; finite-lived intangibles amortization $1.7B/yr (2025) [3]. Deferred tax liabilities $86.0B largely hedge the embedded equity gains [3].

## 5. Long-term Growth Prospects

**Industry trends.** Insurance is in a **softening phase** entering 2026: Abel explicitly warns Berkshire will "write less property and casualty business for a period" as pricing decelerates and casualty claims inflation outpaces pricing [7]. Rail demand is secularly stable; **electricity demand is accelerating from AI/datacenter computing** — a genuine second growth curve for BHE [7]. Housing (Taylor Morrison, Clayton, Lennar) benefits from a long-run supply deficit.

**Company competitive position.** Strengthening: BNSF closing the margin gap to peers; BHE investing into the AI power cycle (rate-regulated, ~24% below national average); GEICO rebuilding retention with technology; equity portfolio rotated toward Alphabet/Delta while trimming financials [7][10][11]. Pricing power exists where regulation or oligopoly applies; the insurance book is deliberately volume-averse.

**Sustainable growth.**

- Historical growth is a blend of M&A (OxyChem, Taylor Morrison, Bell Labs) and organic operating leverage; float has compounded $88B→$176B over a decade [7].
- Future growth is modest by design: Abel: "At Berkshire's scale, the math of compounding works against us" [7]. Realistic 4–6% organic operating-earnings growth, plus buyback accretion.
- Second growth curves: BHE AI-load investment; GEICO telematics; the shift of $365B cash into productive equities/operating assets (the largest optionality in the entire company) [7][10].

**Growth verdict: mature but durable, 4–6% — the value is in quality and compounding, not hyper-growth.**

## 6. Owner Earnings & Intrinsic Value (Buffett-lens DCF + SOTP)

**Owner-earnings definition for a conglomerate/insurer.** For Berkshire, GAAP net income is a poor proxy (it swings with investment gains). The economically meaningful "owner earnings" is **operating earnings** (net earnings ex investment gains/losses and impairments): **TTM ≈ $48.0B** (computed from sourced segment data) [1][2]. I further separate out **insurance investment income (TTM ≈ $12.0B)** because that income is the _return on the investment portfolio_, which I value separately at market to avoid double-counting.

**Normalized owner earnings (operating businesses, ex investment income):** TTM $36.0B (computed); normalize record insurance underwriting ($7.4B TTM, combined ratio 87.1% vs 10-yr avg 93.0%) down to mid-cycle, and haircut MSR/other → **base ~$33.5B**, bear ~$29B, bull ~$38B (all estimates, clearly labeled).

**Net investment assets (valued separately, at tax-affected market):** at 6/30/26 [5]:

- Equity securities (fair value): **$323.8B** (cost $106.5B; embedded pre-tax gains $217.3B)
- Fixed maturities: $17.0B
- Cash + T-bills: $365.5B
- Less borrowings: ($128.6B)
- Less ~15% tax on embedded equity gains: (~$32.6B)
- **≈ $545B**

**DCF scenarios (10-yr growing annuity + terminal value; owner earnings as FCF proxy; discount = required return):**

| Scenario | Norm. OE | 10-yr growth | Terminal g | Discount | PV of franchise | + Invest. assets | **IV total** | **IV / Class B** |
| -------- | -------- | ------------ | ---------- | -------- | --------------- | ---------------- | ------------ | ---------------- |
| **Bear** | $29B     | 2.5%         | 1.5%       | 10.5%    | ~$351B          | $545B            | ~$896B       | **~$415**        |
| **Base** | $33.5B   | 4.0%         | 2.5%       | 10.0%    | ~$511B          | $545B            | ~$1,056B     | **~$500**        |
| **Bull** | $38B     | 5.5%         | 3.0%       | 9.0%     | ~$790B          | $545B            | ~$1,335B     | **~$620**        |

_(All DCF figures are my estimates from sourced inputs; discount 9–10.5% per Buffett-lens guidance; terminal g ≤ GDP.)_

**Weighted intrinsic value** (25/50/25 bear/base/bull): **~$510 per Class B share** — essentially the current price.

**SOTP cross-check (per Class B share):**

- Net investment assets: $545B / 2,147.5M shares = **$254/share**
- Operating franchise: at 10× normalized base OE ($33.5B) = $335B → **$156/share**
- Sum ≈ **$410/share** on a _no-growth_ capitalization; the DCF above adds ~4% growth → ~$500. Both approaches bracket the market price [3][5].

**Margin of safety at $504:** base IV ≈ market → **~0–1%**. Even my _bear_ case ($415) is only 18% below market. There is **no margin of safety today**.

## 7. Relative Valuation

- **P/E (TTM GAAP):** 12.7× (504.03 / $39.77 EPS) [12] — misleadingly cheap because GAAP EPS includes investment gains; the market effectively pays ~**22.5× TTM operating earnings** ($22.36 Op EPS, computed) — a fair-to-rich multiple for a mature, slow-growth (4–6%) conglomerate.
- **P/B:** **1.45×** book value per share of ~$348 (6/30/26; computed) [12][5] — near the **top** of Berkshire's historical ~1.1–1.6× range; the market is paying a premium to book that only makes sense if operating returns on the cash pile improve.
- **EV/EBITDA:** not meaningful for a diversified insurer-holding company; EV ≈ $1.17T [12].
- **PEG:** on operating earnings growth (~5%), PEG ≈ 22.5/5 ≈ 4.5× — rich for a value lens.
- **Peer frame:** Berkshire is _the_ benchmark; the appropriate comparator is its own historical P/B and P/operating-earnings, both of which sit at the high end [12].

**Verdict:** Relative valuation confirms the DCF — **fairly priced, not cheap.**

## 8. Margin of Safety

- Current price ($504) vs base IV ($500): discount ≈ **0%**.
- Required ≥30% margin of safety (Buffett discipline) → buy ≈ **$350**; even a first-tranche (≥15–20% discount) requires ≈ **$400–430**.
- **Downside if earnings disappoint:** if equities fall 30% ($324B→$227B) and normalized operating earnings fall to $26B, SOTP ≈ **$350–380/share** (estimate) — a real but survivable drawdown given the fortress balance sheet; Berkshire's equity portfolio concentration means mark-to-market _volatility_ is high even if intrinsic compounding continues.
- **Margin of safety: none at current prices.** Wait for the low-$400s or below.

## 9. Risk Factors

**Industry & macro**

- **Insurance cycle:** P&C pricing is softening into 2026; reinsurance prices declining; casualty claims inflation running ahead of pricing; Berkshire will write less premium, so near-term operating earnings growth slows [7].
- **Rates & deployment:** $365B in T-bills → if Abel deploys aggressively into equities, GAAP earnings get more volatile and deployment mistakes become possible (Burry critique) [10].
- **Equity market drawdown:** concentrated portfolio (5 largest = **66%** of equities at 6/30/26) means a market correction hits both book value and GAAP earnings hard [5][10].

**Company-specific**

- **Succession / key-person:** Buffett (95) still chairman and "in the office five days a week"; Abel is now CEO, Ajit Jain remains the insurance architect. The transition is the single biggest long-term risk to the culture [7].
- **BHE regulatory/wildfire:** PacifiCorp wildfire settlements and the regulatory compact are live liabilities; BHE is "rebalancing" [7].
- **BNSF/rail competitive gap:** operating margin (34.5%) still trails the best Class-I; UP–NSC merger could shift competitive dynamics (Berkshire has ruled out buying a railroad) [7].
- **Portfolio concentration & tax:** $217B of unrealized gains carry a deferred tax liability; concentration in a handful of mega-caps [3][5].
- **Valuation risk:** at 1.45× book, expectations of improving returns on cash are already partly priced; a 20–30% drawdown in the equity portfolio is the main source of short-term loss.

## 10. Investment Decision (Buffett Framework)

**Buy conditions:** Business simple/within circle ✓ · Wide moat ✓ · Management integrity & allocation ✓ · Financial strength ✓ · Growth prospects ✓ · **Margin of safety ✗ (none at $504)** · Risks controllable ✓. → **Fails only on price.**

**Hold conditions:** Fundamentals intact ✓ · Moat solid ✓ · No integrity issues ✓ · Valuation not severely overvalued ✓ (P/E ~12.7 GAAP; ~22.5× operating, top of P/B range but not extreme). → Hold is appropriate for owners.

**Sell conditions:** Would trigger on (a) moat erosion (e.g., float turns persistently costly, GEICO loses cost advantage), (b) integrity failure, (c) valuation > ~1.8× book / >$620 (25%+ above base IV), or (d) a materially better use of capital.

**Final rating: C (Watch/Hold).** An exceptional enterprise that is fairly valued. **Add only on weakness.**
